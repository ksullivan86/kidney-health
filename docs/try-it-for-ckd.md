# Try the app: a walk-through for people who know kidney disease

This is for someone who lives with chronic kidney disease (CKD), cares for someone who does, or works with people who do.
You don't need to know anything about computers or software. We want to know one thing: **would this help someone with CKD
stick to their diet?**

It takes about 45 minutes. You can stop at any point and still send us what you have.

## Before you start

* Open the demo on a phone or a computer: **https://ksullivan86.github.io/kidney-health/demo/**
* There is nothing to install and no sign-up. Nothing you type leaves your browser.
* The demo comes filled in with a made-up person, **Sam**: CKD stage 4, type 1 diabetes, 70 kg (154 lb), not on dialysis.
  Please use the app as if you were Sam, or as if you were helping Sam.
* **Reloading the page starts over.** That's normal. Everything goes back to Sam's sample data.
* The demo has no AI features and only a few barcodes work. The full app has more; you're testing the core.
* The app is not medical advice, and nor is this test. Please don't change your own diet based on it.

Keep a note open (paper is fine) and write down anything that confuses you, surprises you, or seems wrong, even small
things. "I didn't know what this word meant" is just as useful as "this number is wrong".

## The walk-through

The app has tabs along the bottom (or the side on a big screen): **Today**, **Add**, **Plan**, **Trends** and **Profile**.
**Settings** is the gear at the top.

### 1. First look (5 minutes)

Open **Today**.

* What do you think this screen is telling you about Sam's day?
* Can you tell how much potassium, phosphorus, sodium and protein Sam has had, and how much is left?
* Is anything shown as too high? Is it clear why?
* Are the colours and words easy to read?

### 2. Add a food Sam ate (5 minutes)

Go to **Add**. Search for a food you or someone with CKD would really eat, for example a slice of white bread, an apple,
chicken, or rice. Choose an amount and add it to a meal.

* Did you find the food? If not, what did you search for?
* Was the amount easy to enter, in the units you think in (cups, slices, grams, ounces)?
* Back on **Today**, did the numbers change the way you expected?
* Try a food that is known to be high in potassium, such as a banana, a potato or orange juice. Does the app warn about it?
  Is the warning helpful, too strong, or too weak?

### 3. "What fits now" (5 minutes)

On **Today**, find **What fits now**.

* Do the suggestions look like foods someone on a kidney diet could really eat?
* Would you trust them? Would a dietitian?
* Is anything suggested that you would never eat, or that seems wrong for CKD?

### 4. Treating a low blood sugar (3 minutes)

Sam has type 1 diabetes. Imagine Sam's blood sugar is low and Sam drinks some juice to treat it.

* Can you log that juice as **Used to treat a low**?
* The app should **never** tell Sam not to treat a low, even when the juice is high in potassium. Does it ever seem to?
  If so, please tell us exactly what it said. This matters a lot to us.

### 5. Plan the next few days (10 minutes)

Go to **Plan** and find **Plan several days…**. Plan a week.

* Do the planned meals look like real kidney-friendly meals?
* Is there enough variety, or too much? Can you choose which meals may repeat, like the same breakfast every day?
* Open the shopping list. Is it something you could take to the store or send to someone? Try **Copy list** or **Share list**.
* If you plan days by mistake, can you clear them again?
* At the top of Plan you may see a meal suggested by Jordan, from Sam's Care team. Is it clear what **Add to my plan** and
  **No thanks** do?
* At the top of Plan, choose **Ana** under **Planning for**. This is how a family member helps someone on their Care team: plan
  meals, log food they ate, use their saved meals and suggest a meal. Is it clear whose food you are changing, and how to get back
  to Sam's?

### 6. Lab results and targets (7 minutes)

Go to **Profile**. Look at Sam's daily targets (potassium, phosphorus, sodium, protein, fluid).

* Do the numbers look reasonable for stage 4 CKD, compared with what you've been told by a care team?
* Press **Suggest targets**. Is the explanation of each number clear? Would you know where the number comes from?
* Open **Lab results** and add a new potassium result, for example 5.8. What does the app do? Is it clear and calm, or
  alarming? Would it make you call your care team?

### 7. Looking back (5 minutes)

Go to **Trends**.

* Can you see how Sam did over the past week? Which days were over?
* Find **Visit prep** (getting ready for a visit, as one printable page). Would you bring this to a nephrologist or a
  dietitian? Is anything missing that they would ask for?

### 8. Learn (5 minutes)

Look for the links to the handbook (the **Learn** pages, about potassium, phosphorus, label reading and more).

* Is the writing easy to follow?
* Is anything wrong, out of date, or different from what your care team told you?

### 9. Your data (2 minutes)

Open **Settings** (the gear).

* Is it clear where Sam's data is kept and how to get a copy of it?
* Look at **Care team**: the demo shows a sample. Is it clear what a Viewer, a Care helper and a Caregiver can do, and what
  each switch under **What they may see and do** means?
* Is there anything here that worries you about privacy?

## Questions for the end

Please answer these in your own words. Short answers are fine.

1. Would you, or the person you care for, use this every day? Why or why not?
2. What was the most useful thing in it?
3. What was the most confusing thing?
4. Did anything look **wrong or unsafe** for someone with CKD? (Wrong numbers, bad suggestions, scary or missing warnings.)
5. What is missing that you would need? (A food, a feature, a way of entering things, a report.)
6. Who do you think this would help most: early-stage CKD, late stage, dialysis, caregivers, dietitians?
7. Anything else?

## Sending your notes

Send your notes and answers back to the person who gave you this page, in whatever form is easiest: a message, an email,
photos of handwritten notes, or a voice memo. Screenshots of anything confusing help a lot.

Thank you. Every note helps make this better for people living with kidney disease.
