# Kidney Health Handbook

**For people living with chronic kidney disease (CKD) and type 1 diabetes, at any stage, and for the
family and friends who help them.** It explains what each stage means day to day, what to eat, how to
read your lab results, and when to get help, with real numbers and the source for every one.

!!! warning "What this handbook is not"
    It is not your care team's advice and it never replaces it. Your nephrologist, diabetes team and
    renal dietitian set your own targets and doses. **This handbook never gives insulin or medicine
    doses.** Use it to understand your care and to prepare questions. KDIGO asks for education that
    also involves care partners ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.3.2): bring a family member.

**In an emergency, call 911** (or your local number). The [Get help now](get-help-now.md) link is at
the top of every page.

## Five things to know today

1. **Never skip treating a low.** Under 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L), take 15 g of fast sugar straight
   away, best as 4 glucose tablets; if juice or regular soda is what you have, drink 4 oz (½ cup).
   Recheck in 15 minutes and repeat if still low. Potassium never delays treatment
   ([Treating a low](t1d/treating-a-low.md); [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]; [NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]).
2. **Have a written sick-day plan** from your team, including when to pause medicines and when to
   restart them ([Sick days](t1d/sick-days.md); [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 4.3.2).
3. **Know your own potassium and fluid rules, if you have them**, from your latest blood tests and your
   dialysis unit ([Potassium](eat/potassium.md); [Fluid](eat/fluid.md)).
4. **Star fruit is never allowed** at any stage of kidney disease ([NKF][DG17]; [de Oliveira 2015][DG39]).
5. **Bring questions to every visit.** Each page ends with "Ask your care team", and there is a
   printable list on [Questions to ask](reference/questions-to-ask.md).

| eGFR 45–59 | [Stage G3a](stages/g3a.md) |
| eGFR 30–44 | [Stage G3b](stages/g3b.md) |
| eGFR 15–29 | [Stage G4](stages/g4.md) |
| eGFR under 15, not on dialysis | [Stage G5 without dialysis](stages/g5-without-dialysis.md) |
| Hemodialysis at a center | [In-center hemodialysis](stages/hemodialysis-in-centre.md) |

| Peritoneal dialysis | [Peritoneal dialysis](stages/peritoneal-dialysis.md) |
| Waiting for, or living with, a transplant | [Before a transplant](stages/before-transplant.md) · [After a transplant](stages/after-transplant.md) |

eGFR is in mL/min/1.73 m² on your lab report ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 2). Stages are labels for
risk and planning, not a countdown. Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes of
CKD in adults ([NIDDK][NIDDK-CKD]). Most people in the early stages feel nothing, which is why the yearly blood
and urine tests matter ([NIDDK][NIDDK-CKDwhat]).

## If you help someone with CKD

- [ ] Learn the signs of a low and how to treat one, and how to give glucagon
      ([Treating a low](t1d/treating-a-low.md)).
- [ ] Read [Get help now](get-help-now.md) and keep a [wallet card](reference/wallet-card.md) at home.
- [ ] Ask to join visits and education sessions ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.3.2).
- [ ] Look after yourself too ([Caregivers](living/caregivers.md)).

## Where your numbers come from

- **Your lab report:** eGFR, urine albumin (UACR), potassium, phosphate and more. Your lab's reference
  range wins over any typical range here ([Lab results](labs/index.md)).
- **Your care team:** your targets for potassium, phosphorus, sodium, protein, fluid and carbohydrate.
- **The app:** "Suggest targets" gives starting points from the same tables as this handbook, labeled
  "discuss with your care team" ([Targets and warnings](app/targets-and-warnings.md)). When the app
  shows a warning, it links to the page here that explains it.

## How to read this handbook

- **Units:** pages with lab numbers have **US units** and **International** tabs. Pick one and every
  page follows. Try it:

    === "US units"

        A glucose under **70 mg/dL** is a low. Treat it now ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).

    === "International"

        A glucose under **3.9 mmol/L** is a low. Treat it now ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).

    To convert any result yourself, see [Units and conversions](reference/units.md).

- **Draft banner:** every page says "Draft: not yet reviewed by a clinician" until a named clinician
  has reviewed it. The line at the bottom of each page shows when its facts were last checked
  ([About this handbook](reference/about.md)).
- **Sources:** each page ends with its sources; [all sources](reference/sources.md) are listed with
  their links and the date they were checked.
- **Words:** hover over or tap an abbreviation such as eGFR to see what it means, or open the
  [Glossary](reference/glossary.md).
- **Printing:** pages print cleanly. Useful ones to print are [Get help now](get-help-now.md), the
  [wallet cards](reference/wallet-card.md), [Questions to ask](reference/questions-to-ask.md) and the
  [grocery lists](eat/grocery-lists.md).

## Who should see a specialist first

This handbook is written for adults.

- **Children and teenagers** with CKD need a pediatric kidney team; KDIGO gives them separate advice
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], pediatric considerations).
- **Pregnancy, or planning one,** with CKD and type 1 diabetes needs a nephrologist and a
  maternal-fetal medicine team **before** you try, because some kidney medicines must be changed
  first ([UK Kidney Association 2019][UKKA19]; [ADA 2026 §15][A26-15];
  [Sex, fertility and pregnancy](living/sex-fertility-pregnancy.md)).

## Using the handbook with the app

The app is a food log for kidney disease and type 1 diabetes. It does not give doses and is not a
medical device ([About the app](app/index.md)).

- Log meals and see potassium, phosphorus, sodium, protein, fluid and carbohydrate add up
  ([Logging food](app/logging.md)).
- Plan a day ahead and use the sample menus from this handbook
  ([Planning and menus](app/planning-and-menus.md); [Sample menus](eat/menus/index.md)).
- Bring a summary to your dietitian ([Reports for your team](app/reports-for-your-team.md)).

## Outside the US

Use your local emergency number (112 across the EU, 999 in the UK, 000 in Australia). Your lab may use
international units; the tabs on each page show both. Benefits and services differ by country; your
national kidney charity can tell you what is available.
