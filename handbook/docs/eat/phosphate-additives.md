# Phosphate additives

## In short

Food makers add phosphates to keep food moist, help it rise, or stop it separating. Your body
absorbs about 90–100 % of this added phosphorus, far more than the phosphorus in plants or meat
([Kalantar-Zadeh 2010][DG12]). Phosphorus does not have to be listed on the US Nutrition Facts
label, so **the ingredient list is the only clue** ([NKF][DG20]; [NKF label guide][DG38]).
Any ingredient with "phos" in it means the food counts as high in phosphorus, whatever the number.

## The "PHOS" list

Look for these words in the ingredient list ([NIDDK][DG4]; [NKF][DG20]):

- phosphoric acid
- mono-, di- or trisodium phosphate
- sodium hexametaphosphate, sodium tripolyphosphate
- tetrasodium pyrophosphate, sodium acid pyrophosphate
- di- or tricalcium phosphate, monocalcium phosphate
- potassium or dipotassium phosphate
- anything ending in "pyrophosphate" or "polyphosphate"

## Where additives usually hide

- Dark colas and pepper sodas (also *diet* colas: about 27 mg per 12 oz measured, 41–68 mg for
  some brands; clear diet sodas have none) ([measured beverage phosphorus][DG25b]; [USDA][FDC])
- Bottled coffee and tea drinks, non-dairy creamers ([DaVita: creamers][DG30b])
- Processed and spreadable cheese, cheese sauce
- Instant pudding; cake, pancake and biscuit mixes; baking powder
- "Enhanced", "self-basting" or "injected" fresh meat and poultry; deli meats and hot dogs
- Frozen entrées, pizza and fast food
- Some "fortified" cereals and plant milks, oat milk especially ([NKF: milk alternatives][DG28b])

([Review of phosphate additives][DG18]; [NKF][DG20].) The app flags these foods as
`phosphate_additive` and always rates them high for phosphorus.

## What to do

- [ ] Read the ingredient list before the Nutrition Facts panel.
- [ ] If you see "phos", choose another brand or another food.
- [ ] Buy plain fresh meat and poultry; skip packages that say "enhanced" or "contains up to X % of
      a solution".
- [ ] Swap dark colas for water, clear sodas, or tea you brew yourself.
- [ ] Check plant milks and creamers: pick one with no phosphate in the ingredients.

## Examples

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| Cola (phosphoric acid) | Lemon-lime soda or water with lemon |
| Processed American cheese | A small piece of natural cheese, if your team allows |
| Pancakes or biscuits from a mix | Toast or an English muffin |
| Deli turkey or ham | Turkey breast you roast yourself |
| Bottled iced coffee | Coffee brewed at home with a splash of half-and-half |

## Ask your care team

1. Is my phosphate high enough that I need to avoid all additives, or just cut down?
2. Which of the foods I eat most often have additives?
3. Is my plant milk or creamer OK?

## Get help now if…

- Additives do not cause sudden emergencies. For urgent symptoms, see [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
