# Recipes

## In short

These are twelve everyday recipes written for this handbook: three breakfasts, three lunches, four
dinners and two snacks or desserts. Every recipe shows **carbohydrate per serving** for your insulin
plan, and potassium, phosphorus, sodium, protein and fluid per serving. The numbers are computed
from USDA FoodData Central values for each ingredient ([FDC][FDC]) and checked by a script, so they
stay correct when a recipe changes. No recipe uses salt, salt substitutes, or foods with phosphate
additives.

## How the recipes are checked

Each serving is checked against these lines. They are review lines to help you choose, not limits
your team set for you.

| Per serving | A meal (breakfast, lunch, dinner) | A snack or dessert |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium, every recipe | 600 mg or less | 140 mg or less |
| "Lower potassium" tag | 600 mg or less | 200 mg or less |
| "Lower phosphorus" tag | 300 mg or less | 100 mg or less |
| "Dialysis-friendly" tag | lower potassium and lower phosphorus, at least 20 g protein, and 120 mL fluid or less | lower potassium and lower phosphorus, 120 mL fluid or less |

Where the lines come from:

- **600 mg per meal** of sodium or potassium is a review line used in renal diet teaching
  ([University of Michigan][DG47]). Three meals at 600 mg plus snacks still fit a 2,000 mg sodium day.
- **300 mg of phosphorus per meal** is a third of the 900 mg starting ceiling at stage 5
  ([Eating well](../index.md#your-numbers)).
- For snacks, **200 mg potassium** is where NKF calls a serving "high" ([NKF][DG5]), **100 mg
  phosphorus** is AKF's "low" line ([AKF][DG46b]), and **140 mg sodium** is the FDA meaning of "low
  sodium" ([FDA][DG37a]).

## How to use them

- [ ] **Check the carbohydrate box first.** Most meal recipes have 34–51 g; the soup has 22 g. Each one says
      what to add to reach about 55 g, the amount the [sample menus](../menus/index.md) use. Your own
      amount comes from your diabetes team.
- [ ] **Check the serving size.** The numbers are per serving. A bigger plate means bigger numbers.
- [ ] **Match the protein to your stage.** The meat and fish portions are 1½–3 oz cooked; the dinners
      use 2½–3 oz, which suits dialysis. Before dialysis, each recipe says how to make a smaller protein portion
      ([Protein](../protein.md)).
- [ ] **Count soup as fluid** if you have a fluid limit ([Fluid](../fluid.md)).
- [ ] **Cook meat and fish to a safe temperature**, and use a food thermometer: poultry 165 °F (74 °C);
      fish, and pork chops and roasts 145 °F (63 °C) ([FoodSafety.gov][FSTEMPS]). This matters most
      after a transplant.
- [ ] **Log it in the app.** Each recipe lists the foods and grams per serving to log, or you can save
      them once as a meal ([Planning and menus](../../app/planning-and-menus.md)).

## Cooking without salt or phosphate

- **Season with acid, heat and herbs.** Lemon or lime juice, vinegar, black pepper, garlic, onion,
  ginger, paprika, oregano, dill and thyme. Use garlic *powder*, not garlic *salt*.
- **No salt substitutes.** "NoSalt", "Lite Salt" and similar products contain potassium chloride
  ([Sodium](../sodium.md)).
- **Skip baking powder and mixes.** Most baking powders and pancake or biscuit mixes contain a
  phosphate: USDA lists 2,000–10,000 mg of phosphorus per 100 g of baking powder, and the low-sodium
  kind also has about 10,000 mg of potassium per 100 g ([FDC][FDC]; [review of phosphate additives][DG18]). Crepes and yeast doughs do not need it
  ([Phosphate additives](../phosphate-additives.md)).
- **Cook grains and pasta without salt,** and rinse canned vegetables and beans, which removes some of
  the sodium ([FDA][DG34]).
- **Buy plain meat and fish.** Skip packages that say "enhanced", "self-basting" or "contains up to
  X % of a solution" ([Grocery shopping](../grocery-shopping.md)).

## Ask your care team

1. How much carbohydrate should a meal have for me, and does a dessert need its own insulin?
2. How much meat or fish should I eat at one meal before dialysis, or on dialysis?
3. Which of these recipes fit my potassium and phosphorus limits, if I have them?
4. Can you help me change a family favourite recipe so it fits my diet?

## Get help now if…

- You have a low you cannot treat yourself, chest pain or severe breathlessness: call 911 (or your
  local emergency number). See [Get help now](../../get-help-now.md).
