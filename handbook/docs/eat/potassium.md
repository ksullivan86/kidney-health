# Potassium

## In short

Potassium keeps your nerves, muscles and heart rhythm working. About 90 % of the potassium you eat
leaves through the kidneys, so as kidney function falls, blood potassium can rise. A very high level
can cause a dangerous heart rhythm ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). **Only limit potassium if your blood potassium
is high; your care team sets the number** ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 6.4.1). When a limit is needed,
the biggest wins are portion size, processed foods with potassium additives, and a few very high foods
such as bananas, potatoes, tomato sauce and orange juice.

## Your numbers

--8<-- "includes/starting-points.md"

Blood potassium is the same number in mmol/L and mEq/L, so one table covers both unit systems.

| | Typical |
|---|---|
| Blood potassium | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L (= mEq/L); your lab's range wins |
| High (hyperkalaemia) | above 5.0–5.5 mmol/L |
| Call your team today | 6.0–6.4 mmol/L ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28) |
| Emergency | 6.5 mmol/L or more ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28) |
| Food, when a limit is ordered | usually 2,000–3,000 mg a day; AKF suggests aiming for 2,500 mg and no more than 3,000 mg ([AKF][DG52]; [DaVita][DG26]) |
| Peritoneal dialysis | often 3,000–4,000 mg; some people run low and need more ([DaVita][DG25a]) |
| The app's starting ceilings | 4,000 mg (stages 1–3a), 3,500 (3b), 3,000 (4), 2,500 (5 and hemodialysis), 3,500 (peritoneal dialysis): review ceilings, not prescriptions |

**Why it rises.** In people with diabetes, high potassium affects 8.8 % at stage G3 with little albumin
in the urine and 34.4 % at G5 with a lot ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 30). Food is only one cause. Not
enough insulin, high blood glucose, ACE inhibitors and ARBs, finerenone, constipation and acid build-up
push it up too ([KDIGO 2024][K24]; [Sousa 2016][DG36]). So a run of high glucose readings, a missed basal
insulin dose, or a new blood-pressure or kidney-protecting medicine is a reason to be stricter that week
and to expect a blood test ([KDIGO 2022][K22]). See [Blood potassium](../labs/blood-potassium.md).

## How much is in a serving

| Per serving you actually eat | Potassium |
|---|---|
| Low (AKF) | 150 mg or less |
| Medium (AKF) | 151–250 mg |
| High (AKF) | 251 mg or more |
| High (NKF) | 200 mg or more |

([AKF potassium guide][DG46a]; [NKF][DG5]; NKF's shopping cue is 200 mg or less per serving,
[NKF label guide][DG38].) The app marks a single food "medium" at 101–200 mg and "high" above 200 mg.

**Portion size matters most.** "A large serving of a low-potassium food becomes a high-potassium
food" ([NKF][DG5]). Half a cup of cantaloupe is about 214 mg; a full cup is 427 mg ([FDC][FDC]).

**Potassium is on the label.** Since 2020 the US Nutrition Facts label must show potassium in
milligrams ([FDA][FDA-label]). The % Daily Value is based on 4,700 mg ([FDA: Daily Values][DG16b]),
far more than a kidney limit, so read the milligrams ([Reading food labels](label-reading.md)).

## Hidden potassium

Not all potassium is absorbed the same way ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 33, from [Picard 2021][DG30a]):

| Source | How much your body absorbs |
|---|---|
| Fresh fruit, vegetables, beans and grains | about 50–60 % |
| Meat, fish and dairy | about 70–90 % |
| **Potassium additives** (potassium chloride, potassium lactate and other potassium salts) | **about 90 %** |

KDIGO notes that processed foods, meats, dairy, juices and salt substitutes are "actually higher in
absorbable potassium than many plant-based fresh foods". It advises limiting foods rich in
*bioavailable* potassium, such as processed foods, when potassium runs high ([KDIGO 2024][K24],
practice point 3.11.5.2). Where hidden potassium turns up:

- **Salt substitutes** such as NoSalt, Nu-Salt and Lite Salt: they are potassium chloride ([Sodium](sodium.md)).
- **"Reduced-sodium" soups, broths, sauces and deli meats**, which often swap salt for potassium chloride.
- **"Enhanced" fresh meat and poultry**, injected with a salt and phosphate solution. In one study, 5 of
  25 enhanced products had at least 692 mg of potassium per 100 g, against under 387 mg in plain meat,
  and 8 of the 25 did not list the additives ([Sherman and Mehta 2009][SHERMAN09]).

## What to do

- [ ] Ask what your last blood potassium was and whether you need a limit at all.
- [ ] If you do: keep fruit and vegetables to ½-cup portions and pick from the low list
      ([Food lists](food-lists.md)).
- [ ] Swap the few very high foods (table below) before cutting healthy fruit and vegetables.
- [ ] Read ingredient lists for "potassium chloride" and other potassium salts.
- [ ] Buy plain meat and poultry, not "enhanced" or "contains up to X % of a solution".
- [ ] Never use salt substitutes such as NoSalt, Nu-Salt or Lite Salt unless your team says so.
- [ ] Drain the juice from canned fruit and vegetables; it holds potassium ([NKF][DG5]).
- [ ] Leach potatoes and other root vegetables if you eat them ([Potassium leaching](potassium-leaching.md)).
- [ ] Keep your bowels moving and your glucose in range: constipation and high glucose raise potassium.
- [ ] **Never delay treating a low because of potassium.** Glucose tablets have none, and a 4-oz glass
      of orange juice (about 250 mg) still fits a day's budget ([Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md)).

## Examples: swaps that save the most

| Instead of | Potassium | Try | Potassium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banana, 1 medium | 422 mg | Blueberries, ½ cup | 57 mg |
| Baked potato with skin, 1 medium | 926 mg | White rice, ½ cup | 28 mg |
| Orange juice, 1 cup | 496 mg | Apple juice, ½ cup | 125 mg |
| Tomato sauce, ½ cup | 365 mg | Olive oil and garlic on pasta | about 0–25 mg |
| Spinach, cooked, ½ cup | 419 mg | Cabbage, cooked, ½ cup / green beans, ½ cup | 147 / 92 mg |
| Milk, 2 % fat, 1 cup | 342 mg | Almond milk, unsweetened, 1 cup / rice milk, 1 cup | 176 / 65 mg |
| Avocado, ¼ | 242 mg | Cucumber, ½ cup | 76 mg |
| Potato chips, 1 oz | 335 mg | Air-popped popcorn, 3 cups | 79 mg |

Values from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy), as in the app's food list ([FDC][FDC]). Rice milk is
low in potassium but not in phosphorus, so check both if you limit phosphorus.

## Example: one day, two ways

The same four meals, with the high-potassium foods swapped ([FDC][FDC]):

| Meal | Higher-potassium version | K mg | Swapped version | K mg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 1 cup oatmeal, 1 medium banana, 1 cup 2 % milk | 928 | 1 cup Cream of Wheat, ¾ cup blueberries, 1 cup unsweetened almond milk, 1 slice toast | 338 |
| Lunch | 6-inch deli sub, 1 oz potato chips | 884 | Sandwich with 2 oz turkey you roast yourself, lettuce and mayonnaise, ½ cup grapes | 413 |
| Dinner | 3 oz chicken, baked potato with skin, ½ cup cooked spinach | 1,565 | 3 oz chicken, ¾ cup white rice, ½ cup green beans | 353 |
| Snack | 1 cup cantaloupe | 427 | 3 cups air-popped popcorn | 79 |
| **Day** | | **about 3,800** | | **about 1,200** |

The swapped day saves about 2,600 mg, which leaves room for fruit and vegetables you enjoy. The
carbohydrate changes too (about 177 g against 161 g), so count each meal as usual
([Carb counting](carb-counting.md)).

    Every food shows a potassium warning per serving, and your day shows how close you are to your
    potassium target. If you add a potassium result under **Labs**, the app adjusts its review ceiling:
    a normal result relaxes it one step, a high result tightens it, and a low result removes it
    ([design note 05][NOTE05]). See [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md) and

## Ask your care team

1. What was my last potassium result, and do I need a limit? What number?
2. Which of my medicines raise potassium, and when is my next blood test after a change?
3. Can I eat more fruit and vegetables while my potassium is normal?
4. Should I take a potassium binder, and how does it fit with my meals?
5. What should I do if my potassium comes back high but I feel fine?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Your lab potassium is **6.5 mmol/L or more**, or you have fainting, a very slow or irregular pulse,
    chest pain or sudden severe weakness and your potassium has been high ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    A result of **6.0–6.4 mmol/L**: KDIGO advises assessment in hospital if you feel unwell, otherwise
    a repeat test within 24 hours ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28). Also call if vomiting or diarrhoea stops
    you eating or drinking. Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
