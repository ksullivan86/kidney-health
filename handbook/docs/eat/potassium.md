# Potassium

## In short

Potassium keeps your nerves and heart rhythm working, and about 90 % of the potassium you eat
leaves through the kidneys. As kidney function falls, blood potassium can rise, and very high
potassium can stop the heart ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). **Only limit potassium if your blood potassium
is high; your care team sets the number.** When a limit is needed, the biggest wins are portion
size, processed foods with potassium additives, and a few very high foods such as bananas,
potatoes, tomato sauce and orange juice.

## Your numbers

| | Typical |
|---|---|
| Blood potassium | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L (the same number in mEq/L); your lab's range wins |
| High (hyperkalaemia) | above 5.0–5.5 mmol/L |
| Emergency | 6.5 mmol/L or more ([KDIGO 2024][K24]) |
| Food, when a limit is ordered | usually 2,000–3,000 mg a day; AKF suggests aiming for 2,500 mg and no more than 3,000 mg ([AKF][DG52]; [DaVita][DG26]) |
| The app's starting ceilings | 4,000 mg (stages 1–3a), 3,500 (3b), 3,000 (4), 2,500 (5 and hemodialysis), 3,500 (peritoneal dialysis): review ceilings, not prescriptions |

**Why it rises.** As GFR falls, blood potassium rises. In people with diabetes, high potassium
affects 8.8 % at stage G3 with little albumin in the urine and 34.4 % at G5 with a lot
([KDIGO 2024][K24]). Things other than food push it up too: not enough insulin, high blood
glucose, ACE inhibitors and ARBs, finerenone, constipation and acidosis ([KDIGO 2024][K24];
[Sousa 2016][DG36]). So a run of high glucose readings, a missed basal insulin dose or a new
blood-pressure or kidney-protecting medicine are reasons to be stricter with potassium that week
and to expect a blood test ([KDIGO 2022][K22]; [Sousa 2016][DG36]).

## How much is in a serving

| Per serving you actually eat | Potassium |
|---|---|
| Low (AKF) | 150 mg or less |
| Medium (AKF) | 151–250 mg |
| High (AKF) | 251 mg or more |
| High (NKF) | 200 mg or more |

([AKF potassium guide][DG46a]; [NKF][DG5]; NKF's shopping cue is 200 mg or less per serving,
[NKF label guide][DG38].) The app marks a single food "medium" at 101–200 mg and "high" above
200 mg ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

**Portion size matters most.** "A large serving of a low-potassium food becomes a high-potassium
food" ([NKF][DG5]): ½ cup of cantaloupe is about 194–208 mg, a full cup 388–417 mg.

**Additives are absorbed best.** About 90 % of potassium from additives such as potassium chloride
is absorbed, compared with 50–60 % from whole plant foods and 70–90 % from animal foods
([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 33, from [Picard 2021][DG30a]). That is why KDIGO advises limiting
foods rich in *bioavailable* potassium, such as processed foods, when potassium runs high
(practice point 3.11.5.2). Look for "potassium chloride", "potassium lactate" and other potassium
salts in ingredient lists ([Reading labels](label-reading.md)).

## What to do

- [ ] Ask what your last blood potassium was and whether you need a limit at all.
- [ ] If you do: keep fruit and vegetables to ½-cup portions and pick from the low list
      ([Food lists](food-lists.md)).
- [ ] Swap the few very high foods (table below) before cutting healthy fruit and vegetables.
- [ ] Never use salt substitutes such as NoSalt, Nu-Salt or Lite Salt: they are potassium
      chloride ([Sodium](sodium.md)).
- [ ] Leach potatoes and other root vegetables if you eat them ([Potassium leaching](potassium-leaching.md)).
- [ ] Keep your bowels moving and your glucose in range: constipation and high glucose raise
      potassium.
- [ ] **Never delay treating a low because of potassium.** A 4-oz glass of orange juice is about
      250 mg, which a day's budget absorbs ([Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md)).

## Examples: swaps that save the most

| Instead of | Potassium | Try | Potassium |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banana, 1 medium | 422 mg | Blueberries, ½ cup | 57 mg |
| Baked potato with skin, 1 medium | 926 mg | White rice, ½ cup | 28 mg |
| Orange juice, 1 cup | 496 mg | Apple juice, ½ cup | 125 mg |
| Tomato sauce, ½ cup | 364 mg | Olive oil and garlic on pasta | about 0–25 mg |
| Spinach or Swiss chard, cooked, ½ cup | 419–480 mg | Cabbage, cooked, ½ cup / green beans, ½ cup | 147 / 91 mg |
| Milk, 1 % fat, 1 cup | 366 mg | Almond milk, unsweetened, 1 cup / rice milk, 1 cup | 176 / 65 mg |
| Avocado, ½ | 487 mg | Cucumber, ½ cup | 76 mg |
| Potato chips, 1 oz | 335 mg | Unsalted pretzels, 1 oz / air-popped popcorn, 2 cups | 41 / 53 mg |

Values from USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy), as in the app's food list ([FDC][FDC]).

## Ask your care team

1. What was my last potassium result, and do I need a limit? What number?
2. Which of my medicines raise potassium, and when is my next blood test after a change?
3. Can I keep eating more fruit and vegetables if my potassium is normal?
4. Should I take a potassium binder, and how does it fit with my meals?
5. What should I do if my potassium comes back high but I feel fine?

## Get help now if…

- Your lab potassium is **6.5 mmol/L or more**, or you have new weakness, a very slow or irregular
  pulse, or fainting with a known high potassium: call 911 (or your local emergency number).
- A result of **6.0–6.4 mmol/L**: call your team **today**; KDIGO advises assessment in hospital
  if you feel unwell, otherwise a repeat test within 24 hours ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28).
- See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md) and [Blood potassium](../labs/blood-potassium.md).
