# Sodium

## In short

Sodium holds water in the body. When the kidneys cannot get rid of it, the extra water raises
blood pressure, causes swelling, increases protein in the urine and, on dialysis, makes you thirsty
enough to break a fluid limit ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2024][K24]). Aim for **under 2,000 mg a
day** at every stage. Most sodium comes from packaged and restaurant food, not the salt shaker.
**Salt substitutes are potassium chloride and are not safe without your team's OK.**

| 1 teaspoon of table salt | about 2,326 mg sodium |
| Average US intake | about 3,400 mg a day ([FDA][DG34]) |
| From packaged and restaurant food | more than 70 % of the sodium people eat ([FDA][DG34]) |
| A restaurant meal | often 1,000–3,000 mg ([Harvard Health: the salty six][DG35a]) |

The app marks a single food "medium" at 141–400 mg and "high" above 400 mg
([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## Reading the label

| On the label | Means |
|---|---|
| 5 % DV or less (115 mg or less) | low |
| 20 % DV or more (460 mg or more) | high |
| "Sodium-free" | under 5 mg per serving |
| "Very low sodium" | 35 mg or less |
| "Low sodium" | 140 mg or less |
| "Reduced sodium" | 25 % less than the original, which can still be a lot |

([FDA][DG34]; [FDA claim definitions][DG37a]; [NIDDK][DG4].) NKF's rule of thumb: 240 mg or
less per serving, and fewer milligrams of sodium than calories ([NKF label guide][DG38]). The
label's Daily Value for sodium is 2,300 mg, more than your target.

## Salt substitutes

"NoSalt", "Nu-Salt", "Lite Salt", "half salt" and many "low-sodium" soups and broths are made with
**potassium chloride**. NoSalt lists 640 mg potassium per ¼ teaspoon; Morton Lite Salt 350 mg
potassium *plus* 290 mg sodium per ¼ teaspoon, with a label warning against use on sodium- or
potassium-restricted diets without a doctor's approval; Nu-Salt 530 mg per labelled serving
([Morton Lite Salt label][DG35b]; [NKF][DG20]). Potassium from potassium chloride is about 90 %
absorbed, far more than the potassium in fruit ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). Kidney groups have opposed
allowing potassium-chloride salt substitutes in standard foods for this reason ([AAKP letter][DG33]).
NIDDK, NKF and AKF all say to use herbs, spices, lemon and vinegar instead ([NIDDK][DG4];
[NKF][DG5]). Use garlic *powder*, not garlic *salt*.

## What to do

- [ ] Cook at home more often, from fresh food.
- [ ] Take the salt shaker off the table; season with herbs, spices, pepper, lemon and vinegar.
- [ ] Use a salt-free blend **only** if its label does not list potassium chloride.
- [ ] Check the label: under 140 mg per serving is "low sodium"; 460 mg or more is high.
- [ ] Rinse canned vegetables and choose "no salt added" versions.
- [ ] When eating out, ask for "no salt, please" and sauces on the side ([Eating out](eating-out.md)).

## Ask your care team

1. Is my target under 2,000 mg or under 2,300 mg?
2. Is my blood pressure where you want it? Should I check it at home?
3. Is any salt substitute safe for me? (Usually the answer is no.)
4. On dialysis: how much is my thirst affecting my fluid gains?

## Get help now if…

- You are suddenly very short of breath, cannot lie flat, or have a frothy cough (fluid overload):
  call 911 (or your local emergency number).
- New swelling, much less urine, or weight gain above your team's alarm number: call your team
  today. See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
