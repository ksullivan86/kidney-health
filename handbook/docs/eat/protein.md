# Protein

## In short

Protein builds and repairs muscle. Its waste (urea) is cleared by the kidneys, so before dialysis eating
a lot of protein makes failing kidneys work harder. Eating too *little* is also harmful: it causes muscle
loss, and with insulin it raises the risk of lows. With diabetes, aim for about **0.8 g per kg a day
before dialysis** and **1.0–1.2 g per kg on dialysis**, and avoid more than 1.3 g per kg
([KDIGO 2024][K24]; [KDIGO 2022][K22]; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]).

## Your numbers

--8<-- "includes/starting-points.md"

| | Per kg a day | For 60 kg | For 70 kg | For 80 kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stages 1–2 | 0.8 (up to 1.0) | 48–60 g | 56–70 g | 64–80 g |
| Stages 3–5 with diabetes, no dialysis | **0.8** | about 48 g | about 56 g | about 64 g |
| Hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis | **1.0–1.2** | 60–72 g | 70–84 g | 80–96 g |
| Avoid more than | 1.3 | 78 g | 91 g | 104 g |

- **With diabetes, not below 0.8 g/kg before dialysis.** KDIGO 2024 (recommendation 3.3.1.1), KDIGO 2022
  (recommendation 3.1.1) and ADA 2026 (recommendation 11.3) all say 0.8. ADA adds that going lower "is not
  recommended" with diabetes because it "does not alter blood glucose levels, cardiovascular risk
  measures, or the course of GFR decline" ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]). KDOQI 2020 statement 3.0.2 allows
  0.6–0.8 g/kg only "under close clinical supervision" in people who are metabolically stable, which
  excludes poorly controlled diabetes ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]). The app and this handbook therefore use 0.8
  ([design note 05][NOTE05]); your dietitian may set a different number.
- **On dialysis protein goes up** to 1.0–1.2 g/kg (KDOQI 2020 statements 3.0.3–3.0.4; ADA 2026
  recommendation 11.3), often the upper end on peritoneal dialysis, because protein is lost into the
  dialysis fluid ([KDIGO 2022][K22], practice point 3.1.2).
- **Too much:** more than 1.3 g/kg a day is linked to faster loss of kidney function, more albumin in the
  urine and heart deaths ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.1.1; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]).
- **Too little:** below 0.6 g/kg risks malnutrition and lows ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2022][K22]).
- **Older adults who are frail or losing muscle** may need *more* protein and calories, not less
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.1.5).
- **Children** with CKD should not have protein restricted; their kidney team sets it for growth
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.1.4). In pregnancy, needs change too: ask your specialist team
  ([Sex, fertility and pregnancy](../living/sex-fertility-pregnancy.md)).
- **"Per kg" means per kg of a reference weight** your team chooses, not always what the scale says. The
  app uses your weight unless you are above or below the healthy BMI range for your height, and tells you
  which weight it used ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## What a portion looks like

A 3-oz cooked portion of meat or fish is about the size of a deck of cards and gives about 19–27 g of
protein. Each protein brings some potassium and phosphorus with it:

| Food | Portion | Protein | Potassium | Phosphorus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast, roasted | 3 oz | 27 g | 220 mg | 196 mg |
| Turkey breast, roasted (not deli) | 3 oz | 26 g | 212 mg | 196 mg |
| Pork loin chop, broiled | 3 oz | 23 g | 303 mg | 194 mg |
| Ground beef, 90 % lean | 3 oz | 22 g | 283 mg | 172 mg |
| Cod, baked | 3 oz | 19 g | 207 mg | 117 mg |
| Tuna, light, canned in water, drained | 3 oz | 17 g | 152 mg | 118 mg |
| Egg whites | 2 large | 7.2 g | 108 mg | 10 mg |
| Egg, whole | 1 large | 6.3 g | 69 mg | 99 mg |
| Tofu, firm | ¼ block (about 3 oz) | 7.3 g | 120 mg | 98 mg |
| Black beans, cooked | ½ cup | 7.6 g | 305 mg | 120 mg (plant, less absorbed) |
| Milk, 2 % fat | 1 cup | 8.1 g | 342 mg | 224 mg |
| White rice, cooked | ½ cup | 2.1 g | 28 mg | 34 mg |
| White bread | 1 slice | 2.6 g | 37 mg | 28 mg |

From the app's USDA-based food list ([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]; [Food lists](food-lists.md)). Egg
whites, chicken, turkey and fish give the most protein for the least phosphorus. Canned tuna has 210 mg
sodium per 3 oz, so rinse it.

**Plant protein counts.** KDIGO advises a diet with more plant foods than animal foods and fewer
ultra-processed foods ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.1). Beans and tofu fit if your potassium and
phosphate allow; their phosphorus is less absorbed than the phosphorus in meat ([Phosphorus](phosphorus.md)).

## What to do

- [ ] Ask your team which protein target and which weight to use, and enter it in the app.
- [ ] Spread protein over the day: some at breakfast, lunch and dinner.
- [ ] Before dialysis, keep meat to about 2 oz at lunch and dinner for a 70 kg adult
      (see the [sample menus](menus/index.md)); on dialysis, 3 oz or more.
- [ ] Use egg whites when you need protein with little phosphorus.
- [ ] If you are losing weight or not hungry, tell your team: do not cut protein further
      ([Eating enough](eating-enough.md)).
- [ ] When you shrink a meat portion to fit, 1 oz less chicken saves about 9 g of protein and 65 mg of
      phosphorus.
- [ ] Skip protein powders, bars and creatine unless your kidney team approves them: NKF lists
      creatine among supplements to avoid with kidney disease ([NKF][NKFherb];
      [Supplements](../medicines/supplements.md)).

## Examples

- **Stage 4, 70 kg, with diabetes:** about 56 g a day: an egg and toast at breakfast, a sandwich with
  1½ oz of turkey at lunch, 2 oz of chicken with rice at dinner, and bread, rice and snacks for the rest.
  See the [stage 4–5 menu](menus/g4-g5.md).
- **Hemodialysis, 70 kg:** 70–84 g a day: add egg whites at breakfast and 3 oz or more of meat or fish at
  lunch and dinner. See the [hemodialysis menu](menus/hemodialysis.md).
- **Appetite falls on dialysis:** if food alone does not meet your needs, KDOQI suggests trying oral
  nutrition supplements for at least 3 months; your dietitian chooses one that fits your potassium,
  phosphorus and fluid ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 4.1.1).

!!! tip "In the app"
    The app suggests a protein range from your stage and weight and judges protein on your **weekly
    average**, so one big or small day does not matter. Single foods with more than 25 g of protein in a
    serving show a "high" note as a portion reminder ([architecture contract][ARCH]). See
    [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md).

## Ask your care team

1. What is my protein target in grams, and which weight did you use?
2. Should my protein change now that my kidney function has changed, or now that I am on dialysis?
3. Is plant protein (beans, tofu, nuts) OK for me, given potassium and phosphorus?
4. I am older or losing muscle: should my target be higher?
5. If I cannot eat enough, which nutrition drink would you suggest?

## Get help now if…

!!! warning "Call your care team"
    You are losing weight without trying, have no appetite for more than a few days, or cannot eat for a
    day or two. With insulin, eating much less also raises the risk of lows: follow your
    [sick-day plan](../t1d/sick-days.md). For urgent symptoms, see [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
