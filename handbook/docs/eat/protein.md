# Protein

## In short

Protein builds and repairs muscle. Its waste (urea) is cleared by the kidneys, so before dialysis
eating a lot of protein makes failing kidneys work harder. Eating too *little* is also harmful: it
causes muscle loss, and with insulin it raises the risk of lows. With diabetes, aim for about
**0.8 g per kg a day before dialysis** and **1.0–1.2 g per kg on dialysis**, and avoid more than
1.3 g per kg ([KDIGO 2024][K24]; [KDIGO 2022][K22]; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]).

## Your numbers

--8<-- "includes/starting-points.md"

| | Per kg a day | For 60 kg | For 70 kg | For 80 kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stages 1–2 | 0.8 (up to 1.0) | 48–60 g | 56–70 g | 64–80 g |
| Stages 3–5 with diabetes, no dialysis | **0.8** | about 48 g | about 56 g | about 64 g |
| Hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis | **1.0–1.2** | 60–72 g | 70–84 g | 80–96 g |
| Avoid more than | 1.3 | 78 g | 91 g | 104 g |

- **With diabetes, not below 0.8 g/kg before dialysis.** KDIGO 2024 (Rec 3.3.1.1, 2C), KDIGO 2022
  (Rec 3.1.1) and ADA 2026 (Rec 11.3, grade A) all say 0.8; ADA adds that going lower "is not
  recommended" with diabetes because it "does not alter blood glucose levels, cardiovascular risk
  measures, or the course of GFR decline" ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]). KDOQI 2020 statement 3.0.2
  allows 0.6–0.8 g/kg only "under close clinical supervision" in people who are metabolically
  stable, which excludes poorly controlled diabetes ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]). The app and this handbook
  therefore use 0.8 ([design note 05][NOTE05]); your dietitian may set a different number.
- **On dialysis protein goes up** to 1.0–1.2 g/kg (KDOQI 2020 3.0.3–3.0.4; ADA 2026 Rec 11.3,
  grade B), often the upper end on peritoneal dialysis, because protein is lost into the dialysis
  fluid ([KDIGO 2022][K22], practice point 3.1.2).
- **Too much:** more than 1.3 g/kg a day is linked to faster loss of kidney function, more albumin
  in the urine and heart deaths ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.1.1; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]).
- **Too little:** below 0.6 g/kg risks malnutrition and lows ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2022][K22]).
- **"Per kg" means per kg of a reference weight** your team chooses, not always what the scale
  says. The app uses your weight unless you are above or below the healthy BMI range for your
  height, and tells you which weight it used ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## What a portion looks like

| Food | Portion | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast, roasted | 3 oz (a deck of cards) | 26 g |
| Turkey breast, roasted (not deli) | 3 oz | 26 g |
| Cod, baked | 3 oz | 19 g |
| Egg, whole | 1 large | 6.3 g |
| Egg whites | 2 large | 7.2 g |
| Tofu, firm | ¼ block (about 3 oz) | 7.3 g |
| White rice, cooked | ½ cup | 2.1 g |
| White bread | 1 slice | 2.6 g |

([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]; [Food lists](food-lists.md).) A 3-oz cooked portion of meat or
fish (the size of a deck of cards) gives about 21–25 g of protein. Plant protein counts too, and
bread, rice and pasta add a few grams each.

## What to do

- [ ] Ask your team which protein target and which weight to use, and enter it in the app.
- [ ] Spread protein over the day: some at breakfast, lunch and dinner.
- [ ] Before dialysis, keep meat to about 2 oz at lunch and dinner for a 70 kg adult
      (see the [sample menus](menus/index.md)); on dialysis, 3 oz or more.
- [ ] Use egg whites when you need protein with little phosphorus.
- [ ] If you are losing weight or not hungry, tell your team: do not cut protein further
      ([Eating enough](eating-enough.md)).
- [ ] When you shrink a meat portion to fit, shrinking 1 oz of chicken saves about 9 g of protein
      and 65 mg of phosphorus.

## Examples

- **Stage 4, 70 kg, with diabetes:** about 56 g a day: an egg and toast at breakfast, a sandwich
  with 1½ oz of turkey at lunch, 2 oz of chicken with rice at dinner, and bread, rice and snacks
  for the rest. See the [stage 4–5 menu](menus/g4-g5.md).
- **Hemodialysis, 70 kg:** 70–84 g a day: add egg whites at breakfast and 3 oz or more of meat or
  fish at lunch and dinner. See the [hemodialysis menu](menus/hemodialysis.md).

## Ask your care team

1. What is my protein target in grams, and which weight did you use?
2. Should my protein change now that my kidney function has changed, or now that I am on dialysis?
3. Is plant protein (beans, tofu, nuts) OK for me, given potassium and phosphorus?
4. I am older or losing muscle: should my target be higher?

## Get help now if…

- You are losing weight without trying, or cannot eat for more than a day or two: call your team.
  See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
