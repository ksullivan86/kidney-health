# Eating well with kidney disease and type 1 diabetes

!!! note "This supports your care team; it never replaces it"
    Your nephrologist, diabetes team and renal dietitian set your real targets for potassium,
    phosphorus, sodium, protein, fluid and blood glucose from your own blood tests, urine output,
    medicines and insulin plan, and they change them as your labs change. KDIGO asks for renal
    dietitians to tailor these "to their individual needs" ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point
    3.3.2). **Nothing here changes an insulin dose.** A food marked "avoid" can be fine for someone
    whose potassium is normal; a food marked "eat" can be wrong for someone on a fluid limit.

## In short

Kidneys that are failing lose the ability to get rid of **potassium**, **phosphorus**, **sodium**,
the waste from **protein** and, late on, **water**. The kidney diet keeps those five in a range your
kidneys can still handle. The sixth number, **carbohydrate**, keeps doing what it always did in
type 1 diabetes: it decides the insulin dose.

- **Portions, not bans.** Most kidney-diet problems are size problems: ½ cup of fruit or
  vegetables, 3 oz of meat (a deck of cards), 1 oz of cheese. "A large serving of a low-potassium
  food becomes a high-potassium food" ([NKF][DG5]).
- **Fresh over packaged.** More than 70 % of sodium comes from packaged and restaurant food, not
  the salt shaker ([FDA][DG34]), and the phosphate *additives* in processed food are absorbed almost
  completely while natural phosphorus is not ([Kalantar-Zadeh 2010][DG12]; [Noori 2010][DG13a]).
- **Read the ingredient list for "PHOS" and "potassium chloride".** Phosphorus is not required on
  the Nutrition Facts label ([NKF][DG20]; [NKF label guide][DG38]).
- **A low blood glucose is always treated first.** Use glucose tablets or apple or cranberry juice
  when there is a choice, but if orange juice or cola is what is within reach, drink the measured
  4 oz now, recheck in 15 minutes and log it. One rescue portion is a budgeting note; an untreated
  low is an emergency ([ADA 2026 §5][A26-5]; [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]). See
  [Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md).
- **Star fruit is never allowed** at any stage of kidney disease ([de Oliveira 2015][DG39];
  [NKF][DG17]).
- **Protein goes *up*, not down, when dialysis starts** (to 1.0–1.2 g per kg), and fluid becomes
  limited ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2022][K22]).

## Your numbers

These are the app's starting targets ("Suggest targets" in your profile), worked out for an example

For a 70 kg adult with diabetes that means about **56 g of protein a day before dialysis**
(0.8 g/kg) and **70–84 g on dialysis**, with **1,750–2,450 kcal** of energy (25–35 kcal/kg)
([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2024][K24]; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]).

    2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2024][K24]). Potassium and phosphorus have **no guideline milligram
    number**: KDOQI says to adjust intake "to maintain serum potassium within the normal range"
    (6.4.1) and to keep serum phosphate normal (6.3.1). The figures below are the ranges renal

## Which numbers count per day, and which per week

Not every number is judged the same way, and the app follows exactly this split
([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)):

- **Potassium, sodium and fluid act within the day.** Blood potassium can rise within hours of a
  large load, and a low day does not "bank" against a high one: the goal is never to have a
  dangerous day ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).
- **On hemodialysis** those three build up until the next session, so they are also judged over
  the gap *between* sessions. The long weekend gap is the dangerous one: deaths on the day after it
  were 22.1 vs 18.0 per 100 patient-years, and hospital stays for heart-rhythm problems roughly
  doubled ([Foley 2011][DG53]). Weight gain between sessions above 3.5 % of body weight is linked
  to death ([Cabrera 2015][DG28a]). See [Dialysis days](dialysis-days.md).
- **Phosphorus and protein are judged on the average over a week or so.** Blood phosphate, PTH and
  nutrition reflect weeks of eating, not one meal; KDOQI itself assesses intake from food records
  over several days ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [Noori 2010][DG13a]). One high-phosphorus dinner is
  balanced by lower days around it.
- **Carbohydrate** is counted per meal and per day for insulin; its weekly average is just
  information.

## Does age or sex change my numbers?

Mostly not. Age and sex change how much **energy** (calories) you need, and they are part of the
**eGFR** calculation and the **anaemia** thresholds. They do **not** change protein per kg,
sodium, potassium or phosphorus: those depend on your kidney function, your blood tests and your
treatment ([design note 05][NOTE05]). Calcium needs differ by age and sex only when kidney function
is near normal. The app's "Suggest targets" shows which of your details it used
([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md); [Lab results](../labs/index.md)).

## Why protein goes up on dialysis

Before dialysis, extra protein makes the kidneys work harder, and more than 1.3 g per kg a day is
linked to faster loss of kidney function ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). Too *little* protein is also harmful:
it causes muscle loss and, with insulin, raises the risk of lows ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]). Dialysis
removes protein and the body's need rises, so the target goes up to 1.0–1.2 g per kg
([KDIGO 2022][K22]; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]). See [Protein](protein.md).

## Ask your care team

1. Which of my labs mean I need to limit potassium or phosphorus right now?
2. What protein target should I aim for, and which weight do you use for it?
3. Do I have a fluid limit? How much, and does it change on hot days or sick days?
4. Should my insulin-to-carb ratio change if I switch to more white bread and rice?
5. Can I see a renal dietitian, and how often?

## Get help now if…

- You have a low you cannot treat yourself, chest pain, severe breathlessness, or sudden severe
  weakness: call 911 (or your local emergency number). See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
