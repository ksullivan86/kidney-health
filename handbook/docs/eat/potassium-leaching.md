# Potassium leaching

## In short

Potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots, beets and winter squash are high in potassium. Cutting them
small and boiling them in plenty of water, then throwing the water away, removes about half of
their potassium. Soaking alone does almost nothing, and baking, roasting, microwaving or frying
removes none. A leached potato still has potassium, so it still counts toward your day.

## Your numbers

| | Potassium |
|---|---|
| Potato, boiled, not leached, ½ cup | 256 mg |
| Potato, leached or double-boiled, ½ cup | about 130 mg |
| Potato, baked with skin, 1 medium | 926 mg (baking removes none) |

([NKF][DG5]; [Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]; [USDA FoodData Central][FDC].)

## What to do: two methods

=== "Double-boil (simplest)"

    1. Peel the vegetable and cut it into small pieces (about ½ inch / 1 cm cubes) or shred it.
    2. Boil in plenty of unsalted water.
    3. Drain and throw the water away.
    4. Boil again in fresh unsalted water until soft (20–30 minutes in total).
    5. Drain again, then season or mash.

    Boiling 1-cm cubes removed about 50 % of the potassium and boiling shredded potato about
    75 %; soaking first added nothing beyond boiling ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6];
    [DaVita][DG32a]; [AKF: double-cook potatoes][DG49]).

=== "The NKF soak method"

    1. Peel and keep the vegetable in cold water so it does not darken.
    2. Slice it **⅛ inch** thick.
    3. Rinse in warm water.
    4. Soak for **at least 2 hours** in warm unsalted water, using **10 parts water to 1 part
       vegetable**. If you soak longer, change the water every 4 hours.
    5. Rinse again in warm water.
    6. Cook in **5 parts fresh unsalted water** to 1 part vegetable, and throw the water away.

    ([NKF][DG5].) KDIGO notes that soaking in previously boiled water "can effectively reduce
    the potassium by half for some foods" ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).

- [ ] Use leaching for potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots, beets and winter squash.
- [ ] Count a leached ½ cup of potato as about 130 mg of potassium.
- [ ] Keep leached vegetables to ½-cup portions if your potassium is limited.

## Examples

- Mashed potatoes made from double-boiled potatoes, with unsalted butter and a little milk
  alternative instead of milk.
- The [stage 3 sample menu](menus/g3.md) (Day 3) and the [peritoneal dialysis menu](menus/peritoneal.md)
  use ½ cup of leached potato; the numbers there are for *unleached* potato, so they are on the
  safe side.

## Ask your care team

1. Do I need to leach vegetables, or is my potassium fine?
2. How often can I have potatoes, and what portion?
3. Which other vegetables should I leach?

## Get help now if…

- You have muscle weakness, a slow or irregular heartbeat or fainting and your potassium has been
  high: see [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
