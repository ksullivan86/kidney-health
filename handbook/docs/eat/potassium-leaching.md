# Potassium leaching

## In short

"Leaching" means cooking a vegetable in water and throwing the water away, so some of its potassium goes
down the drain. Potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots, beets and winter squash are the usual targets. Cutting
them small and boiling them, then draining, removes about half of the potassium; shredding removes more.
Soaking raw pieces in cold water does almost nothing, and baking, roasting, microwaving or frying removes
none ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]). A leached potato still has potassium, so it still counts toward
your day. You only need this if your team has asked you to limit potassium.

## Your numbers

Potassium before leaching, per ½ cup unless stated ([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]):

| Vegetable | Potassium |
|---|---|
| Potato, boiled, peeled, not leached | 256 mg |
| Potato, leached or double-boiled | **about 130 mg** ([NKF][DG5]) |
| Potato, baked with skin, 1 medium | 926 mg (baking removes none) |
| Sweet potato, baked | 475 mg |
| Butternut squash, baked | 293 mg |
| Beets, boiled | 259 mg |
| Carrots, boiled | 183 mg |

What each method removes:

| Method | Potassium removed |
|---|---|
| Soaking raw pieces in cold water, no boiling | almost none ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]) |
| Boiling ½-inch (1 cm) cubes, then draining | about 50 % ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]) |
| Boiling shredded potato, then draining | about 75 % ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]) |
| Double-boiling thin slices, small dice or shreds | 50 % to up to 72 % ([DaVita][DG32a]) |
| Soaking first, then boiling | no more than boiling alone ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]) |
| A 5–10 minute soak in just-boiled water | 10–20 % for potatoes and other tubers; 30–49 % for leafy greens, other vegetables, grains, meat and fish ([de Abreu 2023][ABREU23]) |
| Baking, roasting, microwaving, frying | none |

Boiling also removed some phosphorus from the potatoes ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]).

## What to do: two methods

=== "Double-boil (simplest)"

    1. Peel the vegetable and cut it into small pieces (about ½ inch / 1 cm cubes), thin slices, or
       shred it.
    2. Boil in plenty of unsalted water.
    3. Drain and throw the water away.
    4. Boil again in fresh unsalted water until soft (about 30 minutes or less in total).
    5. Drain again, then season, mash or pan-fry.

    ([DaVita][DG32a]; [AKF: double-cook potatoes][DG49]; [Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6].)

=== "The NKF soak method"

    1. Peel and keep the vegetable in cold water so it does not darken.
    2. Slice it **⅛ inch** thick.
    3. Rinse in warm water.
    4. Soak for **at least 2 hours** in warm unsalted water, using **10 parts water to 1 part
       vegetable**. If you soak longer, change the water every 4 hours.
    5. Rinse again in warm water.
    6. Cook in **5 parts fresh unsalted water** to 1 part vegetable, and throw the water away.

    ([NKF][DG5].) Newer studies suggest the boiling, not the soaking, does most of the work
    ([Bethke and Jansky 2008][DG6]; [DaVita][DG32a]), so the double-boil is a fine shortcut.

**A short hot soak.** KDIGO notes that soaking foods for 5–10 minutes in water that has just boiled "can
effectively reduce the potassium by half for some foods" ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). In the study behind it,
that worked best for leafy greens, grains and meat, and least for potatoes and other tubers
([de Abreu 2023][ABREU23]). For potatoes, double-boil.

## Checklist

- [ ] Ask whether you need to leach at all. If your potassium is normal, you do not.
- [ ] Use leaching for potatoes, sweet potatoes, carrots, beets and winter squash.
- [ ] Cut small or shred: more surface means more potassium in the water.
- [ ] Always throw the cooking water away; never use it for soup, gravy or sauce.
- [ ] Count a leached ½ cup of potato as about 130 mg of potassium, and keep to ½-cup portions if your
      potassium is limited.
- [ ] Remember that fries, hash browns, baked potatoes and potato chips are not leached.
- [ ] Drain the liquid from canned vegetables too, and do not use it ([NKF][DG5]).

## Examples

- **Mashed potatoes:** double-boil ½-inch cubes, mash with unsalted butter and a little unsweetened
  almond milk instead of milk.
- **Home fries:** double-boil diced potato until just tender, drain well, then brown in a pan with oil,
  onion and paprika.
- **On the menus:** the [stage 3 sample menu](menus/g3.md) and the [peritoneal dialysis menu](menus/peritoneal.md)
  use leached potato. Their numbers are for *unleached* potato, so they are on the safe side.

!!! tip "In the app"
    The app's food list has boiled potato but no leached potato. Log it as "Potato, boiled, peeled": the
    number is about twice what is left after leaching, so you stay on the safe side. Or add your own
    custom food with about 130 mg potassium per ½ cup ([Logging food](../app/logging.md)).

## Ask your care team

1. Do I need to leach vegetables, or is my potassium fine?
2. How often can I have potatoes, and what portion?
3. Which other vegetables should I leach?
4. Can I count leached potato as a lower-potassium food in my plan?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Your potassium has been high and you have fainting, a very slow or irregular pulse, chest pain or
    sudden severe weakness, or a lab tells you your potassium is 6.5 mmol/L or more
    ([Blood potassium](../labs/blood-potassium.md); [Get help now](../get-help-now.md)).
