# Portions

## In short

Most kidney-diet problems are size problems. "A large serving of a low-potassium food becomes a
high-potassium food" ([NKF][DG5]), and the same is true for phosphorus, sodium and carbohydrate. Learn
a few standard portions, measure them for a week, and you will be able to judge them by eye at home
and when eating out.

## Your numbers: standard portions

| Food | Kidney-diet portion | Looks about like |
|---|---|---|
| Fruit or vegetables | ½ cup | half a baseball (1 cup is a fist or a baseball) |
| Cooked meat, poultry or fish | 3 oz (85 g) | a deck of cards or the palm of your hand; fish fillet: a checkbook |
| Cheese | 1 oz | a pair of dice |
| Cooked rice, pasta or cereal | ½ cup is about 22 g carbohydrate (white rice) | a rounded handful or a tennis ball |
| Peanut butter | 2 tbsp | a ping-pong ball |
| Nuts | ¼ cup | a golf ball |
| Juice for a low | 4 oz (½ cup) | not "a glass" |

Hand and object guides from [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-PORTION]; kidney portions from [NKF][DG5] and
[NKF dining out][DG48]. Hands differ in size: check your own hand against a measuring cup once.

**Portion and serving are not the same.** A *portion* is how much you choose to eat. A *serving* is
the amount on the Nutrition Facts label, and the two often do not match ([NIDDK][NIDDK-portions]). If
you eat two label servings, double every number, carbohydrate included ([NKF label guide][DG38]).

## What to do

- [ ] **Measure your dishes once.** Fill your usual bowl, mug and glass with water from a measuring cup
      and write down what each holds. Many bowls hold more than one label serving.
- [ ] **Measure for one week.** Use a measuring cup for starches, fruit and vegetables, and a kitchen
      scale for meat if you have one. After that, check yourself by eye and measure again now and then.
- [ ] **Serve on a smaller plate** and put the extra away before you sit down.
- [ ] **Use your hand away from home.** Meat the size of your palm, starch the size of your fist or
      less, and ask for a box at the start of a restaurant meal ([Eating out](eating-out.md)).
- [ ] **Log what you really ate.** In the app, pick the food, then change the number of servings or
      type the grams. The warnings and the meal's carbohydrate total change with the amount, before
      you save ([app contract][ARCH]; [Logging food](../app/logging.md)).

## Examples: when the portion changes the answer

| Food | Small portion | Bigger portion |
|---|---|---|
| Cantaloupe | ½ cup: about 210 mg potassium | 1 cup: about 430 mg |
| Tomato | 2 thin slices: about 95 mg potassium | 1 whole tomato: 292 mg |
| Yogurt, plain low-fat | ¼ cup is the portion | 6-oz pot: 398 mg potassium, 245 mg phosphorus |
| Peanut butter | 1 tbsp: about 90 mg potassium | 2 tbsp: about 180 mg potassium, 107 mg phosphorus |
| Bagel | ½ bagel is the portion | whole bagel: about 420 mg sodium, 52 g carbohydrate |
| White rice | ½ cup: about 22 g carbohydrate | restaurant plate (2 cups): about 89 g |
| Restaurant protein | 3 oz kidney portion | 4–8 oz restaurant serving, 2–3 times as much ([NKF][DG48]) |

USDA FoodData Central values through the app's food list ([FDC][FDC]).

**A worked example.** The label on a box of cereal says 1 cup is 25 g of carbohydrate and 30 mg
sodium. Your bowl holds 2 cups when it is full. A full bowl is 50 g of carbohydrate and 60 mg sodium,
plus the milk. If you measured 1 cup into the same bowl, it would look half empty, which is why the
first week of measuring matters.

## Ask your care team

1. Which portions matter most for me: fruit, vegetables, meat or starch?
2. How big should my meat portion be at lunch and dinner?
3. Can you show me my portions on a plate at my next dietitian visit?
4. How much carbohydrate should my usual bowl of cereal or rice have?

## Get help now if…

- Portions do not cause emergencies. If a large meal is followed by a low or a very high glucose with
  ketones, follow your plan; see [Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md),
  [Sick days](../t1d/sick-days.md) and [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
