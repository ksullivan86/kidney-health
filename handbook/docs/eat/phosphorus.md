# Phosphorus

## In short

Phosphorus builds bone together with calcium. Failing kidneys cannot clear it, so it starts to build up
from about stage 3–4, and dialysis removes only part of it ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [K/DOQI 2003][DG10]).
High blood phosphate pulls calcium out of bone and leads to calcium deposits in "blood vessels, lungs,
eyes, and heart" ([NKF][DG20]). The most useful thing you can do is avoid **phosphate additives** in
processed food, because the body absorbs almost all of them. Phosphorus is judged on your average over
a week, not one meal.

## Your numbers

=== "US units"

    | | Typical |
    |---|---|
    | Blood phosphate (phosphorus) | 2.5–4.5 mg/dL; your lab's range wins |
    | A food limit is usually started when blood phosphate is above | 4.6 mg/dL at stages 3–4; 5.5 mg/dL at stage 5 and on dialysis |
    | Food, when a limit is ordered | 800–1,000 mg a day, "adjusted for protein needs" on dialysis |

=== "International"

    | | Typical |
    |---|---|
    | Blood phosphate | 0.81–1.45 mmol/L; your lab's range wins |
    | A food limit is usually started when blood phosphate is above | 1.49 mmol/L at stages 3–4; 1.78 mmol/L at stage 5 and on dialysis |
    | Food, when a limit is ordered | 800–1,000 mg a day, "adjusted for protein needs" on dialysis |

([NKF][DG20]; [K/DOQI 2003][DG10], guideline 4.1.) The app's starting ceiling is 1,000 mg a day
(900 mg at stage 5 before dialysis). KDOQI 2020 asks your team to adjust phosphorus to keep your blood
level normal and to think about *where* it comes from, not only how much ([KDOQI 2020][Q20],
statements 6.3.1–6.3.2). See [Phosphate, calcium and PTH](../labs/phosphate-calcium-pth.md).

**Why it matters.** On hemodialysis, people who ate the most phosphorus had a higher risk of death than
those who ate the least (hazard ratio 2.37, highest vs lowest third) ([Noori 2010][DG14a]).

## Natural phosphorus and additives

| Where it comes from | How much your body absorbs |
|---|---|
| Plants (whole grains, beans, nuts) | about 20–50 % (bound as phytate) |
| Meat, fish, eggs, dairy | about 40–60 % |
| **Phosphate additives** | **about 90–100 %** |

([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [Kalantar-Zadeh 2010][DG12]; [Noori 2010][DG13a]; [St-Jules 2017][DG15].)

Additives are everywhere. In a study of 2,394 best-selling grocery items, **44 %** had a phosphate
additive, including most frozen meals, dry mixes and packaged meats. Meals made mostly from additive
foods had **736 mg more phosphorus a day** than the same meals made without them, close to a whole
day's limit ([León 2013][LEON13]). "Enhanced" fresh meat and poultry carried on average 28 % more
phosphate for the same protein ([Sherman and Mehta 2009][SHERMAN09]).

Phosphorus does not have to be listed on the US Nutrition Facts label, so the ingredient list is your
main tool ([NKF label guide][DG38]). Every food database under-counts additive phosphorus, so the app
treats any food flagged with a phosphate additive as "high" whatever its number. See
[Phosphate additives](phosphate-additives.md) for the ingredient names.

**Per serving** ([AKF phosphorus guide][DG46b]): low 100 mg or less, medium 101–199 mg, high 200 mg or
more, and *any* "phos" ingredient counts as high. The app marks a food "medium" at 101–150 mg and
"high" above 150 mg.

**Phosphorus-to-protein ratio.** If a label lists phosphorus, under 10–12 mg of phosphorus per gram of
protein is an efficient protein: egg white is under 2 mg/g and chicken breast about 7. Processed cheese,
nuts, beans and colas are not ([Noori 2010][DG13a]; [Noori 2010][DG14a]).

## Binders

Phosphate binders are tablets, chews, powders or liquids that hold on to phosphorus in your gut so it
leaves in your stool. They "must be taken every time you eat, including meals and snacks", the way your
team prescribes them ([NKF][DG20]; [KDIGO 2017][K17]). Binders work on the food in your gut, so they only
help when taken with food. Calcium-based binders count toward your calcium total ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 4.1.6).

## What to do

- [ ] Read ingredient lists and skip anything with "phos" in it ([Reading food labels](label-reading.md)).
- [ ] Choose fresh meat and poultry that is not "enhanced" or "injected" with a solution.
- [ ] Swap dark colas and pepper sodas for clear sodas or water.
- [ ] Cook from plain ingredients instead of boxed mixes, frozen meals and processed cheese.
- [ ] Keep dairy to the portions your dietitian suggests: it is one of the richest natural sources.
- [ ] If you take binders, keep some in your bag, car or desk so a meal out is never without them.
- [ ] Look at your phosphorus over the week in the app, not one meal.
- [ ] On dialysis, do not cut protein to save phosphorus: pick efficient proteins (egg white, chicken,
      fish) instead ([Protein](protein.md)).

## Examples

| Food | Serving | Phosphorus |
|---|---|---|
| Egg white | 2 large | 10 mg |
| Chicken breast, roasted | 3 oz | 196 mg |
| White rice, cooked | ½ cup | 34 mg |
| Brown rice, cooked | ½ cup | 104 mg (plant phosphorus, less absorbed) |
| Milk, 2 % fat | 1 cup | 224 mg |
| Yogurt, plain, low fat | 6 oz | 245 mg |
| Cheddar cheese | 1 oz | 127 mg |
| Cream cheese | 1 tbsp | 16 mg |
| American cheese, processed (additive) | 1 oz | 179 mg |
| Cola, regular or diet (phosphoric acid) | 12 oz | 33–36 mg; 27–68 mg when measured ([lab study][DG25b]) |

From the app's USDA-based food list ([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]); see [Food lists](food-lists.md).

**Swaps that cut additive phosphorus** ([FDC][FDC]):

| Instead of | Phosphorus | Try | Phosphorus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boxed macaroni and cheese, 1 cup (additive) | 222 mg | Pasta, 1 cup, with 1 tbsp butter and 1 tbsp grated Parmesan | 106 mg |
| Deli turkey, 2 oz (additive) | 142 mg | Turkey breast you roast yourself, 2 oz | 131 mg, none of it additive |
| Chocolate pudding cup, 4 oz (additive) | 60 mg | Orange sherbet, ½ cup | 30 mg |
| Cola, 12 oz | 33 mg | Lemon-lime soda, 12 oz | 0 mg |

The milligrams look close for the turkey, but additive phosphorus is absorbed almost fully and natural
phosphorus is not, so the swap matters more than the numbers suggest ([Kalantar-Zadeh 2010][DG12]).

!!! tip "In the app"
    The app judges phosphorus and protein on your **weekly average**, so one high-phosphorus dinner is
    balanced by the days around it. Any food flagged with a phosphate additive shows a "high" warning
    ([architecture contract][ARCH]). See [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md).

## Ask your care team

1. What was my last phosphate result, and do I need a limit?
2. Do I need a phosphate binder? With which meals and snacks, and what if I skip a meal?
3. Are whole grains and beans OK for me, given that their phosphorus is less absorbed?
4. How much milk, yogurt and cheese fits my plan?
5. How often will you check phosphate, calcium and PTH?

## Get help now if…

!!! warning "Call your care team"
    High phosphate rarely causes sudden symptoms; the harm builds over months. Call your team if you
    cannot take your binders as prescribed (they upset your stomach, cost too much or run out), or if
    your phosphate stays high. For urgent symptoms, see [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
