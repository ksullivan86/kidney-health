# Phosphorus

## In short

Phosphorus builds bone together with calcium. Failing kidneys cannot clear it, so it starts to
build up from about stage 3–4, and dialysis removes only part of it ([KDOQI 2020][Q20];
[K/DOQI 2003][DG10]). High blood phosphate pulls calcium out of bone and deposits it in "blood
vessels, lungs, eyes, and heart" ([NKF][DG20]). The most useful thing you can do is avoid
**phosphate additives** in processed food, because the body absorbs almost all of them. Phosphorus
is judged on your average over a week, not one meal.

    | Food, when a limit is ordered | 800–1,000 mg a day, "adjusted for protein needs" on dialysis |

=== "International"

    | | Typical |
    |---|---|
    | Blood phosphate | 0.81–1.45 mmol/L; your lab's range wins |
    | Food, when a limit is ordered | 800–1,000 mg a day, "adjusted for protein needs" on dialysis |

([NKF][DG20]; [K/DOQI 2003][DG10].) A limit is usually started when blood phosphate is above
4.6 mg/dL at stages 3–4 or above 5.5 mg/dL at stage 5 ([K/DOQI 2003][DG10]). The app's starting
ceiling is 1,000 mg (900 mg at stage 5 before dialysis).

**Why it matters.** On hemodialysis, people who ate the most phosphorus had a higher risk of death
than those who ate the least (hazard ratio 2.37, highest vs lowest third) ([Noori 2010][DG14a]).

## Natural phosphorus and additives

| Where it comes from | How much your body absorbs |
|---|---|
| Plants (whole grains, beans, nuts) | about 20–50 % (bound as phytate) |
| Meat, fish, eggs, dairy | about 40–60 % |
| **Phosphate additives** | **about 90–100 %** |

([KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [Kalantar-Zadeh 2010][DG12]; [Noori 2010][DG13a]; [St-Jules 2017][DG15].)
Every food database under-counts additive phosphorus, so the app treats any food flagged with a
phosphate additive as "high" whatever its number ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).
See [Phosphate additives](phosphate-additives.md) for the ingredient names.

**Per serving** ([AKF phosphorus guide][DG46b]): low 100 mg or less, medium 101–199 mg, high
200 mg or more, and *any* "phos" ingredient counts as high. The app marks a food "medium" at
101–150 mg and "high" above 150 mg.

**Phosphorus-to-protein ratio.** If a label lists phosphorus, under 10–12 mg of phosphorus per
gram of protein is an efficient protein: egg white is under 2 mg/g and chicken breast about 7.
Processed cheese, nuts, beans and colas are not ([Noori 2010][DG13a]; [Noori 2010][DG14a]).

## Binders

Phosphate binders are tablets that hold on to phosphorus in your gut so it leaves in your stool.
They work **only when taken with meals and snacks**, the way your team prescribes them
([NKF][DG20]; [KDIGO 2017][K17]). Calcium-based binders count toward your calcium total.

## What to do

- [ ] Read ingredient lists and skip anything with "phos" in it ([Reading labels](label-reading.md)).
- [ ] Choose fresh meat and poultry that is not "enhanced" or "injected" with a solution.
- [ ] Swap dark colas for clear sodas or water.
- [ ] If you take binders, take them with the meals and snacks your team told you to.
- [ ] Look at your phosphorus over the week in the app, not one meal.

## Examples

| Food | Serving | Phosphorus |
|---|---|---|
| Egg white | 2 large | 10 mg |
| Chicken breast, roasted | 3 oz | 194 mg |
| White rice, cooked | ½ cup | 34 mg |
| Brown rice, cooked | ½ cup | 104 mg (plant phosphorus, less absorbed) |
| Milk, 1 % fat | 1 cup | 232 mg |
| American cheese, processed (additive) | 1 oz | 182–242 mg |
| Cola, regular or diet (phosphoric acid) | 12 oz | 33–36 mg, measured 27–68 mg |

From the diet guide's USDA-based lists ([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]); see [Food lists](food-lists.md).

## Ask your care team

1. What was my last phosphate result, and do I need a limit?
2. Do I need a phosphate binder? With which meals and snacks?
3. Are whole grains and beans OK for me, given that their phosphorus is less absorbed?
4. How often will you check phosphate, calcium and PTH?

## Get help now if…

- High phosphate rarely causes sudden symptoms. Very low calcium can cause muscle cramps or
  tingling around the mouth: call your team. See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
