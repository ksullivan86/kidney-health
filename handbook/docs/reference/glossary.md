# Glossary

Plain meanings of the words you will hear from your care team and read in this handbook. We use the
words KDIGO agreed on in 2020: "kidney" rather than "renal", and "kidney failure" rather than
"end-stage" ([KDIGO nomenclature][K20NOM]). Abbreviations show a short meaning when you hover over or
tap them anywhere on the site. Lab units are explained on [Units and conversions](units.md).

## A

A1c (HbA1c, A1C)
:   A blood test that shows your average glucose over the last 2–3 months. It becomes less reliable at
    stage 4–5 and on dialysis ([KDIGO 2022][K22], practice point 2.1.2). See [A1c](../labs/a1c.md).

ACE inhibitor (ACEi)
:   A blood pressure medicine that also lowers the albumin leak and protects the kidneys
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 3.6.3).

Acute kidney injury (AKI)
:   A sudden drop in kidney function over hours or days, for example from dehydration, an infection or
    some medicines. It is different from CKD, which lasts at least 3 months ([KDIGO nomenclature][K20NOM]).

AGP (ambulatory glucose profile)
:   The standard one-page CGM report: 14 days of readings drawn as one typical day, with time in
    ranges and the statistics ([time in range consensus][TIR19]). See [CGM metrics](../labs/cgm-metrics.md).

Albumin
:   The main protein in blood. In the urine it is an early sign of kidney damage. In the blood, a low
    level can mean poor nutrition or inflammation. See [UACR](../labs/uacr.md) and [Albumin](../labs/albumin.md).

Albuminuria
:   Albumin in the urine, measured as UACR. Grouped as A1, A2 or A3 ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 3).

Anemia
:   Too few red blood cells, which causes tiredness and breathlessness. In CKD it means hemoglobin under
    13 g/dL (130 g/L) in men or under 12 g/dL (120 g/L) in women ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]).

ARB (angiotensin receptor blocker)
:   A blood pressure medicine that protects the kidneys in the same way as an ACE inhibitor
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 3.6.3).

AV fistula
:   A join made by a surgeon between an artery and a vein, usually in the arm, so that it can be used
    for hemodialysis. You can feel a buzz (the **thrill**) over it ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]).

AV graft
:   A soft tube placed under the skin to join an artery and a vein, used for hemodialysis when a
    fistula is not possible ([NIDDK][NIDDK-HD]).

## B

Basal insulin
:   Long-acting or background insulin that covers the body's needs between meals and overnight.

Bicarbonate
:   A blood test of the body's acid balance. Kidneys remove acid, so bicarbonate can fall in CKD
    ([NIDDK][NIDDK-kidneys]). Lab reports may call it CO2, total CO2 (TCO2) or HCO3.
    See [Bicarbonate](../labs/bicarbonate.md).

Binder (phosphate binder)
:   A medicine taken with meals that holds on to phosphorus in food so less of it is absorbed.

Blood urea nitrogen (BUN)
:   Urea nitrogen forms when protein breaks down. BUN can be high in kidney failure, but also with
    dehydration, bleeding in the gut or a lot of protein, so teams rely on eGFR to judge filtering
    ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-BUN]).

BMI (body mass index)
:   Weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. The app uses it to choose the weight that
    protein and energy goals are based on. See [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md).

Bolus insulin
:   Rapid-acting insulin taken for a meal or to correct a high glucose.

## C

CGM (continuous glucose monitor)
:   A small sensor that reads glucose every few minutes and shows trends on a phone or reader.
    Some CGMs connect to a pump that adjusts insulin automatically (automated insulin delivery, AID).

Chronic kidney disease (CKD)
:   Kidney damage, or an eGFR under 60, that lasts at least 3 months ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 1).
    See [What CKD is](../ckd/index.md).

CKD-MBD
:   CKD–mineral and bone disorder: changes in calcium, phosphate, PTH and vitamin D that affect the
    bones and blood vessels ([KDIGO 2017][K17]).

Coefficient of variation (CV)
:   On a CGM report, how much your glucose swings. 36 % or less is the usual goal
    ([time in range consensus][TIR19]).

Conservative kidney management
:   Care for kidney failure that focuses on symptoms and quality of life, without dialysis or a
    transplant ([NIDDK][NIDDK-conservative]).

Creatinine
:   A waste product from the normal breakdown of muscle. The blood level is used to work out eGFR
    ([NIDDK][NIDDK-tests]).

Cystatin C
:   Another blood marker of filtering. Combined with creatinine it gives a more accurate eGFR
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 1.1.2.1).

## D

Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
:   A dangerous build-up of ketones that makes the blood acidic. It is defined by ketones of
    3.0 mmol/L or more with acid in the blood, in someone with diabetes, whatever their glucose
    ([2024 consensus][HC24]). See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

Dialysate
:   The cleaning fluid used in dialysis.

Dialysis
:   A treatment that does some of the kidneys' work by removing waste and extra fluid. See
    **hemodialysis** and **peritoneal dialysis**.

Dry weight
:   Your weight without extra fluid, set by your dialysis team. Weight gained above it between
    sessions is mostly fluid ([NIDDK][NIDDK-HD]). See [Fluid](../eat/fluid.md).

## E

ECG (electrocardiogram)
:   A painless tracing of the heart's electrical activity. High potassium can change it.

eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate)
:   How well your kidneys filter, worked out from a creatinine blood test plus age and sex
    ([CKD-EPI 2021 equation][NKF-CKDEPI]). Measured in mL/min/1.73 m². Grouped as G1 to G5
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 2). See [CKD stages](../ckd/stages.md).

Electrolytes
:   Minerals in the blood such as sodium, potassium and bicarbonate. Kidneys keep them in balance
    ([NIDDK][NIDDK-kidneys]).

End-stage renal disease (ESRD)
:   An older name for kidney failure. Medicare still uses it as the legal name for kidney failure
    treated with dialysis or a transplant ([Medicare][MED-ESRD]). This handbook says "kidney failure"
    ([KDIGO nomenclature][K20NOM]).

ESA (erythropoiesis-stimulating agent)
:   An injection that helps the body make red blood cells, used for anemia in CKD
    ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]). ESAs copy erythropoietin (EPO), the hormone healthy kidneys make.

Exit site
:   The place where a dialysis catheter comes out through the skin. Redness, pus or pain there can mean
    infection ([NIDDK][NIDDK-PD]).

## F – G

Ferritin and TSAT (transferrin saturation)
:   Blood tests of iron stores. Low results mean the body may not have enough iron to make red blood
    cells ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]). See [Hemoglobin and iron](../labs/haemoglobin-and-iron.md).

Finerenone (Kerendia)
:   A kidney-protecting medicine (a non-steroidal MRA). In 2026 it was FDA-approved to lower UACR in
    adults with CKD and type 1 diabetes ([Kerendia label][KER]).

G category
:   The filtering part of your CKD stage, from G1 (eGFR 90 or more) to G5 (under 15)
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 2).

GDO, GDH-PQQ and GDH-FAD
:   Names of chemistries used in some glucose meters and test strips. On icodextrin peritoneal dialysis
    fluid, GDH-PQQ and GDO strips and some GDH-FAD strips can read **falsely high** ([MHRA][MHRA]).
    See [Peritoneal dialysis](../stages/peritoneal-dialysis.md).

GFR (glomerular filtration rate)
:   How much fluid the kidneys' filters clear from the blood each minute. eGFR is the estimate from a
    blood test ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 2).

Glomerulus (plural glomeruli)
:   One of the tiny filters in the kidney.

Glucagon
:   A rescue medicine for a severe low, given by injection or nasal spray by another person
    ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).

GMI (glucose management indicator)
:   An A1c-like number worked out from CGM data ([KDIGO 2022][K22], practice point 2.1.3).

## H

Hemodialysis (HD)
:   Dialysis in which blood is cleaned by a machine and filter outside the body, usually 3 times a week
    in a center, or more often at home (home hemodialysis, HHD) ([NIDDK][NIDDK-HD]).

Hemoglobin
:   The protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Low hemoglobin is anemia ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]).

Hyperglycemia
:   High blood glucose.

Hyperkalemia
:   High potassium in the blood. KDIGO calls 6.0–6.4 mmol/L moderate and 6.5 mmol/L or more severe
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28). See [Blood potassium](../labs/blood-potassium.md).

Hypoglycemia (a low)
:   Low blood glucose: level 1 is under 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L), level 2 is under 54 mg/dL
    (3.0 mmol/L), and level 3 is any low where you need someone else's help ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).
    See [Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md).

## I – K

Icodextrin (Extraneal)
:   A peritoneal dialysis fluid that can make some glucose meters read **falsely high**
    ([MHRA][MHRA]).

Immunosuppressants (anti-rejection medicines)
:   Medicines taken every day after a transplant so the immune system does not attack the new kidney
    ([NIDDK][NIDDK-Tx]).

Interdialytic weight gain (IDWG)
:   The weight you gain between hemodialysis sessions, mostly from fluid.

IV (intravenous)
:   Into a vein, as in an IV drip or IV line. Before dialysis, ask staff to keep IVs and blood draws
    out of the arm your team wants to save for a fistula ([KDOQI][Q19VA], statement 6.10).

Ketones
:   Acids made when the body burns fat because it lacks insulin. A blood ketone meter reads them in
    mmol/L. High ketones can lead to DKA ([2024 consensus][HC24]).

Kidney failure
:   An eGFR under 15 (G5), or needing dialysis or a transplant. The KDIGO word for what used to be
    called end-stage kidney or renal disease ([KDIGO nomenclature][K20NOM]; [KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 2).

Kidney Failure Risk Equation (KFRE)
:   A tested calculation that uses age, sex, eGFR and UACR to estimate your chance of kidney failure in
    2 and 5 years ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 2.2.1).

Kidney replacement therapy (KRT)
:   Dialysis or a kidney transplant. Older name: renal replacement therapy ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).

## M – N

MRA (mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist)
:   A group of medicines that block a hormone that harms the kidneys and heart. Finerenone is one
    ([Kerendia label][KER]).

Nephrologist
:   A doctor who specializes in the kidneys.

Nephrotic syndrome
:   Very heavy protein loss in the urine, usually a UACR above about 2,200 mg/g (220 mg/mmol), with
    swelling ([KDIGO 2022][K22], albuminuria categories).

NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs; one is an NSAID)
:   Pain medicines such as ibuprofen and naproxen. They can harm the kidneys; see
    [Medicines to avoid](../medicines/avoid.md).

## P

Peritoneal dialysis (PD)
:   Dialysis inside your body: fluid goes into the belly through a catheter, collects waste and
    extra fluid through the lining of the belly (the peritoneum), and is drained out. Done by hand
    in the day (CAPD) or by a machine at night (APD) ([NIDDK][NIDDK-PD]).

Peritonitis
:   Infection of the belly lining, a risk on PD. Cloudy fluid, belly pain and fever are warning signs
    ([NIDDK][NIDDK-PD]).

Phosphorus (phosphate)
:   A mineral in food. When kidneys fail it can build up in the blood and weaken bones
    ([KDIGO 2017][K17]). On ingredient lists, "PHOS" in a name means a phosphate additive.
    See [Phosphorus](../eat/phosphorus.md).

PICC line (peripherally inserted central catheter)
:   A long IV line put in through a vein in the arm. KDOQI advises avoiding them when you may need a
    fistula later ([KDOQI][Q19VA], statement 6.10). See [Dialysis access](../prepare/dialysis-access.md).

POLST (also MOLST, POST or MOST)
:   A portable medical order, signed by a health care professional, that turns your wishes about
    treatment into orders that travel with you ([National POLST][POLST]). See
    [Advance care planning](../prepare/advance-care-planning.md).

Practice point (PP)
:   Expert advice in a KDIGO guideline. Practice points are not graded: KDIGO uses them where no
    formal evidence review was done or the evidence was too thin for a graded recommendation
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).

Pre-emptive transplant
:   A kidney transplant done before dialysis is needed ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.4.3).

PTH (parathyroid hormone)
:   A hormone that controls calcium and phosphate; it often rises in CKD ([KDIGO 2017][K17]).

## R – S

RAAS (renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system)
:   Hormones that raise blood pressure and hold on to salt. ACE inhibitors and ARBs block part of it
    (the renin–angiotensin system, RAS), and MRAs block aldosterone ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).

REMS (Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy)
:   An FDA safety program for some medicines. The mycophenolate REMS ended in 2026, but the label's
    pregnancy warnings still apply ([FDA][FDA-MYCO26]; [Mycophenolate REMS][REMS]).

Renal
:   An older word for "kidney" ([KDIGO nomenclature][K20NOM]).

Renal dietitian
:   A dietitian who specializes in kidney disease ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.2).

Retinopathy
:   Diabetic damage to the back of the eye. Having it, or not, helps show whether kidney disease comes
    from diabetes. See [Type 1 diabetes and your kidneys](../ckd/diabetes-and-your-kidneys.md).

SGLT2 inhibitor (SGLT2i)
:   A medicine approved for type 2 diabetes and, for some of them, for kidney disease or heart
    failure. It is not approved for type 1 diabetes in the US: its safety there has not been
    established, and DKA is a concern ([KDIGO 2022][K22]; [Farxiga label][FARXIGA]).

Sick-day plan
:   Your team's written plan for when you are ill: how often to check glucose and ketones, which
    medicines to pause and when to restart them ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 4.3.2).

## T – Z

T1D, T2D
:   Type 1 diabetes and type 2 diabetes.

Thrill
:   The buzz you feel over a working fistula or graft. If it stops or changes, call your team right
    away ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]).

Time in range (TIR)
:   The share of the day your CGM reads 70–180 mg/dL (3.9–10.0 mmol/L)
    ([time in range consensus][TIR19]). See [CGM metrics](../labs/cgm-metrics.md).

Transplant
:   An operation to place a healthy kidney from a donor. A pancreas can be transplanted at the same
    time (simultaneous pancreas–kidney, SPK) for some people with type 1 diabetes ([NIDDK][NIDDK-Tx];
    [OPTN][OPTN]).

UACR (urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio)
:   A urine test for albumin. 30 mg/g (3 mg/mmol) or more is a sign of kidney damage
    ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 1). See [UACR](../labs/uacr.md).

Uremia
:   Symptoms from waste building up when the kidneys fail, such as poor appetite, nausea, itching and
    tiredness ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.2.2.1 and Tables 38 and 41).

Vascular access
:   The way blood gets to the hemodialysis machine: a fistula, a graft or a catheter
    ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]). See [Dialysis access](../prepare/dialysis-access.md).
