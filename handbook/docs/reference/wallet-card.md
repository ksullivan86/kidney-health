# Wallet cards

## In short

These four cards fit in a wallet. The first says when to get help. The second tells paramedics and
hospital staff what they need to know if you cannot speak for yourself. The third and fourth hold
**your own** numbers and sick-day plan, written in by you and your care team. Paper still works when
a phone is flat, locked or lost.

## How to print and use them

1. Print this page (Ctrl+P, or Share → Print on a phone). Each card prints at credit-card width,
   85.6 mm (3.4 in).
2. Cut along the border. Fold or tape two cards back to back, and cover them with clear tape to keep
   them dry.
3. Fill in the blanks in pen **with your care team**, not from this handbook. Leave a line blank rather
   than guess.
4. Keep one card with your glucose tablets and one in your wallet. Give a copy to the people you live
   with.
5. Wear a medical ID bracelet or necklace as well: it tells others you have diabetes and need care
   right away ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]). Many phones can also show medical information on the lock screen.
6. Update the cards after any change to your stage, your treatment or your medicines.

## Card 2: for paramedics and hospital staff

<div class="kh-wallet" markdown>

**I have type 1 diabetes and chronic kidney disease**

Name: ________________ Date of birth: ____________

**If I am confused, sweaty, drowsy or acting strangely, I may have low blood glucose.** Check my
glucose. If I can swallow, give me sugar. If I cannot, give glucagon, turn me on my side and call 911.

Kidney: ☐ CKD stage ____ ☐ hemodialysis ☐ peritoneal dialysis ☐ kidney transplant (year ____)

☐ Fistula or graft in my ______ arm: **no blood pressure cuff, blood draw or IV in this arm**

☐ I use **icodextrin (Extraneal)** PD fluid: some glucose meters read **falsely high**. Use a
glucose-specific method or a lab test.

☐ I take **anti-rejection medicines**: do not stop them.

☐ Insulin pump ☐ CGM ☐ Glucagon in my bag

Allergies: ________________

Emergency contact: ________________ Phone: ____________

Kidney team: ____________ Diabetes team: ____________

</div>

Why each line is there:

- **Lows:** a severe low needs glucagon and a 911 call; the person should be turned on their side,
  because they may vomit as they wake, and fed only when they can swallow ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo];
  [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]; [glucagon instructions][GLUCAGON-KIT]).
- **Access arm:** blood pressure cuffs, blood draws and IVs can damage a fistula or graft
  ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]).
- **Icodextrin:** this PD fluid makes some glucose meters read falsely high, and insulin given for a
  false high reading has caused deaths. The effect can last up to two weeks after stopping the fluid
  ([MHRA][MHRA]).
- **Transplant:** without anti-rejection medicine, your immune system may attack the new kidney
  ([NIDDK][NIDDK-Tx]).

## Card 3: my numbers

Your team writes these in. They are **your** limits, not the typical starting points in this handbook.

<div class="kh-wallet" markdown>

**My numbers** (from my care team, date ________)

Potassium: under ______ mg a day · Sodium: under ______ mg a day

Phosphorus: under ______ mg a day · Protein: about ______ g a day

Fluid: ______ a day (counting soups, ice and gelatin) · Carbohydrate: ______ g per meal

Dry or target weight: ______ · Call if I gain more than ______ in ______

Blood pressure goal: under ______ / ______

Latest labs (date ______): eGFR ______ UACR ______ potassium ______

My insulin plan is written by my diabetes team. I do not change it from this card.

</div>

The app can hold the same numbers and warn you when a day goes over them
([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## Card 4: my sick-day plan

KDIGO asks that any medicine paused during an illness comes with a clear plan for when to restart it,
written down, because forgetting to restart is a common harm ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points
4.3.2–4.3.3). Ask your team to fill this card in.

<div class="kh-wallet" markdown>

**My sick-day plan** (filled in by my care team, date ________)

When I am vomiting, have diarrhea or cannot eat or drink:

Check glucose every ______ hours and ketones every ______ hours.

Medicines to pause: ______________________________

Restart them when: ______________________________

Keep taking insulin, even if I am not eating. Call ______________ if ketones are 1.6 mmol/L or more.

Extra fluid allowed while ill: ______

</div>

Why "keep taking insulin": stopping or skipping insulin while ill can lead to DKA, so the NHS advises
"do not stop taking insulin, even if you're not eating" ([NHS][NHS-DKA]; [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]). Your
team tells you how to adjust it. More on illness: [Sick days](../t1d/sick-days.md).

## Ask your care team

1. Will you fill in my numbers card and my sick-day card at this visit?
2. Which arm, if any, should never be used for blood pressure or blood tests?
3. If I use icodextrin, which glucose meters are safe with it?
4. Who should I call out of hours, and what number goes on the card?
