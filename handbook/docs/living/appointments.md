# Getting the most from appointments

## In short

Visits are short, and you see several teams: kidney, diabetes, dietitian, and perhaps dialysis or
transplant. **The more you plan for a visit, the more you get from it** ([NIDDK][NIDDK-managing]).
Bring your numbers, your medicine list and your top three questions. Bring a family member or friend
if you can: KDIGO says education works best when care partners are included ([KDIGO 2024][K24],
practice point 5.3.2). Before you leave, **say the plan back in your own words** (teach-back) to check
you have it right ([AHRQ][AHRQ-TB]). Big choices, such as which treatment to have, should be made
**with** you, based on what matters to you ([KDIGO 2024][K24], section 5.5).

## What to bring

| Bring | Why | Where to get it |
|---|---|---|
| Your **medicine list**, or all your medicine bottles, including supplements and over-the-counter products | Doses often depend on kidney function; supplements can be harmful | [NIDDK][NIDDK-managing]; [Your medicine list](../medicines/your-medicine-list.md) |
| Your **lab results** over time | Trends matter more than one number | [NIDDK][NIDDK-managing]; [Lab results](../labs/index.md) |
| Home **blood pressure** readings and **weights** (on dialysis: weights before and after sessions) | Show how blood pressure and fluid behave between visits | Your notebook or home monitor |
| Your **CGM report** (AGP) | 14 days of data with the sensor worn at least 70 % of the time gives a reliable picture | [Time-in-range consensus][TIR19]; [CGM reports](../labs/cgm-metrics.md) |
| The app's **period summary** or **CSV export** | Shows your average potassium, phosphorus, sodium, protein, carbohydrate and fluid against your targets, for a dietitian | [Reports for your care team](../app/reports-for-your-team.md) |
| Your **sick-day plan** and when you last used it | So the team can check and update it | [Sick days](../t1d/sick-days.md) |
| **Three questions**, written down | So the most important things get answered | [AHRQ][AHRQ-Q]; [Questions to ask](../reference/questions-to-ask.md) |

## During the visit

- **Start with your top question**, before time runs out.
- **Write the answers down**, or ask your companion to.
- **Teach-back:** say the plan in your own words, for example: "So I keep taking my ACE inhibitor,
  have my potassium checked in two weeks, and call if I am sick." This is the surest way to check you
  understood ([AHRQ][AHRQ-TB]).
- **Ask for anything important in writing**, especially medicine changes and sick-day rules.
- **Make decisions together.** Ask: "What are my options? What are the good and bad points of each?
  What would happen if I waited?" Then say what matters most to you ([KDIGO 2024][K24], section 5.5).
- Ask who to call with questions, and how soon you will hear about results.

## Your team and your rights (US dialysis units)

- Your dialysis team must include you (if you wish), a nurse, a doctor, a social worker and a
  dietitian. Together you make a **written plan of care**, which you are asked to sign, and it is
  reviewed at least once a year ([42 CFR 494.80 and 494.90][CFR494]).
- A doctor, nurse practitioner, clinical nurse specialist or physician assistant must see you at least
  once a month ([42 CFR 494.90][CFR494]).
- You have the right to information you can understand, to see your own results, and to make a
  complaint, inside the unit or to outside bodies, without losing care. The unit must post the phone
  numbers ([42 CFR 494.70][CFR494]).

KDIGO also supports care by phone, video and apps alongside visits ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point
5.3.3). Ask whether some visits can be remote.

## What to do

- [ ] A week before: write your top three questions and update your medicine list.
- [ ] Two days before: export the app's period summary or CSV and download your CGM report.
- [ ] Bring your home blood pressure and weight readings.
- [ ] Bring a family member or friend if you can.
- [ ] At the end: teach-back the plan, and ask for changes in writing.
- [ ] Afterwards: update your medicine list, targets in the app, and your calendar for the next tests.

## Examples

**Food questions for the dietitian.** You export a 30-day CSV and the period summary from the app's
Trends screen. The dietitian sees that your average sodium is over target on work days, from
sandwiches. You swap two lunches a week for home-made ones ([Reports for your care team](../app/reports-for-your-team.md)).

**A medicine change.** Your nephrologist starts finerenone. You say back: "Blood test for potassium in
four weeks; avoid grapefruit; call if I am vomiting." The doctor adds: "And bring your sick-day plan
next time." The label asks for a potassium check 4 weeks after starting, and says to avoid grapefruit
([Kerendia label][KER]). See [Kidney-protecting medicines](../t1d/kidney-protecting-medicines.md).

## Ask your care team

1. What are my latest results, and are they better, worse or the same as last time?
2. What should I do differently before my next visit?
3. Which changes to my medicines or insulin plan should I have in writing?
4. Who do I call with questions between visits?
5. Can my next visit be by phone or video?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Do not wait for your appointment"
    Chest pain, severe breathlessness, a low the person cannot treat (give glucagon, then call 911),
    ketones of 3.0 mmol/L or more, or a lab potassium of 6.5 mmol/L or more: call 911 now. Potassium
    6.0–6.4, ketones 1.6–2.9, vomiting or a missed dialysis session: call your team today.
    Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
