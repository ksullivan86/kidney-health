# Sleep, restless legs and itch

## In short

About half of people with kidney disease sleep poorly, and almost as many are bothered by itch
([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 49). Common causes are **restless legs**, **itch**, **sleep apnoea**,
**waking to pass urine**, worry, and with type 1 diabetes, **lows at night**. Most of these can be
treated, so tell your team: KDIGO asks kidney teams to ask about symptoms at every visit, and the ADA
asks diabetes teams to ask about sleep ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.2.2.1;
[ADA 2026 §5][A26-5]). For restless legs, the first step is a simple iron test
([AASM 2024][AASM24]).

## Your numbers

**How common** ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 49): tiredness about 70 % of people with CKD; poor sleep
about 49 %; itch about 46 %.

**Iron and restless legs** ([AASM 2024][AASM24]):

=== "US units"

    | Who | Iron treatment is considered when |
    |---|---|
    | Adults with restless legs | ferritin 75 ng/mL or less, or transferrin saturation (TSAT) under 20 % |
    | Restless legs with kidney failure | ferritin under 200 ng/mL **and** TSAT under 20 % (iron given into a vein) |

=== "International"

    | Who | Iron treatment is considered when |
    |---|---|
    | Adults with restless legs | ferritin 75 µg/L or less, or transferrin saturation (TSAT) under 20 % |
    | Restless legs with kidney failure | ferritin under 200 µg/L **and** TSAT under 20 % (iron given into a vein) |

These thresholds are for restless legs. Your kidney team also checks iron for anaemia, with its own
numbers ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]; [Haemoglobin and iron](../labs/haemoglobin-and-iron.md)).

**Night-time lows on a CGM report** ([time-in-range consensus][TIR19]):

=== "US units"

    | Measure | Most adults | Older or higher-risk adults |
    |---|---|---|
    | Time below 70 mg/dL | under 4 % (about 1 hour a day) | under 1 % |
    | Time below 54 mg/dL | under 1 % (about 15 minutes a day) | — |

=== "International"

    | Measure | Most adults | Older or higher-risk adults |
    |---|---|---|
    | Time below 3.9 mmol/L | under 4 % (about 1 hour a day) | under 1 % |
    | Time below 3.0 mmol/L | under 1 % (about 15 minutes a day) | — |

## Restless legs

Restless legs cause an uncomfortable urge to move your legs, mostly in the evening or at night when
you are still. Moving eases it for a while ([AASM 2024][AASM24]).

**What your team may do** ([AASM 2024][AASM24]; [KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 38):

- **Check your iron** (ferritin and TSAT), ideally in the morning, and treat low iron.
- **Look for things that make it worse:** caffeine, alcohol, sedating antihistamines (ask your
  pharmacist whether a sleep or allergy aid contains one), some antidepressants and anti-sickness
  medicines, and untreated sleep apnoea. A high phosphate level can also play a part.
- **Medicines:** gabapentin-type medicines are now a first choice. Older dopamine-type medicines
  (such as pramipexole, ropinirole and rotigotine) are no longer the standard choice, because over
  time they can make restless legs worse. With kidney failure, the AASM also suggests vitamin C.
  Doses must be set for your kidney function, so never start or change one on your own.

## Itch

- Itch is common and can wreck sleep. Tell your team; do not just put up with it
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.2.2.1).
- For dry skin, use a moisturiser every day. Other options your team may
  suggest include gabapentin-type medicines and light therapy (UVB) ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 38).
- **Severe itch that nothing helps** can be a sign that kidney failure needs treatment, such as
  starting or adjusting dialysis ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 41).

## Sleep apnoea and waking at night

- Loud snoring, stopping breathing in your sleep, gasping, morning headaches or falling asleep in
  the day are reasons to ask for a sleep test. Untreated sleep apnoea also makes restless legs worse
  ([AASM 2024][AASM24]; [ADA 2026 §5][A26-5]).
- Waking often to pass urine is worth raising. Your team can look for causes, including high
  glucose overnight ([ADA 2026 §5][A26-5]).

## Night-time lows

Kidney disease makes lows more likely, because failing kidneys clear less insulin and make less
glucose ([Pecoits-Filho 2016][DG55a]). Lows at night are easy to miss. Signs include nightmares or crying out, sheets or pyjamas damp with sweat, and
waking tired, irritable or confused ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]).

- A CGM can alarm when glucose drops below your target ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]; [ADA 2026 §7][A26-7]).
  Keep the alarm on and loud enough to wake you, or share alerts with a family member.
- Keep glucose tablets by the bed. They work fast and add no potassium, phosphorus or fluid
  ([Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md)).
- **Always treat a low at night**, even if you are tired: 15 g, recheck in 15 minutes
  ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).
- If you have lows at night, tell your diabetes team. Your plan may need to change. Never change your
  insulin on your own because of this page.

## What to do

- [ ] Keep a one-week sleep diary: bedtime, waking, what woke you (legs, itch, toilet, a low), and
      how you felt in the day.
- [ ] Ask your team for an iron test if you have restless legs ([AASM 2024][AASM24]).
- [ ] Cut down on caffeine and alcohol, which can make restless legs worse ([AASM 2024][AASM24]).
- [ ] Bring every medicine and sleep aid you take, including over-the-counter ones, to your next visit.
- [ ] Keep a regular bedtime, a dark, cool room, and screens out of bed ([ADA 2026 §5][A26-5];
      [KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 38).
- [ ] Moisturise dry skin every day.
- [ ] Turn on your CGM's low alert and keep glucose tablets within reach of the bed.

## Examples

**Restless legs on dialysis.** You cannot sit still in the evenings. Your team checks your iron:
ferritin 150 ng/mL (150 µg/L) and TSAT 15 %. That is below the AASM threshold for people with kidney
failure, so they give iron into a vein during dialysis ([AASM 2024][AASM24]). You also stop the
over-the-counter sleep aid you were taking, after your pharmacist explains that its antihistamine can
make restless legs worse.

**Sweaty sheets.** You wake up with damp sheets and a headache. Your CGM shows 3 hours under 70 mg/dL
(3.9 mmol/L) overnight. You tell your diabetes team the next day, and they change your plan
([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]; [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).

## Ask your care team

1. Could my restless legs be from low iron? Can you check ferritin and TSAT?
2. Do any of my medicines or sleep aids make restless legs or sleep worse?
3. What can I do about itch? Is my dialysis or phosphate playing a part?
4. Should I have a sleep test for sleep apnoea?
5. How should my CGM low alert be set at night?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    You cannot breathe lying flat or wake up gasping and breathless (this can be fluid on the lungs);
    someone cannot be woken up or has a low they cannot treat (give glucagon, then call); chest pain.
    Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your team today"
    Lows at night that keep coming back, or itch, restless legs or sleeplessness that suddenly get much
    worse.
