# Sick days with type 1 diabetes and CKD

--8<-- "includes/no-dosing.md"

## In short

Illness, vomiting and diarrhea can push glucose and ketones up fast, even when you eat little. With
type 1 diabetes that can lead to diabetic ketoacidosis (**DKA**). With kidney disease, losing fluid can
also hurt the kidneys and change your potassium. **Never stop your basal insulin**, check glucose and
ketones often, and follow a sick-day plan your team wrote down *before* you got ill. The plan says
which of your medicines to pause, and exactly when to restart them ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6];
[KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 4.3.2–4.3.3).

## Your numbers

**When to check:**

=== "US units"

    | Check | How often when you are ill |
    |---|---|
    | Glucose | at least every 4 hours, day and night ([CDC][CDC-SICK]); a CGM helps |
    | Ketones | when you feel ill, miss insulin or have symptoms, especially if glucose is above 200 mg/dL ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]); then as your plan says |

=== "International"

    | Check | How often when you are ill |
    |---|---|
    | Glucose | at least every 4 hours, day and night ([CDC][CDC-SICK]); a CGM helps |
    | Ketones | when you feel ill, miss insulin or have symptoms, especially if glucose is above 11.1 mmol/L ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]); then as your plan says |

**What blood ketones mean** (the same numbers in every country):

| Blood ketones | What to do |
|---|---|
| under 0.6 mmol/L | normal; keep checking glucose |
| 0.6–1.5 mmol/L | follow your sick-day plan; check again in 2 hours. If you are also ill, call your team |
| 1.6–2.9 mmol/L | **call your diabetes team now**. If you cannot reach them quickly, or you are vomiting, go to the emergency department |
| 3.0 mmol/L or more | **emergency: call 911 or go to the emergency department** |

Sources: [NHS][NHS-DKA]; [ADA][ADAK] (the American Diabetes Association advises emergency care at
1.6 mmol/L or more). DKA is confirmed with blood ketones of 3.0 mmol/L or more and acid in the blood
([2024 consensus][HC24]). UK dialysis guidance also says that blood ketones above 3.0 mmol/L need
medical assessment straight away ([JBDS 2022][JBDS22]).

**Urine strips** are less exact. NHS advice reads + as roughly 0.6–1.5 mmol/L, ++ as roughly
1.5–3.0 and +++ as above 3.0 ([NHS ketone advice][NHSK]). This handbook treats **++ or more** like
3.0 mmol/L and says get emergency help, because the 2024 consensus counts urine ketones of 2+ or more
toward DKA ([2024 consensus][HC24]). A blood ketone meter
is better: in a trial, blood checks halved hospital and emergency visits compared with urine strips
([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).

!!! warning "Ketones are the deciding number with kidney disease"
    DKA is partly diagnosed by a low bicarbonate. Many people with advanced kidney disease have a low
    bicarbonate anyway ([Bicarbonate](../labs/bicarbonate.md)), so that number does not tell you much. Watch your **ketones** and how you feel.
    People with kidney failure often have **lower** ketone numbers in DKA than other people
    ([2024 consensus][HC24], Table 4; [JBDS 2022][JBDS22]). On dialysis, the usual signs, such as
    thirst, dry mouth and deep fast breathing, may be missing ([JBDS 2022][JBDS22]). Trust symptoms
    like vomiting, belly pain, drowsiness or fruity breath even if the meter reads under 3.0.

## What to do when you get ill

- [ ] **Keep taking your basal insulin, even if you are not eating.** Illness often raises insulin
      needs. Your plan says how to adjust ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]; [NHS][NHS-DKA]).
- [ ] Check glucose at least every 4 hours, including at night ([CDC][CDC-SICK]).
- [ ] Check **blood** ketones and act on the table above. If you make little or no urine, urine
      strips will not work: use a blood ketone meter ([JBDS 2022][JBDS22]).
- [ ] If you cannot eat meals, take carbohydrate in small amounts through the day. CDC suggests about
      50 g every 4 hours ([CDC][CDC-SICK]). Kidney-friendly choices are below.
- [ ] Take your sick-day plan out and follow the medicine part: pause only what it says to pause.
- [ ] Write down the time and result of every check, and what you took. The app's log works for food
      and drinks ([Logging food](../app/logging.md)).
- [ ] Call your team early, not late. Use the after-hours number if needed.

## Medicines on sick days

When you lose fluid through vomiting, diarrhea or fever, some medicines can harm the kidneys, drop
your blood pressure or raise potassium. KDIGO lists medicines that sick-day plans often pause:
**sulfonylureas, ACE inhibitors, diuretics ("water pills"), metformin, ARBs, NSAIDs and SGLT2
inhibitors** ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 4.3.2). Metformin and sulfonylureas are rarely used
in type 1 diabetes.

| Medicine | Why it matters when you are dehydrated | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| ACE inhibitor or ARB | can lower blood pressure, strain the kidneys and raise potassium | "Should I pause it while I am vomiting? When do I restart?" |
| Diuretic (water pill) | removes more fluid when you are already low | the same question |
| NSAID (ibuprofen, naproxen) | harms the kidneys; best avoided anyway | "What can I take for fever or pain instead?" ([Medicines to avoid](../medicines/avoid.md)) |
| SGLT2 inhibitor (not established in type 1; see [Kidney-protecting medicines](kidney-protecting-medicines.md)) | raises the risk of DKA, even with normal glucose ([Farxiga label][FARXIGA]) | "Do I stop it as soon as I feel ill? Check ketones even if glucose is normal?" |
| **Insulin** | **never on the stop list** | "How do I adjust my insulin when I am ill?" |

KDIGO adds two warnings. There is little evidence that sick-day rules prevent kidney injury, and
mistakes happen. **The most common problem is forgetting to restart** a paused medicine, so the
restart plan must be written down ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 4.3.2–4.3.3). A medicine
review within a month after the illness helps catch anything not restarted ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).

## Food and fluids when you are ill

If you cannot eat normal meals, these are kinder to a kidney diet than orange juice or milk. Values
are per serving from USDA FoodData Central, as in the app's food list ([USDA][FDC]):

| Food or drink | Carbohydrate | Potassium | Fluid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cranberry juice cocktail, ½ cup | 17 g | 18 mg | 120 mL |
| Apple juice, ½ cup | 14 g | 125 mg | 120 mL |
| Ginger ale or lemon-lime soda, regular, 4 oz | 11–13 g | 1 mg | 120 mL |
| Gelatin dessert, regular, ½ cup | 19 g | 1 mg | 120 mL (counts as fluid) |
| Ice pop, 1 (1¾ fl oz) | 10 g | 8 mg | about 50 mL |
| Applesauce, unsweetened, ½ cup | 14 g | 90 mg | small |
| Saltine crackers, 5 | 11 g | 23 mg | none (141 mg sodium) |
| White bread or toast, 1 slice | 14 g | 37 mg | none (142 mg sodium) |
| Glucose tablets, 4 | 16 g | 0 mg | none |

Fluid is counted by volume, the cautious way ([Fluid](../eat/fluid.md)). When glucose is high,
sugar-free drinks may suit you better; they still count toward a fluid limit.
If glucose is low, treat the low first ([Treating a low](treating-a-low.md)).

**On a fluid limit (dialysis, or stage G4–G5 with swelling):** general advice says "drink plenty"
([CDC][CDC-SICK]), but that does not fit a fluid limit. Losing fluid through vomiting or diarrhea may
mean you need more for a day or two; not losing fluid means you do not. Ask your team **now** for a
sick-day fluid allowance and write it on your [sick-day card](../reference/wallet-card.md#card-4-my-sick-day-plan).
See [Fluid](../eat/fluid.md).

**On dialysis:** do not skip a session because you feel unwell. Call the unit. Missed sessions can
make DKA symptoms worse ([JBDS 2022][JBDS22]).

**After a transplant:** if you cannot keep your anti-rejection medicines down, call your transplant
center the same day, because missed doses risk rejection ([KDIGO 2009 transplant][K09TX], chapter 11).
Call them too for a fever over 100 °F (37.8 °C) ([NIDDK][NIDDK-Tx]).

## Your sick-day kit

Check it every few months:

- [ ] Blood ketone meter and strips, **in date** (strips expire)
- [ ] Glucose meter, strips and spare CGM sensor
- [ ] Glucose tablets or gel, and your measured juice or soda
- [ ] Glucagon, in date, and a family member who knows how to use it ([Treating a low](treating-a-low.md))
- [ ] Spare pump supplies or pens, and insulin
- [ ] Your written sick-day plan, with restart rules ([wallet card](../reference/wallet-card.md#card-4-my-sick-day-plan))
- [ ] Phone numbers: diabetes team, kidney team, dialysis unit or transplant center, after-hours line
- [ ] Thermometer

## Example

Ana has stage G4 kidney disease, takes an ACE inhibitor and has a fluid limit.

- **Day 1, 7 a.m.** She wakes up vomiting. Glucose 260 mg/dL (14.4 mmol/L); blood ketones
  0.9 mmol/L. She keeps her basal insulin, follows her plan for extra insulin, and pauses her ACE
  inhibitor because her written plan says to.
- **9 a.m.** Ketones 1.2. She sips small amounts of water within her sick-day fluid allowance and
  checks again in 2 hours.
- **11 a.m.** Ketones 1.8 and she vomits again. She calls her diabetes team straight away. They tell
  her to go to the emergency department because she cannot keep fluids down.
- **Day 4.** She is well. She restarts the ACE inhibitor on the day her written plan says, books the
  blood test the plan asks for, and ticks the restart off on her sick-day card.

## Ask your care team

1. Please write my sick-day plan: how to adjust insulin, how often to check, and what to do at each
   ketone level.
2. Which of my medicines should I pause when I am vomiting or have diarrhea, and when do I restart each one?
3. How much extra fluid can I have on a sick day?
4. Can you prescribe a blood ketone meter and strips?
5. Who do I call at night or at the weekend?
6. When should I have a blood test after an illness?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number) or go to the emergency department"
    - Blood ketones **3.0 mmol/L or more**, or urine ketones ++ or more ([NHS][NHS-DKA]; [2024 consensus][HC24]).
    - Ketones **1.6 mmol/L or more** and you cannot reach your team quickly, or you are vomiting ([ADA][ADAK]).
    - Any ketones with vomiting so you cannot keep fluids down ([NHS][NHS-DKA]; [CDC][CDC-DKA]).
    - You cannot keep liquids down for more than 4 hours, or vomiting or bad diarrhea lasts more than
      6 hours ([CDC][CDC-SICK]).
    - Glucose stays at 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L) or above, deep or fast breathing, fruity breath,
      confusion or drowsiness ([CDC][CDC-DKA]).
    - A low the person cannot treat themselves: give glucagon and call 911.

See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md) for every warning sign.
