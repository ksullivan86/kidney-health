# Treating a low

--8<-- "includes/no-dosing.md"

## In short

**Treat a low straight away. Potassium never delays treatment.** Take 15 g of fast carbohydrate,
recheck in 15 minutes, and repeat if you are still low ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6], recommendation 6.15).
Glucose tablets are the best choice on a kidney diet: exact, fast, and free of potassium,
phosphorus, sodium and fluid. If orange juice or cola is what is within reach, drink the measured
4 oz now and log it: one rescue portion is a budgeting note, an untreated low is an emergency.
Everyone on insulin should have glucagon, and the people around them should know how to use it
([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6], recommendation 6.16).

## Your numbers

=== "US units"

    | Level | Glucose | What it means |

    | Level 2 | below 54 mg/dL | treat now with 15 g; tell your team |
    | Level 3 | any low where you need someone else's help | glucagon, then call 911 |

=== "International"

    |---|---|---|
    | Level 1 | below 3.9 mmol/L | treat now with 15 g |
    | Level 2 | below 3.0 mmol/L | treat now with 15 g; tell your team |
    | Level 3 | any low where you need someone else's help | glucagon, then call your emergency number |

([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6].) Kidney disease makes lows more likely, because failing kidneys clear less
insulin and make less glucose ([Pecoits-Filho 2016][DG55a]).

## What to do: the rule of 15

- [ ] Glucose below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) and you can swallow: take **15 g of fast carbohydrate**
      (5–10 g if you use an automated insulin delivery system) ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).
- [ ] Do not use high-fat or high-protein foods (chocolate, milk, peanut butter) for the first
      treatment: they slow it down.
- [ ] **Recheck in 15 minutes.** Still low? Take another 15 g.
- [ ] If your next meal is more than an hour away, have a snack once you are back in range
      ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]).
- [ ] Log the treatment in the app like any food. The app never warns against or blocks a low treatment.
- [ ] If the person cannot swallow, is very drowsy or is fitting: **give glucagon, put them on their
      side, and call 911** (or your local emergency number). Never put food or drink in the mouth of
      someone who cannot swallow safely.

## Which 15 g to choose on a kidney diet

The ranking is about *which* 15 g to reach for when there is a choice. One 4-oz glass of orange juice
is about 250 mg potassium, which a day's budget absorbs; an untreated level 2–3 low is a medical
emergency. Never under-treat, and never use "sugar-free" sweets (sugar alcohols) to treat a low
([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]; [NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]).

| 15 g option | Carb g | Potassium mg | Phosphorus mg | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose tablets, 4 × 4 g (Dex4) | 16 | 0 | 0 | **First choice**: exact, fastest, no potassium, phosphorus or sodium ([Dex4][DG24b]) |
| Glucose gel, 1 tube | 15 | about 0 | 0 | Same; keep one at the bedside |
| Table sugar, 1 tbsp (4 tsp, about 17 g) | 13 | 0 | 0 | Clean and cheap |
| Hard candy, 3 pieces | 18 | 1 | 0 | Check the label; not "sugar-free" |
| Jelly beans, 15 small | 15 | about 6 | 0 | |
| Honey, 1 tbsp | 17 | 11 | 1 | Fine |
| Cranberry juice cocktail, ½ cup | 17 | 18 | 1 | Lowest-potassium juice; NIDDK's named alternative to orange juice ([NIDDK][DG4]) |
| Lemon-lime soda or ginger ale, regular, 4 oz | 11–13 | 1 | 0 | No phosphoric acid |
| Lemonade from concentrate, ¾ cup | about 20 | about 27 | 3 | |
| Applesauce, unsweetened, ½ cup | 14 | 90 | 6 | Good |
| Apple juice, ½ cup | 14 | 125 | 9 | Acceptable ([NIDDK][DG4]) |
| Grape juice, ½ cup | 19 | 132 | 18 | Acceptable ([NIDDK][DG4]) |
| Peaches or pears canned in juice, ½ cup | 14–16 | 119–160 | 15–21 | Acceptable, slower |
| Regular cola, 4 oz | 13 | 6 | about 11 (phosphoric acid) | Use it if it is what is there; clear soda is the better habit |
| Orange juice, ½ cup | 13 | **248** | 21 | Use it if it is what is there, and log it ([NIDDK][DG4]; [USDA][FDC]) |
| Milk, 1 % fat, 1 cup | 12 | **366** | **232** | Poor: slow (protein and fat), high potassium and phosphorus |
| Banana, small | 23 | **362** | 22 | Poor |
| Milk chocolate bar | 26 | 164 | 92 | Poor: fat slows absorption ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]) |

Values per serving, USDA FoodData Central ([FDC][FDC]); see also [DaVita][DG27].

**Two habits:** keep glucose tablets everywhere you go, and measure juice at 4 oz rather than
"a glass". Over a day with two or three lows, tablets vs orange juice is several hundred milligrams
of potassium.

**On a fluid limit:** glucose tablets and gel add no fluid; 4 oz of juice adds about 110 mL
([Fluid](../eat/fluid.md)).

## Glucagon

- Glucagon should be prescribed for **everyone who uses insulin**, preferably a form that does not need
  mixing, such as nasal or ready-to-use injectable glucagon ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6], recommendation 6.16).
- Family, friends, coworkers and the dialysis unit should know where it is and how to give it; ask your
  team or pharmacist to show them.
- After glucagon, call 911 (or your local emergency number) and turn the person on their side.

## Ask your care team

1. Please prescribe glucagon (nasal or ready-to-use) and show my family how to use it.
2. My lows are more frequent since my kidney function changed: should my insulin plan change?
3. Should I treat with 15 g or less because I use an automated insulin delivery system?
4. Can I keep glucose tablets at the dialysis unit, and what should staff do if I go low there?
5. Should I be screened for not feeling my lows (impaired awareness)?

## Get help now if…

- The person cannot treat the low themselves, cannot swallow, is very drowsy, confused or fitting:
  **give glucagon and call 911** (or your local emergency number).
- Lows keep happening, happen at night, or you no longer feel them coming: call your team today.
  See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
