# CKD stages: G and A categories

## In short

Kidney teams describe CKD with two labels: a **G category** from your eGFR (how well your kidneys
filter) and an **A category** from your UACR (how much albumin leaks into your urine)
([KDIGO 2024][K24], Tables 2–3). Together they read like "G3a A2". The risk of kidney failure and of
heart problems goes up both as eGFR falls **and** as albumin rises, so both numbers matter
([KDIGO 2024][K24], chapter 2). Stages are labels for planning, not a countdown. Your team can also
work out your **personal risk** of kidney failure from a tested equation.

## Your numbers

### G categories: filtering (eGFR)

eGFR comes from a blood test for creatinine, plus your age and sex ([NKF][NKF-eGFR];
[CKD-EPI 2021 equation][NKF-CKDEPI]); UACR comes from
a urine sample ([NIDDK][NIDDK-tests]). The eGFR unit is the same everywhere: mL/min/1.73 m².

| Category | eGFR | KDIGO term | Your stage page |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | 90 or more | normal or high | [G1 and G2](../stages/g1-g2.md) |
| G2 | 60–89 | mildly decreased | [G1 and G2](../stages/g1-g2.md) |
| G3a | 45–59 | mildly to moderately decreased | [G3a](../stages/g3a.md) |
| G3b | 30–44 | moderately to severely decreased | [G3b](../stages/g3b.md) |
| G4 | 15–29 | severely decreased | [G4](../stages/g4.md) |
| G5 | under 15 | kidney failure | [G5 without dialysis](../stages/g5-without-dialysis.md), or your dialysis or transplant page |

G1 and G2 count as CKD **only** if there is also a sign of damage, such as albumin in the urine
([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 2).

### A categories: albumin in the urine (UACR)

=== "US units"

    | Category | UACR | KDIGO term |
    |---|---|---|
    | A1 | under 30 mg/g | normal to mildly increased |
    | A2 | 30–300 mg/g | moderately increased |
    | A3 | over 300 mg/g | severely increased |

=== "International"

    | Category | UACR | KDIGO term |
    |---|---|---|
    | A1 | under 3 mg/mmol | normal to mildly increased |
    | A2 | 3–30 mg/mmol | moderately increased |
    | A3 | over 30 mg/mmol | severely increased |

Source: [KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 3. More on the test itself: [UACR](../labs/uacr.md).

### How the two combine

These examples show why both numbers count. Risk here means the chance of kidney failure, heart
problems and death compared with people without CKD ([KDIGO 2024][K24], chapter 2).

| Example results | Categories | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| eGFR 75, UACR 10 mg/g, no other damage | not CKD | normal for age; keep yearly checks if you have diabetes |
| eGFR 75, UACR 120 mg/g | G2 A2 | CKD. Albumin is the early sign in diabetes. A kidney-protecting medicine (ACE inhibitor or ARB) is recommended ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 3.6.3) |
| eGFR 40, UACR 15 mg/g | G3b A1 | high risk |
| eGFR 50, UACR 450 mg/g | G3a A3 | **higher** risk than the G3b A1 example, even though the eGFR is better |
| eGFR 22, any UACR | G4 | very high risk; time to plan with a nephrologist |

## Your risk of kidney failure (KFRE)

At G3a to G5, KDIGO recommends using a tested risk equation to estimate your own chance of kidney
failure ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 2.2.1). The best known is the **Kidney Failure Risk
Equation (KFRE)**. It uses your age, sex, eGFR and UACR, and gives a 2-year and a 5-year risk. KDIGO
says most people want to know this number, and asks labs to print it on the report when they can
([KDIGO 2024][K24], chapter 2).

| Your KFRE result | What KDIGO suggests it can be used for |
|---|---|
| 5-year risk of 3–5 % or more | a referral to a kidney specialist (nephrologist) |
| 2-year risk over 10 % | care from a whole team: diet advice, a medicine review, education about treatment choices, and emotional and social support (practice point 5.3.1) |
| 2-year risk over 40 % | getting ready for kidney failure: learning the options, planning dialysis access, a transplant referral |

Source: [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 2.2.1–2.2.3, 5.3.1 and 5.4.3. KDIGO also suggests planning for a
transplant or dialysis access when eGFR is under 15–20. The KFRE was built for G3 to G5 and **may not
be valid at G1–G2** (practice point 2.2.4).

## How often to check, and when a change is real

- **eGFR and UACR at least once a year**, and more often when your risk is higher and the result would
  change your care ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 2.1.1–2.1.2). ADA says 1 to 4 times a year,
  depending on the stage ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendation 11.1b).
- **eGFR:** a change of **more than 20 %** from one test to the next is more than normal day-to-day
  variation and should be looked into (practice point 2.1.3).
- **After starting a kidney-protecting medicine**, a small, early drop in eGFR is expected. A fall of
  **more than 30 %** should be looked into (practice points 2.1.4 and 3.6.4).
- **UACR:** a result that has **doubled** is more than lab variation (practice point 2.1.5).

**How to work out a change:** (old − new) ÷ old × 100.

- eGFR 50 → 41: 9 ÷ 50 = **18 %**. Within normal variation; check again as planned.
- eGFR 50 → 38: 12 ÷ 50 = **24 %**. More than noise: ask your team to repeat it and look for a cause,
  such as dehydration, an illness or a new medicine like ibuprofen.
- UACR 80 → 170 mg/g: **more than doubled**. Ask what it means for your treatment.

## Does age or sex change my numbers?

- The **G and A categories are the same for everyone**. KDIGO looked at changing the eGFR cut-off for
  younger and older adults and decided the current staging works at all adult ages
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], introduction).
- **Age and sex go into the eGFR equation** and into the KFRE, so they are already counted in those
  numbers ([CKD-EPI 2021 equation][NKF-CKDEPI]; [KDIGO 2024][K24], chapter 2).
- **Sex changes the anemia cut-offs**: hemoglobin under 13 g/dL (130 g/L) in men and under 12 g/dL
  (120 g/L) in women ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]).
- **Food goals:** protein is set per kilogram of body weight and the sodium goal is the same for all
  adults ([KDIGO 2022][K22], recommendations 3.1.1–3.1.2). Calorie needs do change with age, sex and
  activity ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], guideline 3.1.1). The app's "Suggest targets" uses these factors; see
  [First setup](../app/first-setup.md) and [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md).

## What to do

- [ ] Find your latest eGFR and UACR on your lab report and write down your G and A categories.
- [ ] Ask your team for your **KFRE** 2-year and 5-year risk if you are at G3a or beyond.
- [ ] Put your next eGFR and UACR test dates in your calendar.
- [ ] For a UACR test, give a **first-morning** urine sample if you can. Hard exercise, a period,
      blood in the urine or a urine infection can raise the result for a short time, so tell the lab
      or repeat the test ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 1.3.1.1 and Table 16).
- [ ] A cooked meat or fish meal can raise creatinine for hours and make eGFR look lower. KDIGO says
      waiting at least 12 hours after meat or fish best avoids this, so ask whether to fast from them
      before your blood test. Creatine supplements also raise creatinine
      ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 1.2.2.4).
- [ ] If a result changes by more than the amounts above, ask what might have caused it.
- [ ] Read your stage page ([Your stage, day to day](../stages/index.md)).

## Examples

- **Maria, 45, type 1 diabetes for 25 years:** eGFR 52, UACR 520 mg/g (59 mg/mmol). She is **G3a A3**.
  Her nephrologist starts an ACE inhibitor and talks about finerenone, because the albumin level puts
  her at higher risk than her eGFR alone suggests.
- **John, 72:** eGFR 38, UACR 18 mg/g. He is **G3b A1**. His 5-year KFRE is 2 %, so for now his family doctor
  keeps up yearly checks and a blood pressure plan, and will refer him if his risk rises.
- **Priya, 58, on her third year at G4:** her 2-year KFRE has risen to 42 %. Her team starts education
  on dialysis, transplant and conservative care, and refers her for a transplant evaluation.

## Ask your care team

1. What are my G and A categories today, and what were they a year ago?
2. What is my KFRE 2-year and 5-year risk?
3. How often will you check my eGFR and UACR?
4. Was my last change in eGFR or UACR more than normal variation? What might explain it?
5. At what point will you refer me to a nephrologist or a kidney team?

## Get help now if…

A stage change is not an emergency on its own. Much less urine, new swelling, breathlessness or a very
high potassium result can be: see [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
