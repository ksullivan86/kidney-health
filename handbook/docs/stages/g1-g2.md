# Stages G1 and G2

## In short

At G1 and G2 your kidneys still filter well (eGFR 60 or more), but there are signs of damage. With
type 1 diabetes the usual sign is **albumin leaking into the urine**. Most people feel nothing at
this stage, so the blood and urine tests are the only way to know ([NIDDK][NIDDK-whatis]). This is
the stage where treatment does the most good: blood pressure, glucose in range, a kidney-protecting
medicine if albumin is raised, activity and no smoking. You do **not** need a low-potassium or
low-phosphorus diet unless your blood tests say so.

### Tests to expect

| Test | How often |
|---|---|
| UACR and eGFR | at least once a year; 1–4 times a year once CKD is found, depending on your stage ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendations 11.1a–b; [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 2.1.1) |
| Potassium, creatinine and blood pressure | within 2–4 weeks of starting or raising an ACE inhibitor or ARB ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.6.2) |
| A1c or a CGM report | at your diabetes visits |

## What changes now

**Medicines.** If your UACR is in A2 or A3, KDIGO recommends an **ACE inhibitor or an ARB**
(strong recommendation, 1B), whatever your blood pressure ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 3.6.3).
Your prescriber aims for the highest approved amount you tolerate, because that is what protected
kidneys in the trials (practice point 3.6.1). An ACE inhibitor and an ARB are **never combined**
(recommendation 3.6.4). If you could become pregnant, ask about each medicine's safety in pregnancy
and about contraception before you start; KDIGO asks prescribers to review this every time
(practice point 4.1.4). Pregnancy planning is for your specialists: see
[Sex, fertility and pregnancy](../living/sex-fertility-pregnancy.md).

**Finerenone (Kerendia)** was approved in the US in September 2026 to lower UACR in adults with CKD
and type 1 diabetes. It is not started if blood potassium is above 5.0 mmol/L, and grapefruit must
be avoided while taking it ([Kerendia label][KER]). Ask whether it fits you. See
[Kidney-protecting medicines](../t1d/kidney-protecting-medicines.md).

**SGLT2 inhibitors** (such as dapagliflozin or empagliflozin) protect the kidneys in type 2 diabetes,
but none is approved for type 1 diabetes in the US, because they raise the risk of diabetic
ketoacidosis (DKA) ([ADA 2026 §9][A26-9]). If one is ever prescribed for you, ask for a written plan
for checking ketones and for sick days before you start.

**Pain medicines.** The National Kidney Foundation says people with CKD should avoid NSAIDs such as
ibuprofen and naproxen, because they cut blood flow to the kidneys ([NKF][NKFpain]). Acetaminophen at
the dose on the label is usually safer for the kidneys, but ask first.

**Food.** Eat more plants and fewer ultra-processed foods ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.3.1).
Keep sodium under 2,000 mg. Potassium and phosphorus limits are **not** needed unless your blood
levels are high.

**Diabetes.** Insulin needs fall as eGFR falls, so that matters more from G3 on
([JBDS–Renal Association][JBDS]). For now, keeping glucose in range is one of the main ways to slow
kidney damage ([KDIGO 2022][K22]).

## What to do

- [ ] Get your latest UACR and eGFR and write them down with the date.
- [ ] Check your blood pressure at home and bring the readings to your next visit.
- [ ] If your UACR is 30 mg/g (3 mg/mmol) or more, ask whether you should take an ACE inhibitor or
      ARB, and whether you are on the full amount you can tolerate.
- [ ] Ask whether finerenone is right for you.
- [ ] Read labels and keep sodium under 2,000 mg a day ([Sodium](../eat/sodium.md)).
- [ ] Move for 150 minutes a week, for example 30 minutes on 5 days ([Exercise](../living/exercise.md)).
- [ ] Do not take ibuprofen or naproxen as a habit. Ask the pharmacist before any pain or cold medicine.
- [ ] Stop smoking, and ask for help to do it ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.2.1).
- [ ] Never eat star fruit or drink its juice ([NKF][DG17]).
- [ ] Set your stage in the app and use "Suggest targets" ([First setup](../app/first-setup.md)).

## Examples

**A sandwich and the sodium budget.** Two slices of white bread have about 284 mg of sodium and a
tablespoon of mayonnaise about 88 mg. One ounce of processed American cheese adds about 470 mg. That
one sandwich is over 840 mg, more than 40 % of a 2,000 mg day ([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]). Swap
the cheese for home-roasted turkey or chicken and you save over 400 mg.

**Breakfast without a potassium rule.** A cup of cooked oatmeal (about 164 mg potassium) and a
banana are fine at G1–G2 if your blood potassium is normal ([USDA][FDC]). Count the carbohydrate for
your insulin as usual.

**A sore back on a Saturday.** Reach for acetaminophen at the label dose, not ibuprofen, and ask the
pharmacist if you are not sure ([NKF][NKFpain]).

## Ask your care team

1. What is my UACR, and is it A1, A2 or A3? Is it going up or down?
2. Should I be on an ACE inhibitor or an ARB? Am I on the amount that protects the kidneys?
3. Am I a candidate for finerenone?
4. What blood pressure goal should I aim for at home, and how often should I check it?
5. How often will you check my eGFR and UACR?
6. Do I need any change in what I eat?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Chest pain, severe breathlessness, a low the person cannot treat themselves (give glucagon, then
    call), or possible DKA: blood ketones of 3.0 mmol/L or more, or any ketones with vomiting, deep
    fast breathing or fruity breath ([2024 consensus][HC24]). Full list:
    [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    You feel faint or dizzy when you stand after starting a blood pressure medicine ([KDIGO 2024][K24],
    practice point 3.6.5), you have new swelling of the legs or face, or you are vomiting or cannot eat
    (start your [sick-day plan](../t1d/sick-days.md)).
