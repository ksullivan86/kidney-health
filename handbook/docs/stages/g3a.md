# Stage G3a

## In short

G3a means your eGFR has been **45–59** for more than 3 months. Most people still feel well
([NIDDK][NIDDK-whatis]). Three things change now. **Medicines** need checking against your eGFR.
**New blood tests** start for bones and anaemia. And **lows can become more common**, because the
kidneys clear less insulin ([JBDS–Renal Association][JBDS]; [ADA–KDIGO 2022][AK22]). This is also the
time to write a sick-day plan with your team.

## Your numbers

**Starting numbers at G3a** (70 kg adult with type 1 diabetes; the same as the app's "Suggest targets"):

| Protein | Potassium | Phosphorus | Sodium | Fluid | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.8 g/kg (about 56 g) | review ceiling 4,000 mg, only if your blood potassium is high | 1,000 mg, used when blood phosphate is high | under 2,000 mg | no set limit | 30 kcal/kg (about 2,100 kcal) |

Protein stays at 0.8 g/kg with diabetes: going lower does not help glucose, the heart or kidney
function ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendation 11.3; [KDIGO 2022][K22], recommendation 3.1.1).

## What changes now

**Medicines are dosed by eGFR.** Many medicines leave the body through the kidneys, so the dose may
need to change as eGFR falls ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 4.2.1). Tell every doctor, dentist and
pharmacist "I have kidney disease, stage 3a, eGFR …". Ask for a full medicine review at each change of
care, such as a hospital stay (practice point 4.3.1). Over-the-counter and herbal products need a
check too (practice point 4.1.3). Avoid NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen; NKF says to avoid them
especially when eGFR is under 60 ([NKF][NKFpain]).

**New blood tests.** Calcium, phosphate, PTH and alkaline phosphatase are checked from G3a, because
failing kidneys upset the balance that keeps bones strong and blood vessels soft
([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 3.1.1). Haemoglobin is checked for anaemia
([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]).

**Potassium and phosphorus.** Limit them only if your blood levels are high. Then a renal dietitian
should help you ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 3.11.5.1–3.11.5.2; [KDOQI 2020][Q20]).

**Lows become more likely.** The kidneys clear insulin and make some glucose. As eGFR falls, insulin
needs fall too ([JBDS–Renal Association][JBDS]). People with CKD have lows about twice as often as
people with normal kidney function, according to the JBDS review. The ADA asks for a check of your
awareness of lows at least once a year ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6], recommendation 6.11). Do not change
your insulin yourself: tell your diabetes team about any pattern of lows.

**Your risk number.** From G3, your team can use the Kidney Failure Risk Equation (KFRE) to estimate
your chance of needing dialysis or a transplant ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 2.2.1). A 5-year
risk of 3–5 % or more is a reason to see a nephrologist (practice point 2.2.1).

**A sick-day plan.** When you are ill and not eating or drinking, some medicines may need a pause.
KDIGO asks for a clear, written plan that says **when to restart** (practice point 4.3.2). Insulin
is never stopped: see [Sick days](../t1d/sick-days.md).

## What to do

- [ ] Write your eGFR on your medicine list and show it to every prescriber and pharmacist
      ([Your medicine list](../medicines/your-medicine-list.md)).
- [ ] Ask for a medicine review against your eGFR.
- [ ] Clear NSAIDs out of the house, or label them "not for me" ([Medicines to avoid](../medicines/avoid.md)).
- [ ] Ask when your calcium, phosphate, PTH and haemoglobin will first be checked.
- [ ] Ask whether you need any potassium or phosphorus limit, based on your last results.
- [ ] Look at your CGM report for lows, especially at night, and bring it to your diabetes visit
      ([CGM metrics](../labs/cgm-metrics.md)).
- [ ] Check your glucagon is in date and someone at home knows how to use it
      ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6], recommendation 6.16).
- [ ] Write a sick-day plan with your team, including when to restart each paused medicine.
- [ ] Ask what your KFRE is.
- [ ] Update your stage in the app so the warnings and targets fit
      ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## Examples

**At the pharmacy.** You have a cold and pick up a "multi-symptom" product. Many contain ibuprofen or
naproxen. Ask the pharmacist for one without an NSAID, and say your eGFR ([NKF][NKFpain]).

**A new pattern of lows.** Your CGM shows glucose under 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) two mornings a week
since your eGFR fell. Treat each low straight away with 15 g of fast carbohydrate, such as 4 glucose
tablets, and recheck after 15 minutes ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]). Then send the report to your diabetes
team. They may change your insulin plan. Do not change it yourself.

**A blood test after a new medicine.** You start an ACE inhibitor in March. Book a blood test for
potassium and creatinine in 2–4 weeks ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.6.2). A small rise in
creatinine is expected; a rise of more than 30 % needs review (practice point 3.6.4).

## Ask your care team

1. Which of my medicines need a different dose at my eGFR?
2. When will you check my calcium, phosphate, PTH and haemoglobin, and what were the results?
3. Do I need any limit on potassium or phosphorus now?
4. What is my KFRE? Should I see a nephrologist?
5. Should my insulin plan change now that my kidneys clear less insulin?
6. Can we write my sick-day plan today, with restart dates?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Chest pain, severe breathlessness, fainting or a very slow or irregular pulse (especially with a
    lab potassium of 6.5 mmol/L or more), a low the person
    cannot treat themselves (give glucagon, then call), or possible DKA: blood ketones of 3.0 mmol/L
    or more, or any ketones with vomiting, deep fast breathing or fruity breath
    ([2024 consensus][HC24]). Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    Vomiting, diarrhoea or not eating (start your sick-day plan); lows that keep happening or that you
    no longer feel coming; dizziness on standing after a new blood pressure medicine; a lab potassium
    of 6.0–6.4 mmol/L: KDIGO advises a repeat test within 24 hours, or checks and treatment in
    hospital **now** if you feel unwell ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28; 6.5 or more: see
    [Get help now](../get-help-now.md)).
