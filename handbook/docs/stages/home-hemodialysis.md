# Home hemodialysis

## In short

With home hemodialysis you, usually with a trained care partner, run the dialysis machine at home
after weeks of training ([NKF][NKF-HHD]; [NIDDK][NIDDK-HD]). You can dialyse on a standard schedule,
for a short time on most days, or overnight while you sleep. More frequent sessions remove fluid more
often, so the **daily fluid allowance is usually larger** ([DaVita][DG23]). You need space, supplies,
and a plan for **power cuts and supply delays** ([CMS][CMSPREP]). With type 1 diabetes, check glucose
before and after every session, just as in a unit ([JBDS–Renal Association][JBDS]).

### Schedules

| Type | How often | How long | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | 3 times a week, or every other day | 3–5 hours | [NIDDK][NIDDK-HD] |
| Short daily | 5–7 days a week | 2–4 hours | [NIDDK][NIDDK-HD]; [NKF][NKF-HHD] |
| Nocturnal (overnight) | 3–6 nights a week | while you sleep, often 6–8 hours | [NIDDK][NIDDK-HD]; [NKF][NKF-HHD] |

### Other numbers

| What | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Training | 4½–6 hours a day, 5 days a week, for 3–8 weeks | [NIDDK][NIDDK-HD] |
| Fluid | larger than in-centre, because fluid is removed more often; your unit sets it | [DaVita][DG23] |
| Protein | 1.0–1.2 g/kg a day (70–84 g at 70 kg), as on in-centre dialysis | [KDOQI 2020][Q20]; [KDIGO 2022][K22] |
| Emergency supplies | keep spare supplies as your unit directs; 5–7 days of medicines, insulin, strips and glucose | [CMS][CMSPREP] |
| Medicare start | can begin in the **first month** of dialysis if you start a home training programme in the first 3 months (otherwise the fourth month) | [Medicare][MED-ESRD] |

### Tests to expect

The same blood tests as in-centre dialysis: calcium and phosphate every 1–3 months, PTH every 3–6
months ([KDIGO 2017][K17]), haemoglobin and iron about every 3 months ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]), and
dialysis dose checks by your home unit ([KDOQI 2015][Q15HD]).

## What changes now

**What people notice.** NKF reports that people on short daily and overnight schedules often need
fewer blood pressure medicines, feel less "washed out" after dialysis, have more energy for daily
tasks, and have fewer and shorter hospital stays ([NKF][NKF-HHD]).

**What KDOQI asks your team to tell you.** Longer or more frequent home dialysis can mean more access
problems, more strain on your care partner, and a faster loss of the urine you still make
([KDOQI 2015][Q15HD]).

**A care partner.** Most programmes ask for a care partner to be with you during each treatment
([NKF][NKF-HHD]). Both of you are trained.

**Diet and fluid.** Ask your dietitian whether your potassium, phosphorus and fluid limits change on
your new schedule. Protein stays at 1.0–1.2 g/kg.

**Diabetes** ([JBDS–Renal Association][JBDS]):

- Check glucose before and after each session. Glucose is often lowest after dialysis.
- Your insulin plan is set around your schedule by your team. Ask for it in writing for dialysis days
  and non-dialysis days.
- If you dialyse overnight, agree with your team how your CGM low alert is set and who responds to it.
- Keep glucose tablets within reach of the machine and the bed.

**Access care.** The same as in a unit: feel the thrill daily, no blood pressure cuffs or blood tests
on the access arm, and watch for signs of infection ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]).

## What to do

- [ ] Ask your unit whether your home has the space, water supply and electrics the machine needs.
- [ ] Choose and train a care partner, if your programme asks for one.
- [ ] Register with your power and water companies for priority restoration ([CMS][CMSPREP]).
- [ ] Keep a flashlight and batteries next to the machine ([CMS][CMSPREP]).
- [ ] Ask your unit for a back-up unit, near home and further away, for times you cannot dialyse at home.
- [ ] Keep a 5–7 day supply of medicines, insulin, strips and glucose, and in-date glucagon.
- [ ] Set your dialysis days in the app so it totals fluid, potassium and sodium between sessions
      ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).
- [ ] Write your session times next to your glucose readings, so your team can see the pattern.

## Examples

**A power cut on a dialysis night.** Follow your unit's emergency steps. Call the unit and, if needed,
arrange a session at your back-up unit. Until then, follow your unit's 3-day emergency diet and fluid
limit, and try to get dialysis within 3 days of your last treatment ([CMS][CMSPREP]). Keep treating
lows straight away.

**A short-daily week.** On 6 days a week of short sessions, your fluid is removed every day instead of
three times a week, so your daily allowance is usually larger ([DaVita][DG23]). Ask your unit for your
number, and put it in the app.

**An overnight low.** Your CGM alerts at 3 a.m. during a session. Your care partner gives 4 glucose
tablets (or you take them), you recheck in 15 minutes ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]), and you report it to your
team in the morning.

## Ask your care team

1. Which home schedule would suit me and my care partner?
2. What does my home need: space, plumbing, electrics, storage?
3. How long is training, and can my care partner train with me?
4. Will my fluid, potassium and phosphorus limits change?
5. How should my insulin plan change on my schedule?
6. What is the plan for power cuts, storms or late supply deliveries?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Chest pain; severe breathlessness; fainting; bleeding from your access that you cannot stop with
    firm pressure; a low the person cannot treat themselves (give glucagon, then call). If you must
    leave in an emergency, follow your unit's disconnection training. Full list:
    [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your home unit today"
    No buzz (thrill) in your access; redness or pus at an access or catheter site; a missed session;
    weight gain above your limit; machine or supply problems you cannot fix.
