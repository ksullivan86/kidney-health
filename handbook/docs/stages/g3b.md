# Stage G3b

## In short

G3b means your eGFR has been **30–44** for more than 3 months. Everything on the
[G3a page](g3a.md) still applies. At G3b more people need a **potassium** limit, the blood
**bicarbonate** (acid balance) starts to matter, **anaemia** becomes more common, and it is time to
see a **nephrologist** if your risk of kidney failure is rising ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). Many people still
feel well, so the blood tests guide what to do.

### Blood tests that matter more now

=== "US units"

    | Test | Typical (your lab's range wins) | What your team watches for |
    |---|---|---|
    | Potassium | 3.5–5.0 mEq/L | 6.0–6.4 "moderate"; 6.5 or more "severe" |
    | Bicarbonate (CO2) | 22 mEq/L or more; lower means acid is building up | under 18: treatment is considered |
    | Haemoglobin | men 13 g/dL or more; women 12 g/dL or more | below that is anaemia; iron is checked first |
    | Iron (TSAT and ferritin) | – | TSAT under 20 % with ferritin under 100 ng/mL means iron deficiency |
    | Calcium and phosphate | phosphate 2.5–4.5 mg/dL | checked every 6–12 months |

    | Test | Typical (your lab's range wins) | What your team watches for |

## What changes now

**Seeing a nephrologist.** KDIGO lists reasons to be seen by kidney specialists, including a
5-year kidney failure risk (KFRE) above 3–5 %, an eGFR under 30, a fall in eGFR of more than 20 %,
a UACR of 300 mg/g (30 mg/mmol) or more with blood in the urine, a UACR above 700 mg/g, and problems
with potassium, acid, anaemia, bones or nutrition ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.1.1 and
Figure 48; [ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendation 11.12a).

**Potassium.** Limit potassium only if your blood level runs high, and then with a renal dietitian
([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.11.5.1). The biggest wins are portion size, foods with potassium
**additives**, and a few very high foods. About 90 % of potassium from additives is absorbed, so
KDIGO advises limiting processed foods first (practice point 3.11.5.2). A day's limit, when one is
needed, is often 2,000–3,000 mg ([DaVita][DG26]). With diabetes, high glucose and too little insulin
also push potassium up ([Sousa 2016][DG36]).

**Acid (bicarbonate).** Failing kidneys keep more acid in the body. If your bicarbonate falls, your
team may suggest tablets or, if your potassium allows, more fruit and vegetables. Treatment is
considered when bicarbonate is under 18 mmol/L ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.10.1).

**Anaemia.** Low haemoglobin makes you tired and short of breath. Your team checks iron first,
because iron deficiency is common and treatable ([KDIGO 2026 anemia][K26A]).

**Protein with diabetes.** Keep to about **0.8 g/kg a day** ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendation
11.3; [KDIGO 2022][K22], recommendation 3.1.1; [KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 3.3.1.1). KDOQI 2020
allows 0.6–0.8 g/kg with diabetes only under close supervision ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 3.0.2).
This handbook and the app use 0.8 g/kg. Your dietitian decides with you. See [Protein](../eat/protein.md).

## What to do

- [ ] Ask for your latest potassium, bicarbonate and haemoglobin results, and write them down.
- [ ] Ask what your KFRE is and whether you should see a nephrologist now.
- [ ] If your potassium is high, ask for a renal dietitian appointment.
- [ ] Check ingredient lists for "potassium chloride" and other potassium salts
      ([Reading labels](../eat/label-reading.md)).
- [ ] Never use salt substitutes such as "lite salt": they are potassium chloride ([Morton][DG35b]).
- [ ] Keep fruit and vegetables to ½-cup portions if you have a potassium limit
      ([Potassium](../eat/potassium.md)).
- [ ] Keep glucose in range and never miss basal insulin: both affect potassium.
- [ ] If you feel more tired or breathless than usual, ask for a haemoglobin and iron check.
- [ ] Set your stage to 3b in the app: the potassium review ceiling becomes 3,500 mg
      ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## Examples

**Swaps that save potassium** ([USDA FoodData Central][FDC]; [NKF][DG5]):

| Instead of | Potassium | Try | Potassium |
|---|---|---|---|
| ½ cup orange juice | about 248 mg | ½ cup apple juice | about 125 mg |
| 1 small banana | about 362 mg | ½ cup blueberries | about 57 mg |
| 1 cup cantaloupe | about 417 mg | ½ cup cantaloupe | about 200 mg |
| ¼ tsp "lite" salt | 350 mg | herbs, lemon, garlic, pepper | very little in the amounts used |

**Portion matters.** "A large serving of a low-potassium food becomes a high-potassium food"
([NKF][DG5]). Half a cup of cantaloupe is a medium choice; a whole cup is high.

**A low on a potassium limit.** Treat it straight away. If orange juice is all there is, drink it:
4 oz is about 250 mg, which your day can absorb. Glucose tablets have none
([Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md)).

## Ask your care team

1. What is my KFRE? Should I see a nephrologist, and when?
2. Is my potassium high enough to need a limit? What number should I aim for?
3. What was my bicarbonate? Do I need treatment for it?
4. Is my haemoglobin low? Have my iron levels been checked?
5. Can I see a renal dietitian?
6. Is 0.8 g/kg the right amount of protein for me?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Chest pain; severe breathlessness; fainting, a very slow or irregular pulse or sudden severe
    weakness, especially with a lab potassium of 6.5 mmol/L or more; a low the person cannot treat
    themselves (give glucagon, then call). Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    A lab potassium of 6.0–6.4 mmol/L: KDIGO advises assessment in hospital if you feel unwell,
    otherwise a repeat test within 24 hours ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28). Also vomiting, diarrhoea
    or not eating (start your [sick-day plan](../t1d/sick-days.md)).
