# Stage G4

## In short

G4 means your eGFR has been **15–29** for more than 3 months. It is the **planning stage**. You may
still feel fairly well, but now is the time to learn about every option, from transplant to dialysis
to care without dialysis, while you have time to choose ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.5.1).
Ask for a **transplant referral** now ([KDIGO 2020 transplant][K20TX]). **Protect the veins in your
arms** for a future dialysis access ([KDOQI vascular access][Q19VA]). Your diet tightens a little, and
your diabetes care changes: lows become more likely and A1c becomes less reliable
([ADA–KDIGO 2022][AK22]; [KDIGO 2022][K22]).

### Your planning timeline

| When | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| eGFR under 30 | You should be told about, and considered for, a kidney transplant | [KDIGO 2020 transplant][K20TX], 1.1 |
| At least 6–12 months before dialysis would be needed | Referral for transplant evaluation, so a living donor can be found and tested | [KDIGO 2020 transplant][K20TX], 1.1.1 |
| eGFR 20 or less, once you are on the US waiting list | Your waiting time starts to count | [OPTN policy 8][OPTN] |
| 2-year kidney failure risk (KFRE) over 10 % | Join multidisciplinary kidney care | [KDIGO 2024][K24], 2.2.2 |
| eGFR 15–20, or 2-year KFRE over 40 % | Plan dialysis access and/or a pre-emptive transplant; learn about each treatment | [KDIGO 2024][K24], 2.2.3 and 5.4.3; [KDOQI vascular access][Q19VA], 6.6 |
| Now, if you have Medicare | Up to 6 kidney disease education sessions; you pay 20 % after the Part B deductible | [Medicare][MED-KDE] |

## What changes now

**Learn about every option.** In-centre hemodialysis, home hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, a
transplant, and comprehensive conservative care (care without dialysis) are all real choices
([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 5.5.1–5.5.2). The best option for most people who can have one is
a **transplant from a living donor before dialysis is needed** ([KDIGO 2020 transplant][K20TX], 1.4).
With type 1 diabetes, ask about a **simultaneous pancreas-kidney (SPK) transplant**
([KDIGO 2020 transplant][K20TX], 8.1.1). Compare them in [Choosing a treatment](../prepare/choosing-a-treatment.md).

**Protect your arm veins.** A fistula for hemodialysis needs healthy veins. KDOQI advises avoiding
PICC lines and unnecessary needle sticks from stage 3 on, when dialysis may be needed in the future
([KDOQI vascular access][Q19VA], statement 6.10). Ask which arm may be used for an access, and ask
staff to use the other arm for blood tests and drips. See
[Dialysis access](../prepare/dialysis-access.md).

**Food.** Potassium and phosphorus limits are more often needed now. Cut **phosphate additives**
first: they are almost fully absorbed ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]). Keep sodium under 2,000 mg. Keep protein
at about 0.8 g/kg; going lower does not help glucose, the heart or kidney function
([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendation 11.3).

**Diabetes.** Insulin needs often fall as eGFR falls, and lows become more likely
([JBDS–Renal Association][JBDS]; [ADA–KDIGO 2022][AK22]). Your team, not you, changes the insulin
plan. **A1c becomes less reliable at G4–G5** ([KDIGO 2022][K22], practice point 2.1.2), so your team
may lean on CGM time in range instead (practice point 2.2.2). See [A1c](../labs/a1c.md) and
[CGM metrics](../labs/cgm-metrics.md).

## How people usually feel

Some people at G4 start to feel tired, lose their appetite or feel sick. KDIGO asks kidney teams to
ask about these symptoms at every visit ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.2.2.1). Itch, restless
legs and poor sleep are also worth raising ([Get help now](../get-help-now.md)). Unplanned weight loss is a reason to see a dietitian (practice points
5.2.3.2–5.2.3.3).

## What to do

- [ ] Ask for a transplant referral, and ask whether the centre does pancreas-kidney transplants.
- [ ] Ask family and friends whether anyone would consider being tested as a living donor.
- [ ] Book Medicare kidney disease education sessions if you have Medicare (ask your doctor for a referral).
- [ ] Ask your team which arm to save for an access. Tell every nurse and lab: "save my veins".
- [ ] Ask about your KFRE and when to plan an access.
- [ ] Ask for a renal dietitian.
- [ ] Ask your diabetes team how they will judge your glucose now: A1c, CGM time in range, or both.
- [ ] Keep glucose tablets and glucagon within reach: lows are more likely now.
- [ ] Set your stage to 4 in the app: the potassium review ceiling becomes 3,000 mg
      ([Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md)).

## Examples

**A blood test in the "wrong" arm.** Your team has said your left arm may be used for a fistula. A
nurse at another clinic wants blood from your left elbow. Say: "I have stage 4 kidney disease; please
use my right arm to save my veins" ([KDOQI vascular access][Q19VA]).

**Additives first.** One ounce of processed American cheese has about 180 mg of phosphorus
([USDA][FDC]), and processed cheese is made with phosphate additives ([NKF][DG21]). A 12 oz cola has about 33 mg by USDA figures, and about
62 mg when measured directly ([Di Iorio 2012][DG25b]). Swapping these for unprocessed cheese and a
clear soda is an easy first step.

**Salt substitutes.** A quarter teaspoon of "lite" salt has 350 mg of potassium ([Morton][DG35b]).
Use herbs, pepper, lemon or garlic instead.

**A planning conversation.** Your eGFR has gone from 28 to 22 in a year, and your 2-year KFRE is 15 %.
That is the point for multidisciplinary care ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 2.2.2). Ask about
transplant referral now, rather than waiting for eGFR 15.

## Ask your care team

1. What is my KFRE, and when should I plan for an access or a transplant?
2. Can you refer me for a transplant evaluation now? Does the centre do pancreas-kidney transplants?
3. Which treatment options would suit me? Can I meet people who use them?
4. Which arm should I protect, and when should I see a surgeon?
5. Do I need potassium or phosphorus limits, based on my last results?
6. How will we judge my glucose now that A1c is less reliable?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Chest pain; severe breathlessness or not being able to lie flat; fainting or a very slow or
    irregular pulse; new confusion or a seizure; a low the person cannot treat themselves (give
    glucagon, then call). Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    New or worse swelling, much less urine, vomiting or not eating, a lab potassium of 6.0–6.4 mmol/L,
    or lows that keep happening ([KDIGO 2024][K24]; [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).
