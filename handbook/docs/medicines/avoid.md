# Medicines and products to avoid

--8<-- "includes/no-dosing.md"

## In short

Some everyday medicines and products are risky with kidney disease. Always tell every prescriber and
pharmacist "I have kidney disease, stage X" before you take anything new, including things bought
without a prescription ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 4.1.1–4.1.3).

## From the diet guide

- **Sodium phosphate laxatives and enemas (Fleet-type):** FDA warns they can cause acute kidney injury
  from phosphate, dangerously high phosphate and death, especially with kidney disease, dehydration,
  diuretics, ACE inhibitors or ARBs, or NSAIDs. Avoid them with kidney disease ([FDA][FDANaP]).
- **Magnesium laxatives and antacids** (milk of magnesia, magnesium citrate, Maalox- or Mylanta-type
  antacids, Epsom salts) build up when the kidneys fail and can cause life-threatening high magnesium;
  **aluminium** antacids and **Alka-Seltzer-type** (high-sodium) products are also unsuitable
  ([case report][DG44]; [StatPearls][DG45]). Constipation itself raises potassium, so ask your team
  which laxative is right for you instead ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).
- **Salt substitutes** ("NoSalt", "Nu-Salt", "Lite Salt") are potassium chloride: NoSalt lists 640 mg
  potassium per ¼ teaspoon and Morton Lite Salt 350 mg potassium plus 290 mg sodium
  ([Morton Lite Salt label][DG35b]; [NKF][DG20]; [NKF][DG5]). See [Sodium](../eat/sodium.md).

## Ask your care team

1. Which pain reliever is safest for me?
2. What should I use for constipation?
3. Is there anything in my medicine cabinet I should throw away?

## Get help now if…

- After a laxative or enema: severe weakness, confusion, very slow breathing or fainting: call 911 (or
  your local emergency number). See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
