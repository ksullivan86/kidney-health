# eGFR and creatinine

## In short

**Creatinine** is a waste product your muscles make every day. Healthy kidneys filter it out, so when
kidney function falls, creatinine in the blood rises. **eGFR** (estimated glomerular filtration rate)
turns your creatinine, age and sex into an estimate of how much fluid your kidneys filter out of the
blood each minute ([NKF][NKF-CKDEPI]). NIDDK calls an eGFR of 60 or more the normal range and 15 or
less kidney failure ([NIDDK][NIDDK-tests]). Under 60 for 3 months or more means chronic kidney
disease, and under 15 is stage G5 ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). It is an estimate, so look at the trend over
several tests, and know what can fool it.

## Your numbers

=== "US units"

    | | Typical (your lab's range wins) |
    |---|---|
    | Creatinine, men | 0.7–1.3 mg/dL |
    | Creatinine, women | 0.5–0.95 mg/dL |
    | eGFR | 60 or more mL/min/1.73 m² is in the normal range |

=== "International"

    | | Typical (your lab's range wins) |
    |---|---|
    | Creatinine, men | 62–115 µmol/L |
    | Creatinine, women | 44–84 µmol/L |
    | eGFR | 60 or more mL/min/1.73 m² is in the normal range |

Creatinine ranges from [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CREAT]. To convert creatinine, multiply mg/dL by 88.4
to get µmol/L ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). eGFR uses the same unit everywhere.

| eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) | Stage | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 90 or more | G1 | normal filtering; CKD only if there is other damage, such as albumin in the urine |
| 60–89 | G2 | mildly lower |
| 45–59 | G3a | mildly to moderately lower |
| 30–44 | G3b | moderately to severely lower |
| 15–29 | G4 | severely lower |
| under 15 | G5 | kidney failure |

One result does not set your stage: the low value has to last 3 months or more
([KDIGO 2024][K24]). More on stages: [CKD stages](../ckd/stages.md).

**How often:** at least once a year with CKD, and 1–4 times a year depending on your stage
([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11], recommendation 11.1b).

## How eGFR is worked out

US labs use the **CKD-EPI 2021** equation. It uses creatinine, age and sex, and it no longer uses
race ([NKF][NKF-CKDEPI]; [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 1.2.4.2). Labs report it rounded and per
1.73 m² of body surface, and flag results under 60 as low ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 11).

**Why age and sex matter.** Creatinine comes from muscle, and muscle mass differs on average by age
and sex. The equation adjusts for that. Examples, worked with the CKD-EPI 2021 equation:

| Person | Creatinine | eGFR |
|---|---|---|
| Woman, 60 | 1.4 mg/dL (124 µmol/L) | about 43 (G3b) |
| Man, 60 | 1.4 mg/dL (124 µmol/L) | about 58 (G3a) |
| Woman, 30 | 1.0 mg/dL (88 µmol/L) | about 78 (G2) |
| Woman, 75 | 1.0 mg/dL (88 µmol/L) | about 59 (G3a) |

The same creatinine can mean different kidney function. That is why you should look at your eGFR,
not only your creatinine.

## When cystatin C is better

**Cystatin C** is another blood marker of filtering. It depends much less on muscle. KDIGO recommends
adding it (an "eGFRcr-cys") when the creatinine estimate is likely to be wrong and the answer matters
for treatment ([KDIGO 2024][K24], recommendation 1.2.2.1). Examples from KDIGO's list (Table 8):

- very muscular people, bodybuilders, or extreme exercise;
- an above-knee amputation, spinal cord injury, or muscle-wasting disease;
- eating disorders, malnutrition, or severe obesity (a BMI over about 35–40);
- unusual diets: very low or very high protein, vegetarian, keto, or **creatine supplements**;
- serious illness: cancer, heart failure, cirrhosis;
- some medicines, such as steroids, drugs that block how the kidney removes creatinine, or some
  broad-spectrum antibiotics.

Using creatinine and cystatin C together is more accurate than either one alone
([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 1.2.2.6).

## What can fool creatinine

| What happens | Effect | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A cooked meat or fish meal | In one study creatinine rose by about 0.23 mg/dL (20 µmol/L), peaking 2–4 hours later; in that group eGFR looked about 20 points lower | Skip a big meat or fish meal for about 12 hours before the test if you can ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 1.2.2.4) |
| Creatine supplements or a very high-protein diet | creatinine reads higher, eGFR lower | Tell your team; ask about cystatin C ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 8) |
| A lot of muscle, or very little | high muscle: eGFR looks too low; low muscle or amputation: eGFR looks too high | Ask about cystatin C ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 8) |
| Some medicines, such as trimethoprim or cimetidine | can change creatinine without changing filtering | Tell the team every medicine you take ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CREAT]) |
| A sudden illness (vomiting, dehydration) | eGFR lags behind real changes in kidney function | Tell your team; the test is often repeated when you are well ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 9) |
| Starting an ACE inhibitor, ARB, finerenone or SGLT2 inhibitor | an expected small dip, because these medicines lower the pressure inside the filters to protect them | See the next section |

## The expected dip with kidney-protecting medicines

ACE inhibitors, ARBs, finerenone and SGLT2 inhibitors often lower eGFR a little in the first weeks.
This is expected and is part of how they protect the kidneys. (With type 1 diabetes, SGLT2 inhibitors
are not approved for glucose control and markedly raise the risk of DKA ([Farxiga label][FARXIGA]);
see [Kidney-protecting medicines](../t1d/kidney-protecting-medicines.md#sglt2-inhibitors-not-for-type-1-without-a-plan).)

- KDIGO: keep taking an ACE inhibitor or ARB unless creatinine rises by **more than 30 %** within
  4 weeks of starting or a dose increase. After starting any of these medicines, an eGFR fall of more
  than 30 % is more than expected and is looked into. Blood pressure, creatinine and potassium are
  checked 2–4 weeks after a start or a dose change ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 2.1.4, 3.6.2,
  3.6.4).
- ADA: a creatinine rise of up to 30 % on these medicines "must not be confused with" acute kidney
  injury ([ADA 2026 §11][A26-11]).
- Finerenone: the label describes a small eGFR drop (on average 2–3 mL/min) in the first 4 weeks
  that then levels off ([Kerendia label][KER]).

**Never stop one of these medicines on your own because of a dip.** Ask your team whether the change
is in the expected range.

## What to do

- [ ] Learn your latest eGFR and the one before it, and the change in per cent.
- [ ] Before the test, skip a big meat or fish meal for about 12 hours if you can, and do not take
      creatine supplements.
- [ ] Tell your team about big changes in exercise, weight, diet or muscle mass. Ask whether cystatin C
      would help.
- [ ] Tell every doctor, dentist and pharmacist your eGFR: many medicines are dosed by kidney function
      ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 4.2.1).
- [ ] After starting or increasing a kidney-protecting medicine, go for the blood test 2–4 weeks later.
- [ ] Track the trend on the [lab tracker](index.md#a-lab-tracker-to-print).

!!! tip "In the app"
    Enter a creatinine (mg/dL or µmol/L), a cystatin C, or the eGFR printed on your report under
    **Labs**. The app works out CKD-EPI 2021 and **suggests** a G stage, but never changes the stage
    saved in your profile: one result is not enough ([design note 05][NOTE05]). It needs your age
    (18 or over). If your sex is not set, it shows both the female and the male result. It does not
    calculate eGFR in pregnancy, because the equations were not built for it.

## Examples

- **Same number, different meaning.** Maria and her brother are both 60 and both have a creatinine of
  1.4 mg/dL. Her eGFR is about 43 (G3b); his is about 58 (G3a).
- **Starting an ACE inhibitor.** Sam's eGFR was 50. Three weeks after starting an ACE inhibitor it is
  44, a 12 % dip: expected, and the medicine continues. If it had fallen to 33 (a 34 % drop), his team
  would review it ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 2.1.4 and 3.6.4).
- **The steak dinner.** Lee had a large steak the night before an early blood test, and his eGFR came
  back lower than usual. His team repeated it after a light evening meal, and it was back to his
  normal level.
- **On dialysis.** eGFR is not used to judge dialysis. Your unit checks how well dialysis works with
  other tests ([KDOQI 2015][Q15HD]; [Dialysis adequacy](dialysis-adequacy.md)).

## Ask your care team

1. What is my eGFR trend over the last year, in per cent?
2. Is creatinine a good guide for me, or should we add cystatin C?
3. When should my next blood test be after this medicine change?
4. Are any of my medicines dosed by my eGFR?
5. Is my eGFR low enough that we should talk about kidney failure treatment options?

## Get help now if…

eGFR on its own is rarely urgent, but a fast fall can come with warning signs.

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Severe breathlessness, being unable to lie flat, chest pain, new confusion, or a lab potassium of
    6.5 mmol/L or more ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28). See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    Much less urine than usual, new swelling, or vomiting or diarrhea so you cannot keep fluids down
    (start your [sick-day plan](../t1d/sick-days.md)). Also call if a result shows eGFR fell by more
    than 20 % with no clear reason ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 2.1.3).
