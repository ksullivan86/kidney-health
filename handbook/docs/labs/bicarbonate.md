# Bicarbonate

## In short

Bicarbonate is the body's main buffer against acid. On a lab report it may be called **CO2, total
CO2, TCO2 or HCO3** ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CO2]). Healthy kidneys remove acid every day. As kidney
function falls, acid builds up and bicarbonate drops, mostly from stage G4 on ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). A
low bicarbonate is linked with muscle loss and other problems, so your team may treat it with tablets
or, if your potassium allows, more fruit and vegetables. **A low bicarbonate on its own is not DKA.**
If you are ill, your **ketone** reading is the number that tells you about DKA.

## Your numbers

Bicarbonate is the same number in mmol/L and mEq/L, so there is one table for both unit systems.

| Result (mmol/L = mEq/L) | What it means | Source |
|---|---|---|
| about 22–29 | the usual normal range (MedlinePlus lists 23–29; your lab's range wins) | [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CO2] |
| 24–26 | KDOQI says it is "reasonable" to keep bicarbonate here at stages 3–5 and on dialysis (expert opinion) | [KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 6.1.3 |
| under 22 | low: common as kidney function falls | [KDIGO 2024][K24] |
| **under 18** | KDIGO says treatment should be considered | [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.10.1 |
| above your lab's upper limit while on treatment | too much; treatment should not push it above normal | [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.10.2 |

**How common.** Among people with diabetes, a bicarbonate under 22 was found in about 8 % at stage G3
with little urine albumin, rising to 38 % at stage G5 with a lot ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).

**How often.** It is most often part of an electrolyte or basic metabolic panel, the same blood test
that reports potassium and creatinine ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CO2]). Ask for the number if you do not
see it on your report. On dialysis, blood is taken before the session ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]).

**Age and sex.** Average bicarbonate differs only slightly by age and sex ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 23).
The treatment thresholds are the same for everyone.

## Why it matters

Long-term acid build-up is linked with breakdown of protein and muscle, inflammation, and heart and
other problems ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). It also pushes potassium out of cells, so it can raise your
potassium ([Blood potassium](blood-potassium.md)). The evidence that treatment changes long-term
outcomes is limited: in the BiCARB trial (people aged 60 or over at stages G3–G4 with bicarbonate under
22), bicarbonate tablets did not improve kidney or other outcomes and cost more ([KDIGO 2024][K24]).
That is why KDIGO frames treatment as preventing more severe acidosis, with 18 as the level to avoid.

## How it is treated

Your team chooses the treatment. The options are:

- **Fruit and vegetables.** At stages G1–G4 without dialysis, KDOQI suggests more fruit and vegetables
  to lower the acid load ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 6.1.1). Small trials found plant-rich diets
  worked about as well as bicarbonate tablets ([KDIGO 2024][K24]). **Only if your potassium allows:**
  fruit and vegetables add potassium, so at stage G5 and on dialysis this is not the usual advice
  ([design note 05][NOTE05]).
- **Tablets.** KDOQI recommends lowering acid with bicarbonate supplements at stages 3–5 and on
  dialysis ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 6.1.2). Sodium bicarbonate contains sodium, so your team
  watches your blood pressure, swelling and potassium ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.10.2).
  This handbook does not give doses; your prescriber sets them.

## A low bicarbonate is not DKA by itself

Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is diagnosed when three things happen together ([2024 consensus][HC24]):

1. high glucose (200 mg/dL, 11.1 mmol/L, or more) or known diabetes;
2. ketones: blood ketones **3.0 mmol/L or more** (or urine ketones 2+ or more);
3. acid in the blood: pH under 7.3 and/or bicarbonate under 18 mmol/L.

With advanced kidney disease your bicarbonate may already be under 18 because of the kidneys. So the
bicarbonate on a routine report cannot tell you whether you have DKA, and a low result does not mean
you have it. When you are ill, have high glucose or missed insulin, **check your ketones**: they are
the deciding number. Blood ketones of 1.6–2.9 mmol/L mean call your diabetes team now; 3.0 or more
means emergency care ([NHS][NHS-DKA]; [Sick days](../t1d/sick-days.md)). A low bicarbonate does leave
less buffer: ketoacidosis and diarrhoea both lower bicarbonate further ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CO2]).

## What to do

- [ ] Find "CO2" or "bicarbonate" on your report and write it on your [lab tracker](index.md#a-lab-tracker-to-print).
- [ ] If it is under 22, ask whether you need treatment and which kind.
- [ ] Before adding fruit and vegetables, check your potassium and ask your team or dietitian
      ([Potassium in food](../eat/potassium.md)).
- [ ] If you take bicarbonate tablets, tell your team about new swelling or higher blood pressure
      readings.
- [ ] Keep blood ketone strips at home and use them when you are ill, whatever your bicarbonate.

!!! tip "In the app"
    Add a bicarbonate result under **Labs**. Under 22 the app shows a note that acid builds up as
    kidneys fail; it mentions fruit and vegetables only at stages 1–4 without dialysis, and asks you to
    check with your team first if your potassium is high. Under 18 it says KDIGO suggests considering
    treatment. Bicarbonate does not change your food targets ([design note 05][NOTE05]).

## Examples

- **Stage G4, bicarbonate 19, potassium 4.6.** Mia's team suggests more fruit and vegetables within her
  potassium budget and rechecks in 3 months.
- **Stage G4, bicarbonate 17, potassium 5.4.** More fruit would add potassium, so Leo's team starts
  bicarbonate tablets and watches his blood pressure and swelling.
- **On hemodialysis, bicarbonate 20, vomiting since last night.** Nora's routine bicarbonate is often
  around 20, so it tells her nothing new. She checks blood ketones: 2.1 mmol/L. She calls her diabetes
  team straight away and follows her sick-day plan.

## Ask your care team

1. What is my bicarbonate (CO2), and is it low for me?
2. Do I need treatment? Food, tablets or both?
3. Is it safe for my potassium to eat more fruit and vegetables?
4. If I take bicarbonate tablets, what should I watch for?
5. How should I check for DKA when I am ill, given my kidney disease?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    Blood ketones **3.0 mmol/L or more**, or ketones with vomiting so you cannot keep fluids down, deep
    or fast breathing, fruity-smelling breath or confusion ([NHS][NHS-DKA]; [2024 consensus][HC24]).
    See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    Blood ketones **1.6–2.9 mmol/L** (call now, not later in the day), vomiting or diarrhoea, or new
    swelling or breathlessness after starting bicarbonate tablets ([NHS][NHS-DKA];
    [KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.10.2).
