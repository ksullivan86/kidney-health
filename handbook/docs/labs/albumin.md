# Serum albumin

## In short

Albumin is a protein in your blood. It carries calcium and other substances, and it helps keep fluid
in your blood from leaking into the tissues ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-ALB]). On dialysis, a **low albumin** goes with a higher risk of hospital stays and death
([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 1.2.2). But it is **not a nutrition test on its own**: infection and
inflammation, protein lost in the urine, liver disease and other things lower it too
([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 1.2.1; [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-ALB]). **Serum albumin is a blood test.
It is not the same as urine albumin**, which is the [UACR](uacr.md) test of kidney damage.

## Your numbers

=== "US units"

    | | Value |
    |---|---|
    | Typical range | 3.4–5.4 g/dL (your lab's range wins) |
    | Kidney nutrition-risk cut-off | under 3.8 g/dL, measured by the bromocresol green method |

=== "International"

    | | Value |
    |---|---|
    | Typical range | 34–54 g/L (your lab's range wins) |
    | Kidney nutrition-risk cut-off | under 38 g/L, measured by the bromocresol green method |

Range from [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-ALB]. The 3.8 g/dL cut-off is one of the criteria for
protein-energy wasting, a kind of malnutrition seen in kidney disease ([ISRNM 2008][ISRNM08]).
To convert, multiply g/dL by 10 to get g/L.

**Lab methods differ.** Labs measure albumin in different ways. On hemodialysis the bromocresol green
method tends to read high and the bromocresol purple method low. In a Dutch study of about 500 people on
hemodialysis, centers using the purple method reported an average albumin of 34.5 g/L and centers using
the green method 40.3 g/L ([de Roij van Zuijdewijn 2018][ALB18]). So a result that counts as "low" at
one lab may count as "normal" at another. Ask which method your lab uses, and compare results from the
same lab.

**How often.** US dialysis units must measure albumin and weight at least once a month
([42 CFR 494.90][CFR494]). Before dialysis, ask your team how often you need it.

## What lowers albumin

| Cause | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Not eating enough protein or calories | A dietitian can help; see [Eating enough](../eat/eating-enough.md) and [Protein](../eat/protein.md) |
| Infection or inflammation | Albumin falls with illness and inflammation whatever you eat, so it is best rechecked when you are well ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 1.2.1 and its rationale) |
| Protein lost in the urine (UACR in the A3 range) or into peritoneal dialysis fluid | Low albumin is common with heavy urine protein and on peritoneal dialysis ([KDIGO 2022][K22]; [KDOQI 2020][Q20]) |
| Liver disease, or gut conditions that block absorption | ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-ALB]) |

A result can read **high** with dehydration, or if the tourniquet was left on too long during the blood
draw ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-ALB]).

**Albumin changes other results.** About half of the calcium in blood is carried on albumin, so a low
albumin makes total calcium look low ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CA]; [Phosphate, calcium and PTH](phosphate-calcium-pth.md)).
Low albumin can also throw off glycated albumin and fructosamine, two glucose tests sometimes used
instead of A1c ([KDIGO 2022][K22]; [A1c](a1c.md)).

## Other signs of poor nutrition

Because albumin alone is not enough, teams look at several things together ([KDOQI 2020][Q20],
statement 1.2.1):

- **Weight loss you did not plan**: more than 5 % in 3 months or more than 10 % in 6 months
  ([ISRNM 2008][ISRNM08]). For a 70 kg person, 5 % is 3.5 kg (about 8 lb).
- A poor appetite, eating less than usual, or muscles getting thinner and weaker.
- Grip strength, when there are earlier results to compare ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 1.3.1).

## What to do

- [ ] Weigh yourself once a week at the same time (after dialysis if you are on hemodialysis) and
      write it down.
- [ ] If your albumin is low or your weight is falling, ask to see a renal dietitian.
- [ ] Ask whether an **oral nutrition supplement** (a renal nutrition drink) would help. KDOQI suggests
      at least a 3-month trial when diet advice alone is not enough ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 4.1.1).
- [ ] On dialysis, eat enough protein: more is needed, not less ([Protein](../eat/protein.md)).
- [ ] With type 1 diabetes, eating less with the same insulin can cause lows. Tell your diabetes team
      when your appetite drops ([Eating enough](../eat/eating-enough.md); [Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md)).
- [ ] Get infections treated, and recheck albumin when you are well.

!!! tip "In the app"
    Add an albumin result under **Labs**. Under 3.8 g/dL the app marks a nutrition risk and moves your
    suggested protein and calories to the higher end, with a note that low albumin can also come from
    inflammation or urine protein, and that labs measure it in different ways ([design note 05][NOTE05]).
    See [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md).

## Examples

- **After pneumonia.** On hemodialysis, Ruth's albumin fell from 3.9 to 3.3 g/dL after a chest
  infection. Her appetite is coming back. Her dietitian adds a renal nutrition drink and rechecks next
  month.
- **Protein in the urine.** Omar's albumin is 3.4 g/dL and his UACR is 2,500 mg/g. Much of his
  albumin is being lost in his urine, so his kidney team focuses on lowering the UACR
  ([UACR](uacr.md)).
- **Two labs.** Sara's albumin was 3.6 at a hospital lab and 4.1 at her dialysis unit in the same month.
  Different methods can explain a gap like this; her team compares results from one lab only.

## Ask your care team

1. Is my albumin low for my lab's method?
2. Could inflammation, an infection or urine protein be the cause?
3. Am I eating enough protein and calories? Can I see a renal dietitian?
4. Would a nutrition drink help? Which kind fits my potassium, phosphorus and fluid limits?
5. Is my weight change something to worry about?

## Get help now if…

A low albumin is not an emergency on its own.

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    You cannot keep liquids down for more than 4 hours, or you have ketones with vomiting
    ([CDC][CDC-SICK]; [Sick days](../t1d/sick-days.md)); or you are very short of breath or cannot lie
    flat. See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    You are eating much less than usual, you have vomiting or diarrhea (check your ketones and start
    your [sick-day plan](../t1d/sick-days.md)), or you have new swelling or sudden weight gain.
