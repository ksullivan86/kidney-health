# Blood potassium

## In short

Potassium keeps your heart beating in rhythm and your muscles working. The kidneys remove the extra, so
as kidney function falls, blood potassium can rise. A very high level can cause a dangerous heart
rhythm, **often with no warning symptoms** ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-K]). Food is only one cause:
medicines, high glucose or missed insulin, constipation and acid build-up often matter more
([KDIGO 2024][K24]). A level that is too **low** is also risky. Know your last result, and know the two
numbers that mean "call today" (6.0) and "go now" (6.5).

## Your numbers

Potassium is the same number in mmol/L and mEq/L, so there is one table for both unit systems.

| Result (mmol/L = mEq/L) | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| under 3.5 | low | Call your team soon. Do not cut potassium foods unless told to |
| about 3.5–5.1 | the usual normal range (labs vary a little; your lab's range wins) | Keep doing what you do. If you have a potassium limit, keep it until your team changes it; if you have none, you do not need one while it stays normal |
| 5.2–5.5 | above normal | Your team looks for the cause, may repeat the test and review food and medicines |
| 5.6–5.9 | high | Tell your team promptly. KDIGO lists steps for any potassium over 5.5 (below) |
| **6.0–6.4** | **moderate hyperkalaemia** (KDIGO) | **Call your team today.** If you feel unwell, get checked in hospital now; otherwise repeat the test within 24 hours |
| **6.5 or more** | **severe hyperkalaemia** (KDIGO) | **Emergency: call 911 or go to the emergency department now** |

Normal range from [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-LOWK]; action levels from [KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28 and
Figure 32. KDIGO counts more than 5.0 as high when it describes how common high potassium is.

**How often:** with each kidney check, and 2–4 weeks after starting or increasing an ACE inhibitor or
ARB ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.6.2). Finerenone is not started if potassium is over
5.0 mEq/L, and potassium is checked 4 weeks after starting it ([Kerendia label][KER]).

## How high potassium feels

Often it feels like nothing. When there are symptoms, they can include weakness, nausea, palpitations
(a fluttering or uneven heartbeat), a slow, weak or irregular pulse, chest pain, trouble breathing, or
sudden collapse ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-K]). That is why the **lab number** matters, not how you feel.

## Causes besides food

| Cause | Why it raises potassium |
|---|---|
| **Medicines:** ACE inhibitors, ARBs, spironolactone, eplerenone, finerenone, beta-blockers, digoxin, heparin, amiloride, triamterene, NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen), tacrolimus, cyclosporine, trimethoprim | they reduce how much potassium the kidney can remove ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 26). SGLT2 inhibitors do not seem to raise it |
| **Not enough insulin, or high glucose** | insulin moves potassium into cells; high glucose pulls water and potassium out of them ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 25; [Sousa 2016][DG36]) |
| **Acid build-up** (low bicarbonate) | potassium shifts out of cells ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 25; [Bicarbonate](bicarbonate.md)) |
| **Constipation** | with advanced CKD the gut removes more of the potassium, so slow bowels keep more in ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 25) |
| **The long gap between hemodialysis sessions** | the day after the 2-day gap has more heart-rhythm hospital stays (20.9 vs 11.0 per 100 patient-years) and more deaths ([Foley 2011][DG53]) |
| **Food**, especially potassium additives and salt substitutes | additive potassium is absorbed best ([Potassium in food](../eat/potassium.md)) |

## False highs

Sometimes the blood in the tube is high but the blood in your body is not. This is called
pseudohyperkalaemia. Causes include a tight tourniquet, **clenching or pumping your fist** during the
draw, red cells breaking open (a "haemolysed" sample) after a hard draw, rough handling or a delay
before the sample is processed, and very high platelet or white-cell counts. Serum results also run a
little higher than plasma results ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 25). Potassium also varies by 0.24–0.73
mmol/L over a day. A surprise result is usually repeated, but **do not assume a high result is false**:
call as the table says.

## Low potassium

Below about 3.5 mmol/L is low. Causes include water pills (diuretics), vomiting or diarrhoea, and low
magnesium. Symptoms can be weakness, cramps, constipation and palpitations; a large drop can cause
dangerous heart rhythms ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-LOWK]). People on **peritoneal dialysis** sometimes run
low and need more potassium, not less ([DaVita][DG25a]). Both low and high potassium are linked with a
higher risk of death ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 31).

## What helps

KDIGO sets out the steps for potassium over 5.5 mmol/L in this order ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Figure 32):

1. **Fix what can be fixed.** Review medicines such as NSAIDs and trimethoprim. Look at food with a
   renal dietitian, especially processed foods and salt substitutes ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice
   points 3.11.5.1–3.11.5.2).
2. **Medicines that lower potassium**, chosen by your team: a water pill, treatment for low
   bicarbonate, or a potassium binder.
3. **Last resort:** lowering or stopping an ACE inhibitor, ARB or finerenone. Stopping them is linked
   with more heart problems, so the team tries to restart them later.

High potassium on an ACE inhibitor or ARB "can often be managed" by lowering potassium rather than
stopping the medicine ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 3.6.3).

## What to do

- [ ] Know your last potassium result and its date. Write it on your [lab tracker](index.md#a-lab-tracker-to-print).
- [ ] Ask whether you need a potassium limit at all. Limit potassium only if your blood level is high
      and your team says so ([Potassium in food](../eat/potassium.md)).
- [ ] At the blood draw, keep your hand relaxed and do not pump your fist.
- [ ] Keep a list of the medicines you take that raise potassium. Show it to every prescriber.
- [ ] Avoid NSAIDs such as ibuprofen and naproxen unless your kidney team says otherwise
      ([Medicines to avoid](../medicines/avoid.md)).
- [ ] Keep your bowels moving: fibre from low-potassium foods, fluid within your limit, and ask about a
      laxative if needed.
- [ ] Keep glucose in range and never skip your basal (background) insulin. When you are ill, follow
      your [sick-day plan](../t1d/sick-days.md).
- [ ] On hemodialysis, never skip or shorten a session, and take extra care with potassium over the long
      gap ([Dialysis days](../eat/dialysis-days.md)).
- [ ] **Never delay treating a low because of potassium.** Treat it first
      ([Treating a low](../t1d/treating-a-low.md)).

!!! tip "In the app"
    Add a potassium result under **Labs**. At 6.0 mmol/L or more the app shows a red alert with the
    same advice as the table above, even if you never open "Suggest targets". A fresh result also
    changes the app's potassium review ceiling: normal relaxes it one step, high tightens it, and low
    removes it ([design note 05][NOTE05]). See [Targets and warnings](../app/targets-and-warnings.md).

## Examples

- **A new medicine.** Three weeks after starting an ACE inhibitor, Rosa's potassium is 5.4. Her team
  looks at her food diary, finds a salt substitute and two glasses of orange juice a day, and swaps
  them. The ACE inhibitor stays, and the repeat is 4.9.
- **A haemolysed sample.** Tom's result is 6.1 and the report says "haemolysed". He calls his team the
  same day. He feels well, so they repeat it within 24 hours: 4.8.
- **An emergency.** On Monday morning, after the weekend gap, Jean feels weak and her pulse is slow
  and uneven. Her family calls 911. Her potassium is 7.0.
- **Running low.** On peritoneal dialysis, Kai's potassium is 3.2. His team adds potassium-rich foods
  rather than limiting them.

## Ask your care team

1. What was my last potassium, and do I need a limit? What number?
2. Which of my medicines raise potassium?
3. Should I take a potassium binder? How does it fit around meals and other medicines?
4. Could constipation, high glucose or low bicarbonate be part of the cause?
5. What should I do if my result is high but I feel fine?
6. Who do I call after hours about a lab result?

## Get help now if…

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    A lab or your team tells you your potassium is **6.5 mmol/L or more**, or you have fainting, a very
    slow, weak or irregular pulse, chest pain or sudden severe weakness, especially if your potassium
    has been high ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28; [MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-K]). Do not drive yourself.

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    A potassium of **6.0–6.4 mmol/L**: if you feel unwell, get checked and treated in hospital; if you
    feel well, the test should be repeated within 24 hours ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28). Also call for
    vomiting or diarrhoea that stops you eating or drinking. Full list: [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).
