# Phosphate, calcium and PTH

## In short

These three blood tests watch your **bones and blood vessels**. As the kidneys fail, phosphate builds
up in the blood. The body answers by raising **PTH** (parathyroid hormone), which pulls calcium out of
your bones and makes them weak. High phosphate and calcium can also form hard deposits in blood
vessels, the heart, lungs and eyes ([NKF][DG20]). Doctors call this CKD-MBD (mineral and bone
disorder). KDIGO recommends checking these tests from stage G3a on ([KDIGO 2017][K17],
recommendation 3.1.1). The biggest thing you can do is cut **phosphate additives**,
and take any binder **with** food.

Sources: phosphate ([NKF][DG20]); calcium ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CA]); PTH ([KDIGO 2017][K17],
recommendations 4.2.1 and 4.2.3). PTH is reported in pg/mL or pmol/L, and each lab's test has its own
range, so always compare with the range on your own report.

**Working out "2–9 times".** If your lab's upper limit were 65 pg/mL, 2–9 times would be about
130–585 pg/mL. Big changes inside that range also prompt a change in treatment ([KDIGO 2017][K17],
recommendation 4.2.3).

**How often** ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 3.1.2):

| Stage | Calcium and phosphate | PTH | Alkaline phosphatase |
|---|---|---|---|
| G3a–G3b | every 6–12 months | depends on your first result | – |
| G4 | every 3–6 months | every 6–12 months | every 12 months |
| G5 and dialysis | every 1–3 months | every 3–6 months | every 12 months, or more often if PTH is high |

More often if a result is off or treatment changes. Vitamin D (25-hydroxy vitamin D) may also be
checked ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 3.1.3). On dialysis, blood for these tests is taken before
the session ([KDOQI 2020][Q20]).

## Why they matter

- **Phosphate.** Over months, high phosphate drives PTH up and calcium out of bone, and helps calcium deposit in blood vessels ([NKF][DG20]). People with these deposits are
  at the highest risk of heart disease ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 3.3.2).
- **Calcium.** KDIGO suggests avoiding high calcium in adults ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation
  4.1.3). Calcium comes from food, from calcium-based binders and supplements, and from vitamin D
  medicines, so all of these count. About half of blood calcium rides on albumin, so a low albumin makes
  total calcium look low ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-CA]). The albumin test method matters too: on
  hemodialysis, one common method read about 6 g/L lower than another, which changes "corrected"
  calcium ([de Roij van Zuijdewijn 2018][ALB18]; [Serum albumin](albumin.md)).
- **PTH.** If PTH keeps rising, your team first looks for causes they can fix: high phosphate, low
  calcium, a high phosphate intake, and low vitamin D ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 4.2.1). On
  dialysis, PTH-lowering medicines may be added; surgery is for severe cases that do not respond
  ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendations 4.2.4–4.2.5).
- **After a transplant**, phosphate is often **low** in the first months, and the advice can be to eat
  more phosphorus, not less ([KDOQI 2020][Q20], statement 6.3.3).

## What you can do

- [ ] **Cut phosphate additives first.** Look for "PHOS" in the ingredient list (for example
      phosphoric acid or sodium phosphate). Up to all of the phosphorus in additives is absorbed,
      compared with 40–60 % from meat and less from plants, so this is the biggest lever
      ([Noori 2010][DG13a]; [KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 4.1.8;
      [Phosphate additives](../eat/phosphate-additives.md)).
- [ ] **Take binders with food, every time you eat**, meals and snacks, exactly as prescribed. A binder
      only works on phosphate that is in your gut at the same time ([NKF][DG20]).
- [ ] Ask before taking **calcium or vitamin D supplements**: they add to your calcium
      ([Supplements](../medicines/supplements.md)).
- [ ] On dialysis, **do every session in full**: dialysis removes phosphate, and longer or extra
      dialysis is one way to treat stubborn high phosphate ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 4.1.9).
- [ ] Keep protein up on dialysis by choosing foods with little phosphorus per gram of protein, such as
      egg whites and fresh meat or fish ([Noori 2010][DG13a]; [Phosphorus](../eat/phosphorus.md);
      [Protein](../eat/protein.md)).
- [ ] Track the **trend**. Treatment decisions are based on several results, not one
      ([KDIGO 2017][K17], recommendation 3.1.4).

!!! tip "In the app"
    Add a phosphate result under **Labs** (mg/dL or mmol/L). Above 4.5 mg/dL the app sets your
    phosphorus target to 800 mg a day, the low end of the guideline range; with a normal result it keeps
    1,000 mg as a review ceiling; with a low result it sets no limit ([design note 05][NOTE05];

## Examples

- **The hidden additives.** On hemodialysis, Dee's phosphate is 6.1 mg/dL (1.97 mmol/L). Her dietitian
  finds processed cheese slices, deli ham and a dark cola each day, all with phosphate additives.
  Fresh chicken, home-made sandwiches and a clear soda replace them.
- **The skipped binder.** Ray takes his binder with breakfast and dinner, but not with the large lunch
  he eats at work. The binder cannot work on a meal it never meets. Taking it with lunch fixes the gap.
- **High calcium.** Pat's calcium is 10.6 mg/dL. Her team checks her calcium-based binder, her calcium
  supplement and her vitamin D medicine before changing anything.
- **Rising PTH.** Before dialysis, Sam's PTH rises over three tests. His team checks phosphate, calcium
  and vitamin D first.

## Ask your care team

1. What are my phosphate, calcium and PTH, and which way are they heading?
2. Do I need a phosphate binder? Which meals and snacks do I take it with?
3. Is my binder calcium-based? Should I count it in my calcium?
4. Should I take vitamin D or calcium supplements, or stop any I take?
5. Is my vitamin D level checked?
6. Can I see a renal dietitian about phosphate additives?

## Get help now if…

These tests are rarely an emergency on their own.

!!! danger "Call 911 (or your local emergency number)"
    New confusion, a seizure, chest pain or fainting. See [Get help now](../get-help-now.md).

!!! warning "Call your care team today"
    If the lab or your team phones you about a very high or very low calcium or phosphate result,
    follow their instructions that day. Ask before you change any binder or vitamin D medicine.
