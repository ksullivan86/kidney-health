# Get help now

!!! danger "In an emergency, call 911 now"
    In the US and Canada call **911**. Elsewhere call **112** (across the EU), **999** (UK) or **000**
    (Australia). If you are not sure whether it is an emergency, call. While you wait, **treat a low
    blood glucose straight away**: potassium never delays treating a low
    ([Treating a low](t1d/treating-a-low.md)).

## In short

This page sorts warning signs into three groups: **call 911 now**, **call your care team today**, and
**mention at your next visit**. Kidney disease and type 1 diabetes share a few dangers that can turn
serious within hours: very high potassium, a severe low, diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), fluid on the
lungs and serious infection. Print the [wallet card](#printable-wallet-card) and write in your own
numbers. If your team has given you a written plan that differs from this page, follow your team's plan.

## Call 911 now

Call 911 (or your local emergency number) for any of these. Do not drive yourself.

- **Chest pain or pressure.** Also call for sudden breathlessness or fainting with no clear cause: in
  people with kidney disease, heart trouble often shows up that way instead of as classic chest pain
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], section 3.15).
- **Severe breathlessness**, being unable to lie flat, gurgling breathing, or coughing up pink or
  bloody froth. These are signs of fluid on the lungs, and kidney failure is one cause
  ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-PE]).
- **Fainting or collapse, a very slow, weak or irregular pulse, or sudden severe weakness**, especially if
  your potassium has been high ([MedlinePlus][MEDLINE-K]). If a lab or your team tells you your
  **potassium is 6.5 mmol/L or more**, go now: KDIGO calls this "severe" and says to "take immediate
  action to assess and treat" ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28).
- **A low the person cannot treat themselves:** they cannot swallow safely, are very drowsy or
  confused, or are having a seizure. Give **glucagon** if you have it, turn them on their side (they may
  vomit as they wake), and call 911. Give food or drink only once they are awake and able to swallow
  ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]; [ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]; [glucagon instructions][GLUCAGON-KIT]).
- **Signs of a stroke:** the face droops on one side, an arm or leg is weak or numb, speech is slurred
  or hard to understand, or there is sudden trouble seeing or walking or a sudden severe headache.
  Note the time the signs started ([CDC][CDC-STROKE]).
- **New confusion, a seizure, or someone you cannot wake up.** Check glucose if you can and treat a
  low first ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]). In kidney failure, confusion can also come from waste building up
  in the blood, which KDIGO lists as a reason to start dialysis ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 41).
- **Possible DKA:** blood ketones **3.0 mmol/L or more** (urine ketones 2+ or more); or any ketones with
  vomiting so you cannot keep fluids down, deep or fast breathing, fruity-smelling breath or
  confusion; or glucose that stays at 300 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L) or above ([NHS][NHS-DKA];
  [CDC][CDC-DKA]; [2024 consensus][HC24]). People with kidney failure often have **lower ketone
  numbers** in DKA than other people, so symptoms count even if the meter reads under 3.0
  ([2024 consensus][HC24], Table 4).
- **Bleeding from a fistula or graft** that is heavy or spurting, or has not stopped after 10–15 minutes
  of firm pressure. Press hard on gauze with two fingers and keep pressing while you call
  ([AKF][AKF-bleed]; [NKF][NKF-HDaccess]).
- **Signs of a serious infection (sepsis):** fever, shivering or feeling very cold, together with
  confusion, a fast heartbeat or weak pulse, shortness of breath, clammy skin or extreme pain. Sepsis
  is a life-threatening emergency ([CDC][CDC-SEPSIS]). Take it especially seriously after a transplant
  ([NIDDK][NIDDK-Tx]).
- **Thoughts of harming yourself:** call or text **988** in the US ([988 Lifeline][988]). If you are in
  immediate danger, call 911.

## Call your care team today

Use the number your team gave you, including the after-hours line. If you cannot reach anyone and you
feel worse, go to urgent care or the emergency department.

**Blood tests**

- A lab **potassium of 6.0–6.4 mmol/L**. KDIGO says: if you feel unwell, get checked and treated in
  hospital; if you feel well and the result was unexpected, repeat the test within 24 hours
  ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 28). See [Blood potassium](labs/blood-potassium.md).

**Diabetes**

- **Blood ketones 1.6–2.9 mmol/L:** call your diabetes team **now**, not later in the day
  ([NHS][NHS-DKA]). If you cannot reach them quickly, or you are vomiting, go to the emergency
  department: the American Diabetes Association advises emergency care at 1.6 mmol/L or more
  ([ADA][ADAK]).
- **Blood ketones 0.6–1.5 mmol/L:** follow your sick-day plan and test again in 2 hours. If you are
  also ill, call your team ([NHS][NHS-DKA]).
- **Check ketones** whenever you are ill, have missed insulin or have symptoms, especially if glucose is
  above 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).
- **Vomiting, diarrhea or not eating:** start your written sick-day plan and call. Ask which of your
  medicines to pause and exactly when to restart them ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice points 4.3.2–4.3.3;
  [Sick days](t1d/sick-days.md)).
- **Lows that keep coming back**, lows at night, or lows you no longer feel coming
  ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).

**Dialysis and transplant**

- **On peritoneal dialysis: cloudy fluid, belly pain, fever, or redness or pain around the catheter.**
  Call your PD unit the same day and keep the bag, so the fluid can be tested ([NIDDK][NIDDK-PD];
  [ISPD 2022][I22P]).
- **Fistula or graft: the buzz (thrill) stops, feels much weaker or changes.** Call right away; do not
  wait for your next session ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]).
- **Redness, warmth, swelling, pain or pus** at an access site or a catheter exit site
  ([NKF][NKF-HDaccess]; [NIDDK][NIDDK-PD]).
- **A missed or shortened dialysis session:** call the unit to make it up
  ([KDOQI hemodialysis adequacy][Q15HD]). Risk of death and hospital stays is highest after the long
  two-day gap in a three-times-a-week schedule ([Foley 2011][DG53]).
- **After a transplant: a temperature over 100 °F (37.8 °C)**, feeling unwell, drainage from the scar,
  burning when you pass urine, or a cold or cough that will not go away. Call your transplant center
  right away ([NIDDK][NIDDK-Tx]).

**Fluid**

- **Much less urine than usual, new swelling** of the legs, ankles or face, or **weight gain above the
  alarm number** your team gave you. On hemodialysis, large gains between sessions are linked with
  more heart problems ([Cabrera 2015][DG28a]); fluid that cannot be controlled is a reason to start or
  change dialysis ([KDIGO 2024][K24], Table 41).

## Mention at your next visit

Itch, tiredness, poor appetite or nausea, muscle cramps, restless legs, low mood, poor sleep, or
problems with sex. These are common, often treatable, and KDIGO asks clinicians to ask about symptoms
like these at every visit ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 5.2.2.1). Also mention high glucose
readings that keep happening, a medicine you are struggling to take or pay for, and any new medicine,
supplement or scan that another doctor suggests ([Medicines to avoid](medicines/avoid.md)).

If any of these get worse quickly, do not wait: call today.

## Get ready before you need help

- [ ] Save your care team's numbers, including the after-hours line, in your phone and on the card
      below.
- [ ] Keep glucose tablets and glucagon where you and your family can reach them, and show your family
      how to give glucagon. ADA says glucagon should be prescribed for everyone who takes insulin
      ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6], recommendation 6.16).
- [ ] Keep in-date **blood** ketone strips. In a trial, blood ketone checks halved hospital and
      emergency visits compared with urine strips ([ADA 2026 §6][A26-6]).
- [ ] Ask for a **written sick-day plan** that says which medicines to pause and when to restart them
      ([KDIGO 2024][K24], practice point 4.3.2).
- [ ] Wear a medical ID ([NIDDK][NIDDK-hypo]) and fill in the [wallet cards](reference/wallet-card.md).
- [ ] With a fistula or graft, keep clean gauze and tape at home ([AKF][AKF-bleed]).
- [ ] Know your own potassium and fluid rules ([Potassium](eat/potassium.md); [Fluid](eat/fluid.md)).

## What to tell the 911 call-taker

1. "I have type 1 diabetes and kidney disease", plus "I am on dialysis" or "I have a kidney
   transplant" if that is true.
2. What is happening, and **when it started** ([CDC][CDC-STROKE]).
3. Your latest glucose and ketone readings, and whether glucagon was given.
4. If you can, take your medicine list, glucose meter, wallet card and, on PD, the cloudy bag.

## Outside the US

- **Emergency numbers:** 112 works across the EU, 999 in the UK, 000 in Australia, 111 in New Zealand.
  In England, NHS 111 gives urgent advice when it is not an emergency ([NHS][NHS-DKA]).
- National kidney charities, such as Kidney Care UK ([Kidney Care UK][KCUK]), and your national
  diabetes charity list local help lines.
