### Contradictions and notes for other owners (not edited here)

* `t1d/kidney-protecting-medicines.md`: blood pressure is "usually under 130/80 (ADA rec 11.5)". It leaves out ADA's "systolic < 120 encouraged" and KDIGO's "< 120 if tolerated" (Rec 3.4.1), so it reads differently from `ckd/diabetes-and-your-kidneys`. Low.
* `t1d/kidney-protecting-medicines.md`: "KDIGO recommends [a statin] for people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes and kidney disease (KDIGO 2022)" matches K22. KDIGO 2024 grades ages 18–49 as a suggestion (Rec 3.15.1.3), so the two pages differ in strength. Low.
* Spec 08 F9.7 attributes "up to two weeks" and "deaths have resulted" for icodextrin to MHRA. The MHRA page has neither. Cite the Extraneal label for the two weeks, and use MHRA's "could result in … death".
* Spec 08 §5.1 "glucose above about 250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) not falling for 2 h": no cited source gives this number (CDC uses 250 mg/dL only as a trigger to check ketones).
* KDIGO 2022 chapter 1's reason for the dapagliflozin withdrawal conflicts with the regulator (finding 3). Any page that paraphrases K22 on this point should use `MHRA-DAPA`.

### For a clinician to decide

1. **Transplant fever tier.** Is fever with shaking chills after a transplant a 911 sign (as now), or "call the transplant center right away" first? Should fever or rigors with a tunnelled hemodialysis catheter also go in the 911 tier? KDOQI vascular access 2019 was not re-read for this.
2. **Sepsis line on the wallet card.** "Fever with confusion or a racing pulse" is a 911 trigger for everyone. Confirm the wording.
3. **CGM goals at G4–G5 and on dialysis.** Should the page lead with the high-risk target (over 50 % in range, under 1 % below 70) rather than offer it as an option? CGM accuracy on dialysis has limited data.
4. **Potassium tiers on hemodialysis.** KDIGO Table 28 comes from UK primary-care guidance. Confirm that people on dialysis should use the same 6.0–6.4 "today" and 6.5 "911" tiers, or the unit's own plan (the page already says a written team plan wins).
5. **DKA on dialysis.** HC24 reports lower ketone levels in DKA with kidney failure. The page says symptoms count even below 3.0 mmol/L. Confirm, and decide whether to give a lower 911 ketone threshold for people on dialysis.
6. **ACE inhibitor or ARB in type 1 diabetes with A2 albuminuria and normal blood pressure.** The pages follow KDIGO 2024 Rec 3.6.3, which recommends it whatever the blood pressure. KDIGO 2022 Rec 1.2.1 and ADA rec 11.6a are written for people with hypertension. Confirm the wording.
7. **A1c goal on dialysis.** The page gives the KDIGO 2022 range for people not on dialysis only, and points to CGM. Confirm that no dialysis A1c goal should be stated.

### Contradictions and notes for other owners (not edited here)

* `eat/_targets-table.md` (generated from `docs/research/targets_by_stage.json`, app owners): the G1–G2 protein of 0.8–1.0 g/kg is above KDIGO 2022 Rec 3.1.1 (0.8 g/kg for diabetes and CKD not on dialysis). It is within "avoid > 1.3", but it is an app decision, not a guideline number. The stage page now says so.
* `sources.yml` `JBDS` is the 2016 summary. The current dialysis guidance is `JBDS22` (JBDS 11, August 2022). Pages that cite only `JBDS` for dialysis-day glucose (for example `t1d/insulin-and-dialysis`) should also cite JBDS22, which places the lowest glucose during the session as well as after it.
* Spec 08 §5.3 still lists "Protein 0.6–0.8 g/kg with diabetes (KDOQI)" for G3b and "mycophenolate needs contraception (REMS)" for after-transplant. ARCHITECTURE decision 10 (0.8 g/kg floor) and the July 2026 end of the REMS supersede both; the pages follow the newer facts.
* Spec 08 §5.1 gives "10 minutes" for a fistula bleed. The AKF source used by `get-help-now` says 10–15 minutes; the stage pages now follow AKF.

### For a clinician to decide

1. **Protein at G1–G2 with type 1 diabetes.** Should the app's starting range be 0.8–1.0 g/kg, or 0.8 g/kg as in KDIGO 2022 Rec 3.1.1?
2. **Potassium "review ceilings"** (4,000 / 3,500 / 3,000 / 2,500 mg by stage, 3,500 on PD). These are programme defaults that sit inside published ranges, not guideline numbers. Each stage page says to restrict only when blood potassium is high. Confirm both the numbers and that wording.
3. **DKA on dialysis.** The dialysis and G5 pages now say DKA signs count even when ketones are under 3.0 mmol/L (HC24 Table 4). Should a lower number (for example ≥ 1.6 mmol/L, ADA's "seek emergency care" level) be the 911 trigger for people on dialysis? See also item 5 of the previous section.
4. **Glucose checks during hemodialysis.** JBDS 2022 says glucose is lowest during the session and advises 20–30 g of carbohydrate if the pre-dialysis glucose is under 7 mmol/L (126 mg/dL). Should the page give that number, or leave it as "ask what your unit does"? Is CGM reliable enough during a session for alarms?
5. **A1c goal after a transplant.** KDIGO 2009 says to consider 7.0–7.5 % (ungraded), KDIGO 2022 gives an individual < 6.5 % to < 8.0 %, and ADA gives < 7 % for many adults. Which should lead? kdigo.org lists no update to the 2009 recipient guideline.
6. **Weight gain between HD sessions.** The page gives Cabrera's observational 3.5 % of body weight as "linked to death", beside "your team's limit". Confirm that a number should be shown.
7. **Access bleeding at home.** Confirm the AKF 10–15 minute rule for calling 911. Should home HD patients be told to follow their unit's needle-dislodgement and clamp training before anything else?
8. **Low potassium on PD.** The page says "do not cut potassium unless your blood test says so" (ISPD 2022: avoid hypokalaemia, 2C) and "some people need more". Confirm the wording.
9. **Iron thresholds on the G3b page.** Should patients see the KDIGO 2026 iron-start thresholds, or only "your team checks iron first"?
10. **Finerenone at G1–G2.** The type 1 indication rests on a 6-month UACR trial (FINE-ONE). The label needs eGFR ≥ 25 and K ≤ 5.0 mEq/L to start. Confirm the "ask whether it fits you" framing.
11. **OPTN waiting-time rule.** Policy 8.4.A was quoted from the OPTN text. Kidney allocation policy is being redesigned (continuous distribution), so recheck the rule and its number at each review.

### For a clinician to decide

1. **Glucose goals on dialysis.** Lead with the international CGM goals (70–180 mg/dL, older or high-risk column), or with JBDS 2022's dialysis hierarchy (no severe lows, no time under 54 mg/dL, then 108–216 mg/dL with more than 70 % in range)? The CGM page now shows both.
2. **CGM on dialysis.** The US labels say not to use Dexcom G7 or Libre Plus sensors on dialysis; UK guidance recommends offering CGM. Confirm the wording "only with your team's agreement, and check lows with a meter".
3. **A1c on dialysis.** Confirm "no agreed goal" and the JBDS 2022 "over 9.5 % (80 mmol/mol) usually means high glucose unless iron is very low" for a US audience.
4. **Ketones with vomiting on dialysis.** The labs pages now send ketones ≥ 1.6 mmol/L with vomiting to the emergency department, in line with `t1d/sick-days` and ADA. Confirm, together with item 5 of the first section and item 3 of the stages section.
5. **Bicarbonate targets.** The page gives KDIGO's "consider treatment under 18" and KDOQI's opinion-level "24–26". Which should lead, given BiCARB's null result in G4–G5?
6. **PD adequacy.** Confirm the framing "US rules use weekly Kt/V ≥ 1.7 or another accepted standard; ISPD: do not push the number higher if you are well".
7. **Iron infusion reactions.** Confirm the split: tell the nurse during the infusion; 911 for breathing trouble, facial or throat swelling or faintness afterwards.
8. **Albumin cut-off and lab method.** ISRNM's < 3.8 g/dL assumes bromocresol green; many labs use bromocresol purple, which reads lower. Should the app's nutrition-risk flag use a different cut-off for purple-method results (design note 05 L13)?

### For a clinician to decide

1. **Ketones 1.6–2.9 mmol/L.** NHS: contact the diabetes team now. ADA: seek emergency care at
   1.6 mmol/L or more. The handbook uses the NHS tier, with the emergency department for vomiting or
   when the team cannot be reached quickly. Confirm for a US readership, and for people on dialysis,
   in whom HC24 reports lower ketone levels in DKA.
2. **Urine ketones ++.** NHS reads ++ as about 1.5–3.0 mmol/L; the handbook sends ++ or more to
   emergency care because HC24 counts 2+ toward DKA. Confirm the stricter rule.
3. **CGM on dialysis.** Dexcom and Abbott say not to use their systems on dialysis; JBDS 2022 suggests
   real-time CGM for people on dialysis with repeated or unfelt lows. Confirm "use one only with your
   team's agreement" and whether the page should name a sensor labelled for dialysis if one exists.
4. **Low treatment on automated insulin delivery.** Confirm the wording of finding 1, and whether people
   with CKD on AID (more lows, longer insulin action) should be told to default to 15 g.
5. **SGLT2 inhibitors in type 1 diabetes.** `kidney-protecting-medicines` gives a safety checklist "if
   your team still prescribes one" (off-label). Confirm it should stay.
6. **Finerenone in type 1 diabetes.** The approval rests on a 6-month UACR result, with outcome benefit
   inferred from the type 2 trials, and combinations with SGLT2 inhibitors or GLP-1 receptor agonists
   were not studied. Decide whether the page should say this in patient language.
7. **Statins.** The page says KDIGO recommends a statin with diabetes and CKD and asks people on
   dialysis to check whether it applies. Confirm the wording for ages 18–49 and for dialysis.
8. **Medicines around contrast.** Confirm the KDIGO-based hold wording (non-essential nephrotoxic
   medicines only at eGFR < 30 or AKI; ACE inhibitor or ARB possibly held 48 h before a planned
   contrast CT) against local radiology practice.
9. **Insulin overdose.** The page says to treat lows and call the diabetes team or Poison Help. Decide
   whether a large extra dose of long-acting insulin should go straight to the emergency department.
10. **Oral phenylephrine.** The FDA calls it ineffective but found no safety signal; US labels say to ask
    a doctor first with high blood pressure, heart disease or diabetes. Confirm it belongs on the
    "avoid" list rather than "ask first".

### For a clinician to decide

1. **Hemodialysis catheter with fever or chills.** `dialysis-access` now says to call the unit right
   away (NKF) and 911 for shaking chills, confusion or a racing heart. Decide whether any fever with a
   tunneled catheter belongs in the 911 tier (see S1).
2. **Ketones with vomiting.** Four pages now use the `get-help-now` 911 wording, "any ketones with
   vomiting so you cannot keep fluids down", which is narrower than their old "ketones with vomiting".
   Confirm (see S5 and T1).
3. **Exercise with blood ketones 0.6–1.4 mmol/L.** EX17 allows brief light exercise (with a correction
   the team plans); the handbook's sick-day tier says follow the plan and retest in 2 hours. Pick one
   for people with CKD.
4. **Heavy lifting and competitive endurance events with kidney failure.** EX17 calls them
   contraindicated in long-standing diabetes with renal failure; ADA 2026 Rec 5.39 sees no need for
   specific limits in CKD in general. The page says "ask first". Confirm.
5. **Conservative care diet.** Confirm "eat for comfort and strength; older or frail people may need
   more protein, not less" (KDIGO PP 3.3.1.5), and whether to mention supervised very-low-protein diets
   (PP 3.3.1.2) for people who choose conservative care.
6. **ACE inhibitor before pregnancy with diabetic kidney disease.** UKKA 5.4.2: continue until
   conception with regular pregnancy tests; ADA 15.25a: stop before conception. The page gives both and
   says the teams decide together. Confirm the framing for a US readership.
7. **Restless legs.** Confirm following AASM 2024 over KDIGO 2024 Table 38, and showing the AASM iron
   thresholds for kidney failure (ferritin < 200 ng/mL and TSAT < 20 %) on a patient page.
8. **Waking at night to pass urine.** The spec asks for nocturia on the sleep page; ADA §5 does not
   cover it, so the unsourced causes were removed. Decide whether to add a sourced line (for example
   high glucose, evening fluid or diuretics).
